### Weblog (5)

- **2026-11-10** [Introducing Our Smallest, Most Efficient Model: Muse Ember](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/10/muse-ember/) — Meta's entry in the Haiku/Luna tier, explicitly aimed at "the work agents repeat all day: tool calls, coding subtasks, routing, extraction, and long document parsing". Priced the same as GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 for short prompts ($0.10/M in, $0.50/M out) and "80x that price" below Claude Fable 5.2 ($8/$40), but all three support a million input tokens and all three raise the price past a long-prompt threshold, which leaves Ember priced between the other two. The point worth keeping: the agent-work tier now has its own price point, and the frontier tier's premium is a deliberate line, not an accident.
- **2026-11-10** [Quoting Prompting Claude Fable 5.2](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/10/prompting-claude-fable-52/) — Anthropic's prompt-engineering docs on ownership: the model responds closely to who is said to own the code, and in their small-coding-task testing it merged repeated code in about half the tries when the prompt says the codebase is its own, against under a tenth of the tries when ownership is unstated or attributed to someone else. One sentence of framing changes the model's willingness to restructure — a cheap, measurable prompt lever.
- **2026-11-10** [Yonkers Rises](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/10/yonkers-rises/) — A Three.js animation of Yonkers standing up as a mech and stomping around Manhattan, Queens and Brooklyn on OpenStreetMap data, built to test Claude Fable 5.2 Medium's 3D modelling. The satisfying detail is that the model correctly read "átomos" as a mobile-keyboard typo for "stomps" — the scene depends on intent decoding, not rendering.
- **2026-11-09** [llm-anthropic 0.31](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/llm-anthropic/) — Adds Anthropic's Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 to the CLI; Fable 5.2 is currently top of Artificial Analysis, and Anthropic claim it "solved two open problems in the physics of black holes". Simon ran the pelican-on-a-bicycle prompt at all five reasoning levels (low, medium, high, xhigh, max) — a cheap, repeatable way to feel the difference between the settings.
- **2026-11-09** [Locating the source of a mysterious beep with Claude](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/9/mysterious-beeps/) — Claude Code built "Beep Locator", a system that triangulates an intermittent sound in a half-unpacked box room using several phones, tablets and laptops as microphones (the beep measured at 3345 Hz). A household mystery framed as an agent problem, with calibration and a join-by-QR listener panel — the agent's contribution is the system, not the answer.
