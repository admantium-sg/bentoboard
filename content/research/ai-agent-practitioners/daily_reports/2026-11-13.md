- **2026-07-25** [Own Your Intelligence: The Key to Lasting AI Advantage](https://www.langchain.com/blog/own-your-intelligence) — A July-dated Harrison's In the Loop post that surfaced only now (feed re-ordering), and is the most important thing he has written this quarter. Thesis: over five years every company will either run critical business on AI or sell AI, and generic intelligence will not be enough. "Ownership" does not mean building every layer — it means controlling the parts that determine behaviour, learning, cost and compounding. Three parts: (1) controlling the agent system — model optionality to avoid lock-in and adopt the best model as soon as it appears, the harness as the layer where company-specific behaviour lives ("if the harness is closed, you are accepting someone else's assumptions"), and context/memory as what turns generic intelligence specific; (2) owning economics, quality and risk; (3) compounding intelligence over time. The insurance-claims example is the sharpest part: a generic model knows the word "deductible" but not how to handle this claim, this customer, this jurisdiction, this policy, this evidence. Supply-chain analogy: buy the trucks, own the sourcing-and-forecast system.

## Simon Willison

### New (1)

- **2026-11-12** [llm-gemini 0.37](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/12/llm-gemini/) — Release notes plus a pricing teardown of Google's new Gemini 4 Hydrogen. The scheme: Carbon and Argon are both $2.00 input / $10.00 output through 30 November, then both double to $4.00 / $20.00 from 1 December; Hydrogen is $1.50 / $7.50 — only a 25% discount today, which becomes a 75% discount once the siblings double. Simon's rhetorical kill shot on Carbon vs Argon: they are the same price, so why two models? Google's answer is that Argon spends fewer tokens per turn, which moves the comparison from price per token to price per task. Against the cheap tier's rivals it is blunt: Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna are $0.10 / $0.50, so Hydrogen is 15x that. Simon re-ran his standard pelican test across all three Gemini 4 models at three effort levels and found the quality gap for Hydrogen stark despite near-identical pricing — his recurring point that price is not capability. Two structural notes: Hydrogen is Google's new free-tier model and powers AI features in Google Search, so the weakest model is on track to become the default surface; and any cost model built on Gemini rates has a hard cliff on 1 December.
