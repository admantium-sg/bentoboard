## Summary

Today's radar is dominated by a single theme that arrived from two directions at once: **sandboxing has become the assumed baseline for agents that run code**. Harrison Chase's LangChain published a sandbox-selection guide built on the "lethal trifecta" argument (filesystem isolation, network access, resource limits, microVMs), while Simon Willison, working with Prime Radiant, published the complementary implementation-side piece — an "external sandbox" pattern where the agent itself lives outside the sandbox and three reference servers (including v86 running Alpine in WebAssembly) do the isolating. The rest of Harrison's day extends the same production-hardening story: three new LangSmith controls (who can see trace inputs/outputs, team permissions via user groups, metadata-based trace policies) framed explicitly against GDPR, HIPAA, CCPA and SOC 2, and a twelve-part "Introduction to LangSmith" video series that looks like onboarding curriculum for a product reorganised around a single agent record. The genuinely new argument, though, is his proactive-agents post, which reframes the bottleneck: once agents are competent enough to take goals rather than tasks, the scarce resource is no longer agent capability but human attention, with memory and check-in UX as the design problems that follow — the logical conclusion of the "from tasks to goals" thesis from two days earlier. Simon's other two posts sit in the same practical-reality register: GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol arrive with a 20% price cut and a cached-input price down to $0.40/M, which he explicitly frames as good news for long-running agentic sessions, and Datasette Lite is rewritten with service workers so a version-pinned, plugin-loaded copy of the Python server runs in the browser. Lilian Weng and Chip Huyen contributed only engagement-counter movement — no new posts, no pushes — which makes today's signal unusually concentrated: the platform layer is hardening its security and governance story while two of the six practitioners were flat. The one persistent artefact is Harrison's re-fingerprinted X rows and yesterday's blog re-scrape, a collector churn pattern rather than genuine edits, and it is the reason the raw item count (35 changed) overstates the real news (roughly a dozen distinct pieces).
