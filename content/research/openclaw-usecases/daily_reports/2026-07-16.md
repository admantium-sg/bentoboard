### AI Policy — Thinking Machines Lab Releases "Inkling": 975B Open-Weight MoE, 41B Active Params, 1M Token Context (July 15)

Former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati's new lab, Thinking Machines Lab, released Inkling — its first model trained from scratch with full open weights on Hugging Face. The 975B-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model activates 41B parameters per token, supports up to 1M-token context, and was pretrained on 45 trillion multimodal tokens (text, image, audio, video). An Inkling-Small 12B-active variant is also available. TML raised ~$2B at a $12B valuation. The lab explicitly positions Inkling not as the strongest model on benchmarks but as a base for fine-tuning — the opposite of OpenAI's "best closed model wins" approach. Community: "OpenAI's former CTO just dropped a 975B open-weight model. ClosedAI charges $200/month for GPT-5.6 Sol; Inkling runs on your own hardware. ### Legal — EU Court Rules "OpenAI" Too Descriptive to Trademark in Europe — "Open" Means Open, "AI" Means AI (July 15)

The EU General Court in Luxembourg on July 15 rejected OpenAI's challenge to the EUIPO's partial refusal of its "OPENAI" trademark, ruling the term "purely descriptive and therefore lacks the distinctiveness required for trademark protection" for software and cloud-computing services. The court said the public will read "open" as "freely accessible" and "AI" as artificial intelligence, so the combination is standard English usage rather than a coined term. The irony: OpenAI's name is ruled descriptive while the company itself has become increasingly closed. Community: "EU court rules 'OpenAI' is too descriptive to trademark — irony: OpenAI is the least open major AI lab. ClosedAI can't even trademark the word 'Open.' ## Community Pulse

**Working well:** The v2026.7.1 release is landing well in the community — the Control UI overhaul with resizable multi-pane sessions, live Tasks view, and clearer mobile setup addresses long-standing UX requests. The expanded model support (GPT-5.6, Tencent Hy3, Meta Muse Spark 1.1) keeps OpenClaw's "any model, any platform" promise current. The showcase page continues to highlight real production deployments: one solo founder runs four specialized agents (Milo, Josh, Angela, Bob) for strategy, coding, marketing, and business — all coordinated via Telegram, with shared memory and scheduled background tasks. Another user built an overnight "scientist" pipeline where OpenClaw picks up logged ideas and runs research experiments autonomously, producing decision records each morning.

**Problems:** Community forums (r/openclaw, Discord, StackOverflow threads) surface persistent complaints: (1) Frequent breaking changes — v2026.4.14 updates broke core functionality for many users, forcing downgrades to stable versions like 2026.2.25; (2) Agent unresponsiveness — OpenClaw sometimes stalls on tasks, confirms completion without executing ("says things are done but are really not"), and requires manual prodding every few minutes; (3) Slow performance and high resource usage — complex tasks can take 5-15 seconds per step. One user titled their frustration post "OPENCLAW Currently Sucks!!!" These are real, recurring pain points in production use — the gap between showcase polish and daily-driver reliability remains a known issue.

All three are admissions — by a former OpenAI CTO, by Europe's legal system, and by Elon Musk's own lab — that open actually matters. OpenClaw has been saying this since day one. The lobster's philosophy is becoming mainstream.
