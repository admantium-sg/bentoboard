### Reasoning & Chain-of-Thought

**2610.37375** *Agent-Conditioned Routing: Diverse Reasoning Paths Within One Frozen Model* — asks whether the conditional capacity of a **frozen sparse MoE** can yield genuinely different solution methods from a single prompt, with no user-assigned roles, no prescribed methods, no fine-tuning and no model copies. Agent-Conditioned Routing (ACR) assigns *m* parallel trajectories persistent, untrained shared/private expert-eligibility masks while leaving the learned input-dependent token router intact. On a screened **126-question** multi-mode mathematics and physics benchmark, with model judges reading the reported derivations in full, ACR reaches **37.3 vs. 18.7** problems with ≥2 distinct primary methods under GLM 5.3 (≈2×) and **45.00 vs. 27.00** under GPT-5.6 Sol (1.67×) at matched output budgets. A blinded four-expert audit on **30** stratified problems finds more multi-method solutions under ACR on both backbones, **with lower correctness**; matched-eligibility ablations show only modest multi-method advantages, while token-resampled masks attain higher correctness, and an informed sequential prompt elicits even more diversity. ACR adds about **160 KiB** of mask state, and in a three-problem microbenchmark capped at **1,024 tokens** its latency and peak VRAM stay **within 3%** of batched temperature sampling. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37375

**2610.36437** *CONCUR: Concordant Auxiliary Rewards for Multi-Task, Multi-Reward Reinforcement Learning* — diagnoses a subtle failure of group-relative advantage estimators on multi-task RL: a task's primary reward *gates* auxiliary rewards such as a length reward (on a failing response an auxiliary may not exceed the primary), but group-relative estimators treat a gated auxiliary like any other component, so it can **reverse the primary's ordering**, and at the primary's ceiling the auxiliaries alone order the responses. Keeping gated auxiliaries hurts some tasks, dropping them hurts others. CONCUR replaces each gated auxiliary's standardized score with the closest score satisfying two conditions — responses with higher primary rewards cannot receive lower scores, and responses at the primary's ceiling receive equal scores; CONCUR-T applies both throughout training, while CONCUR-A relaxes the second for an auxiliary that more often agrees with than reverses the primary among passing responses. Evaluated on **three models from two families** across math, code, instruction following and writing, both variants score higher on average than all baselines and than the "drop all gated auxiliaries" variant (**abstract reports no point estimates**). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36437

**2610.37523** *The Structure Gap: When More Data Cannot Replace Structured Reasoning* — formalises **structure-encoded problems**, whose input–output map is determined or sharply constrained by known structure (solver, symmetry group, graph, physical law, measurement operator, grammar, combinatorial search space, optimization objective), and argues the relevant question is not expressivity but finite-data identification, extrapolation and certification. The claimed "structure gap" is the finite-resource gap between a method that receives the governing structure and a learner that receives only examples; the authors state the strongest useful form is **asymptotic rather than absolute** — structure-agnostic learning may approach a structured solver as coverage grows, but every finite dataset leaves residual uncertainty that the structure resolves by construction. Results are theorems plus controlled experiments: an **exact** proof for a Gaussian inverse problem (known-operator Wiener solver attains Bayes risk, while a dense empirical learner in the correct linear class has persistent expected excess risk), a cell-coverage lower bound, an unobserved-orbit non-identifiability result, and experiments in known-operator inversion, sparse recovery and graph reachability with size extrapolation (**abstract reports no point estimates**). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37523

### Prompt Engineering & Agent Self-Improvement

**2610.37255** *Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: The Decision Geometry of Agent Interfaces* — asks what a policy actually learns from its harness (the prompts, tools and control logic that present a task and execute outputs) and what survives when the harness changes. Under KL-regularized control the log-odds of an optimal policy recover how strongly the harness favours each action, so a trained policy **carries the preferences of its harness**; deployed return then equals the harness **ceiling** (best return it offers) minus the policy **mismatch** (return lost to suboptimal decisions). Training changes only the mismatch; changing the harness moves **both** terms — so comparing harnesses with a fixed model mixes what each harness offers with how well that model uses it. In MiniWoB++, post-training makes preferences harness-specific: **reversing the order of the observation fields leaves every optimal decision unchanged yet lowers a trained model's success from 85.0% to 19.2%**. Conversely, every new harness that a fixed-model comparison rejects but that raises the ceiling outperforms the original agent after further training under it, and ceiling/mismatch measured beforehand predict those gains. On coding-agent harnesses (Terminal-Bench Lite), ceiling-minus-mismatch estimated with a stronger model as a stand-in for the optimum **reproduces the accuracy ranking, while either term alone does not**. Code: https://github.com/2020-qqtcg/HRP. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255

**2610.37201** *Does Your Generalist Skill Optimizer Really Incentivize Agentic Tasks? Rethinking Skill Optimization via Bipartite Attribution* — skill optimizers rewrite an agent's natural-language "skill" in context and keep edits that raise success on a small dev set; since success is noisy, a dev gain does not show an edit helps, and a success score does not show what the edit costs. **BiPA** replays the optimizer's acceptance rule on candidates *identical to its seed* to measure what selection alone reports, and records each episode as a bipartite graph of turns and the text they read, tracing extra input tokens to behaviour. Across **three optimizers, three agentic benchmarks and two models**, learned skills succeed about as often as their hand-written seed — **0.1 points higher on average** — and their dev gains **vanish on test**: keeping the best of several candidates on at most **30 tasks** reports gains this large **even when none changed**. They read about **16% more input tokens** (optimizers almost always add rules, half to re-check work and some to act one call at a time; the agent follows them as extra turns, each re-sending the whole conversation). Code: https://github.com/valleysprings/BiPA.git. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37201

**2610.37224** *Spine: Compiling Subtask Trees into Structured Context for Long-Horizon Coding Agents* — long-horizon coding tasks contain nested subtasks; sequential history causes context inflation and degradation, trimming without explicit subtask boundaries discards information needed by unfinished subtasks, and recursive subagent delegation conflates subtask granularity with agent choice. Spine is an interface/runtime where the agent declares explicit subtask boundaries via work scopes, and a persistent **SpineTree** is compiled into context just in time before each model call — retaining unfinished subtasks' interactions, replacing completed ones with their results, and preserving prefix-cache reuse. Integrated into Codex as SpineCodex with **GPT-5.6 Sol**, versus native Codex: **+11.8% SWE-Milestone Score at −32.4% total cost**, **+17.3%** average ProgramBench test-pass rate, and Mean@5 improved on **8 of 10** FrontierSWE tasks; on SWE-Milestone it matches Recursive Subagent's score at **73.7% lower total cost**. Integrated into Pi with **Gemini 3.8 Flash** and **Qwen 3.8 27B**: **+9.5–17.5%** SWE-Milestone Score and **+4.3–8.7%** ProgramBench test-pass rate versus native Pi. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37224

**2610.36660** *The View from Nowhere: Population-Centered Agent Evolution* — self-improving agents typically centre improvement on selected parent agents, leaving the wider population underused; the authors identify a substantial gap between **population coverage** (tasks solved by at least one evolved variant) and the performance of the best individual agent, i.e. capabilities and diagnostic evidence emerge across the population without being consolidated into its leaders. The proposed principle — an agent should be improved by interpreting its behaviour through population-wide patterns of success, failure and prior self-modification — is instantiated as Population-Centered Evolution (PCE), which distils task records and modification histories into a "population block" (recurring failures, complementary successes, effects of previously attempted modifications) to guide improvements to a selected parent's harness. Under matched models, initial harnesses and evolution schedules, PCE achieves **72.1%** success on SWE-bench Verified and **96.7%** on Polyglot, **+3.2 and +5.9 percentage points** over a strong group-evolution baseline, reduces the coverage-vs-best-agent gap by **one third** on SWE-bench and **closes it** on Polyglot. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36660

**2610.36469** *CoHARL: Co-Evolving Agent Policies and Executable Harnesses with Reinforcement Learning* — optimizing a harness for a fixed policy overlooks how harness behaviour changes during learning, while training a policy under a fixed harness leaves the interface that generates training experience unchanged; CoHARL is a population-based RL framework that co-evolves a shared policy with a **population of executable harnesses**, the policy learning from rollouts across the population while a proposer revises harness code from execution traces during training. It achieves a **27.6%** mean resolved rate on SWE-rebench, **+6.4 percentage points** over harness-only search, with additional gains reported on SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0 (**abstract reports no point estimates for those two benchmarks**). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36469

### Efficiency: Distillation, Quantization, Serving

**2610.37157** *What Survives Distillation? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2* — depth-reduction distillation preserves aggregate metrics, so the paper asks what happens mechanistically, using linear CKA, logit lens analysis, attention pattern decomposition, sparse autoencoder feature statistics and activation patching on GPT-2 vs. its **six-layer** student DistilGPT-2 across two canonical circuits. It reports a **compression-collapse dichotomy**: the Indirect Object Identification circuit suffers **complete mechanistic collapse** (DistilGPT-2 **35.2%** accuracy vs. **94.8%** for the teacher, with **zero** name-mover preservation), while the Greater-Than circuit survives with **87.7%** task-performance recovery via active remapping of the teacher's **layer-9** comparison head into student **layer ~4** (mean Pearson **r = 0.945**). Conclusion: circuit compressibility governs mechanistic survival under distillation, and aggregate metrics are insufficient diagnostics for circuit integrity. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157

**2610.36901** *HiLo-RD: Cross-Budget Self-Distillation for Masked Diffusion Language Models* — masked diffusion LMs choose both *what* to generate and *where* to commit it, which under a small generation window causes **budget-induced premature commitment**: the model fixes answer fragments near the window boundary before resolving intermediate steps, and those fragments become context that redirects later reasoning. HiLo-RD pairs **large-window teacher states with cropped small-window views** of the same model and matches their hidden representations at committed positions, supervising both token prediction and commitment order **without an external teacher**. Across mathematics benchmarks it improves low-budget accuracy and shortens responses, and the gains transfer to code generation **without code-domain training**; on GSM8K accuracy rises by **9.4 percentage points** while mean readable length falls by about **25%**. Attention diagnostics and fixed-budget order interventions support the proposed mechanism. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36901

**2610.37062** *EnergyCal: Taming Loss Spikes in Muon-Family Optimizers for Sparse MoE Training* — sparse MoE training with Muon-family optimizers exhibits large loss spikes; EnergyCal is a lightweight rule that attenuates routed-expert updates according to the normalized squared Frobenius norm of their finite-step Newton-Schulz directions, leaving the spectral transform unchanged and adding **no persistent optimizer state**. Evaluated on a **4B-parameter MoE**: against a development-selected lower-learning-rate MuonH recipe, EnergyCal-MuonH reduces **mean thresholded loss-spike burden by 99.51%** and final **geometric-mean perplexity by 4.06%**, with a modest throughput cost; with plain Muon at matched nominal learning rate and weight decay the mean spike-burden reduction averages **95.98%** across the two tested exponents, accompanied by small endpoint improvements. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37062

**2610.36623** *God Does Not Play Dice: Enforcing Batch Invariance in Agentic Kernel Generation* — dynamic batching means the same request can be numerically altered by its batch context if kernels lack **batch invariance (BI)**, breaking reproducibility; existing kernel-generation agents target correctness and efficiency but overlook BI. The authors find **19 confirmed BI violations among 83** batch-applicable released artifacts, and propose a plug-in that combines contract-guided static dependency analysis with dynamic validation of bitwise consistency across batch contexts, wired into the optimization loop as successive acceptance gates that reject risky or non-invariant candidates before benchmarking and return feedback to the agent. Across **three agent frameworks** on real LLM-serving tasks, BI enforcement raises the **BI pass rate from 67.6% to 91.9%** while retaining **91.8%** of the baseline geometric-mean speedup. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36623

### Evaluation & Benchmark Validity

**2610.36544** *Monitor Accuracy Isn't the Answer: A Decision-Value Framework for Evaluating AI Agent Restart Monitors* — shows analytically (via an exact alarm-to-action identity for an independent clean restart with zero monitoring overhead) that a monitor with fixed **90% accuracy** can swing from a **5-point loss** to a **35-point gain** in success rate depending solely on the replacement attempt's residual-budget success probability. Two evaluation techniques are introduced: a **decision-sufficiency criterion** (a binary-action specialisation of Blackwell's comparison of experiments) that identifies which progress representations preserve the information needed to choose optimally between the specified actions, and a finite-sample, model-free **decision audit** using simultaneous binomial intervals over existing traces — no extra model calls — that certifies a restart-vs-continue preference or returns "abstain". Empirical study on **194 externally evaluated model executions** over **19** SymPy issues from SWE-bench Verified, with retrospective audit of **130** traces: a **2.29-point** apparent restart gain traced to a single surviving failed attempt, a **42.29-point** loss on a second model, **no positive replication** of the initial gain on prospective cohorts, and **0 of 103** live comparisons meeting the certification bar. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36544

**2610.37624** *Rare, Relevant, and Still Wrong: How Memory Fails LLM Group Decisions* — when a group's discussion is compressed into a short memory for a final decision maker, the two natural heuristics (keep the rarest claims; keep the claims relevant to the decision criteria) are confounded on hidden-profile tasks. **CrossProfile** is a diagnostic benchmark of **60 templates** with automatically verified answer keys (**480 tasks**) in which ownership of supporting, decoy and irrelevant facts is crossed while the text is held fixed. Across **Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and two DeepSeek models**, both heuristics fail predictably: selecting by relevance **readmits decoys** because decoys are on topic — even a perfect relevance filter loses **23 to 31 points** to rarest-first selection in the classic hidden-profile configuration — while rarest-first is **no better than random** on average and wins only where the rare facts happen to be useful; accuracy follows the balance of evidence the memory keeps, not the policy that kept it. The decision model adds a third failure, social proof: it over-weights facts attributed to many agents, and removing or randomly reassigning the attribution tags (line order fixed) raises accuracy by **13 to 24 points**. In natural discussions the evidence is lost before any memory acts — the complete transcript yields **lower** accuracy than no notes at all. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37624

**2610.37571** *TeamGym: A Gym for Team Decision-Making by AI Agents with Distributed Information* — AI agents are still trained and benchmarked in isolation although real decisions require integrating information distributed across participants; TeamGym builds team decision problems from real-world legal, finance, medical and software-engineering corpora and introduces **pivotal information** — a minimal set of facts each necessary and collectively sufficient for the correct final decision — letting it control pivotal-information distribution and track how well agents identify and integrate it. Examining **8 language models on 12 source corpora**, distributing pivotal information across participants lowers success rate by **22.9 percentage points** on average relative to giving the same information to a single model (**communication gap**); making participants identify and extract pivotal information from source material rather than receiving it directly lowers team success by a further **19.9 points** (**retrieval gap**). Training policies by RL on team decision loss improves final-decision quality and induces emergent coordination in participation, speaker selection and communication even though the reward evaluates only the final decision. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37571

### Other Notable

**2610.37276** *Where Should the Evaluation Budget Go in LLM-Driven Agent Scaffold Evolution?* — evaluation in scaffold evolution plays two roles (steering the search, picking the final scaffold), but evaluating every candidate on the full task set is expensive; the authors find **sparse feedback can sustain productive evolution** while **targeted evaluation improves final selection**, and replay analyses show shared informative items plus a common scoring reference support comparisons among closely matched candidates. Their allocation framework (the abstract renders the method name only as "\method") combines sparse search-time feedback with targeted final confirmation: a short fully evaluated warm-up establishes a shared informative core and calibrates a response model whose item parameters stay fixed during search; after search the model is refit and **paired successive halving** applied, reusing accumulated observations and executing only missing evaluations. Across **four benchmarks and two evolution frameworks** it beats full evaluation on mean performance in most settings while using **approximately half as many evaluation cells**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37276

**2610.37266** *Watermark Basins: Black-Box Detection of Distilled LLMs* — token-level watermark detection is weak when distillation traces are sampled at low temperature; the authors instead identify a **watermark basin**, a persistent key-specific geometric signal in parameter space that lies orthogonal to the ordinary task-specific fine-tuning subspace, and build **WM-Basin**, a black-box detector that queries the suspect model through its API only (no weights, no logits) — mapping generated text to gradient representations with a fixed clean probe model, projecting out the fine-tuning task directions and testing whether the residual aligns with the key's basin. Across teacher models and students from **135M to 8.2B** parameters distilled on mathematical reasoning, at a strict **0.1% false-positive rate** WM-Basin detects **79%** of distilled models at n-gram=1 and **45%** at n-gram=3, versus **29%** and **1.3%** for the token-level state of the art; because the signal lives in a persistent basin it survives quantization, pruning and RL post-training. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37266

**2610.37175** *MLE-World: Predicting Machine Learning Experiment Outcomes with a Learned World Model* — AI research agents that propose, execute and improve ML programs are limited by having to actually train each candidate; MLE-World is a world model finetuned from **Qwen3.5-4B** that predicts a program's terminal output (error traces, training logs, validation scores) from code and data description **without executing it**, plus a confidence score, making test-time scaling affordable because a prediction costs one inference call rather than a training run. Five TTS policies are built on it (sequential revision, best-of-N, pairwise judging, fusion, and a UCB-style rule with an exploration bonus from the confidence score), with a candidate run for real only when confidence is low. On **17 MLE-bench Lite tasks**, MLE-World matches the real terminal output on **77.9%** of held-out programs versus **41.1%** for much larger prompted models, and MLE-World-guided TTS achieves up to **22.1%** higher observed mean performance than the agent without a world model, with fewer real executions. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37175

### Novelty note

The already-reported Oct 28 batch (`findings_oct28_task1.md`) was dominated by **GRPO/RLVR critique, KV-cache/serving mechanics, and multilingual/benchmark audits**. This Oct 28-announcement batch (published 2026-10-27, 332 papers local) is different in character on four axes. (1) **The agent harness becomes an object of study in its own right** — 2610.37255 gives the harness a decision geometry (ceiling minus mismatch) and shows an 85.0% → 19.2% success collapse from a pure field-order change that leaves every optimal decision unchanged; 2610.36469 co-evolves policy and harness population by RL; 2610.37224 treats context compilation as a first-class runtime. This is a step beyond Oct 28's GRPO/RLVR-theoretical corrections: the critique now targets the *interface*, not the optimizer. (2) **Self-improvement moves from single-agent to population level** — 2610.36660's PCE (72.1% SWE-bench Verified, 96.7% Polyglot, +3.2/+5.9 pp) and 2610.37201's audit of skill optimizers (learned skills beat hand-written seeds by 0.1 points, 16% more input tokens, dev gains that vanish on test) together argue that agent self-improvement tooling is currently over-credited. (3) **Efficiency shifts from compression to reliability and dynamics** — 2610.36623 treats batch invariance as an agent-generated-kernel objective (19/83 released artifacts violating BI; pass rate 67.6% → 91.9%), 2610.37157 does mechanistic forensics on what distillation destroys (IOI circuit 35.2% vs 94.8%, zero name-mover preservation), and 2610.37062/2610.36623-style work on Muon-family stability (99.51% spike-burden reduction on a 4B MoE) extends the optimizer-side line the Oct 28 batch touched mainly through KV-cache/quantization compression. (4) **Evaluation validity shifts from "is the benchmark contaminated" to "is the metric decision-relevant"** — 2610.36544's decision-value framework (0 of 103 live comparisons certifiable; a 90%-accurate monitor worth −5 to +35 points depending on residual budget), 2610.37571's 22.9-point communication gap and 19.9-point retrieval gap, and 2610.37624's controlled fact-ownership crossing (relevance filtering loses 23–31 points) are instrument-design papers, not audits. Also distinctly new: memory-of-evidence pathologies (2610.37624), black-box detection of model distillation (2610.37266), and predictive world models for ML research agents (2610.37175). Cross-listed but not expanded above for space: 2610.37645 (MERLIN long-context linear attention, +8.3%/+7.0% RULER, beating baselines using 4× the memory), 2610.37647 (MuonGS, ~15% estimated token-equivalent speedup over MuonH), 2610.37642 (metacognitive decoding for diffusion agents, −20.6% inference FLOPs), 2610.37465 (FMTI pretraining-disclosure audit of 41 model entries), 2610.37162 (CoverageBench-Negate, ~63,000 negated evidence pairs), 2610.37482 (GUI task-advancement memory, 12.0% → 28.2%), 2610.37211 (video-RLVR shortcut amplification, no numbers), 2610.37041 (Guided-RLVR, no numbers).
