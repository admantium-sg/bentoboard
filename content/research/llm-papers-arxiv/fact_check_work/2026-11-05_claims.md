# Fact-check claims — batch 2026-11-05 (source section: 2026-10-28)

All claims are from `archive/cumulative_findings_2026-10.md`, section `## 2026-10-28 (Wednesday Evening)`,
which is the oldest still-unverified section. Each claim cites an arXiv ID; verify the ID resolves to the
stated paper AND that the stated numbers match.

Batch 1 = C1..C5, Batch 2 = C6..C10, Batch 3 = C11..C15.

### C1 — PICO (arXiv:2610.36849)
Claim: When every rollout fails, group-relative estimators collapse to zero advantage; distribution-constrained joint decoding lets expert data guide token-level sampling while preserving the student's relative token probabilities, under a finite divergence budget with dynamic top-k.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36849

### C2 — Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement (arXiv:2610.37553)
Claim: A theory of label-free RLVR collapse as a collective instability: sharpening slows by 1/N while the shared mode grows proportional to lambda^2 independent of N, giving a critical problem count N_c ≈ ln(1/delta)/(lambda^2 * G), with N_c proportional to lambda^-2 up to a log correction. Removing the shared direction prevents collapse entirely; restoring it causes collapse, growing with training-set size and base-model entropy.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37553

### C3 — GEPA-Curator (arXiv:2610.37651)
Claim: Reflective prompt optimizers learn from failed agent rollouts, but many failures come from harness limits, stochastic execution or noisy judges; adding task curation plus prior-success retrieval yields gains up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE across GoBrowse, ALFWorld, AppWorld and tau-bench, with Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 5 as optimizers.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37651

### C4 — Batching the reflective optimization loop (arXiv:2610.36774)
Claim: Most batched GEPA runs finished in at most half the wall-clock time (fastest in about a third to a quarter) and scored higher: LiveBench-Math 68.9% -> 72.1% (2x2), HoVer 49.0% -> 60.0% (8x1).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36774

### C5 — QERA (arXiv:2610.35083)
Claim: More than 80% of CL-bench Life rubrics share less than 60% of their content words with the final request; direct answering passes only 67% of implicit rubrics vs 81% of explicit ones. QERA (requirement checklist + typed information needs + code-verified quotes + guarded repair, ~4 calls, no training) reaches 21.0% average over all 405 tasks with four frontier LLMs, vs 18.2% for the best inference-time baseline and 14.6% for direct answering, using 4-5% of Ctx2Skill's tokens.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35083

### C6 — Folding attention biases into keys (arXiv:2610.36520)
Claim: Compacted KV caches can run on unmodified vLLM/FlashAttention by folding the per-key bias into the keys along one closed-form direction per head: matches Hugging Face answers on all 51 Qwen3-4B questions and greedy output on 180 of 200 Qwen3-14B LongBench samples; within noise of Attention Matching in 13 of 14 settings.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36520

### C7 — MoE channel selection needs no calibration data (arXiv:2610.37386)
Claim: Calibrated thresholds deliver neither promised benefit (a Prox threshold calibrated for an 18% read budget actually reads 12.9%); uncalibrated top-k matches or beats it in 40 of 43 comparisons (all 23 on generation tasks). The weight-only reader retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads, with MoE blocks 1.60-1.90x faster than optimized vLLM at batch one, up to 2.69x at larger batches.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37386

### C8 — CRISP edge MoE inference (arXiv:2610.37442)
Claim: Edge MoE inference is dominated by expert-weight movement, not compute; pruning/scheduling co-design gives up to 2.1x throughput at the same memory budget, with accuracy loss capped at 3.6% across Mixtral-8x7B and DeepSeek-MoE-16B.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37442

### C9 — MPOPD (arXiv:2610.36477)
Claim: The rollout policy in on-policy distillation is usually fixed, creating a compounding teacher-student mismatch that produces repetition amplifying across rounds; a student-anchored, teacher-guided rollout distribution improves math reasoning by +6.91 points over OPD on Qwen3-4B-Base while generation length drops 41%, across 16 benchmarks.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36477

### C10 — Balanced State Subspaces for Mamba-2 (arXiv:2610.36617)
Claim: Halves the state dimension at +0.3 WikiText-2 perplexity (1.8%) across 130M-2.7B models vs +1.6 (9.3%) for GHOST, with single-forward-pass calibration (an order of magnitude faster than GHOST's protocol).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36617

### C11 — S3R stochastic rounding (arXiv:2610.36587)
Claim: One stochastic-rounding threshold per token across the hidden dimension; 1.24-1.32x faster than BF16 and 1.11x over independent stochastic rounding for full INT8 training steps on TPU v5p.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36587

### C12 — CoS-VQ (arXiv:2610.37336)
Claim: Coordinated shared-scale vector quantization for LLM weights; joint index-scale updates beat QTIP and LLVQ on Qwen3.8-27B.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37336

### C13 — Multilingual evaluation audit (arXiv:2610.36990)
Claim: 51 benchmarks / 242 datasets / 219 languages: 36% of languages appear in only one benchmark, low-resource languages get 1-3 task categories vs 14 for high-resource, and 56% of all dataset-language instances are translated from English.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36990

### C14 — Correctness conditioning as selection in CoT-faithfulness evaluation (arXiv:2610.37070)
Claim: The exact observed gap is Delta = Cov(A,C)/P(C=1); protocol review of 18 papers; four sub-ceiling deployments all show positive gaps, the primary GLM-5.3 run yields +7.4 pp (n=1000, p<0.0001), pooled CMH OR = 1.70 (p<1e-6).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37070

### C15 — LiveSearch (arXiv:2610.37166)
Claim: Benchmark questions stored as executable verifier code; 70% of served questions change gold value day to day, and a literally reused snapshot fails on a majority of questions within a day. Six tool-calling models reach 70.9%-76.7% answer accuracy under general web search.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37166
