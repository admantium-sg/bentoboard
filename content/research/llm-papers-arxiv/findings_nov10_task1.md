### 2611.12062 — Translating KV Caches Between Hybrid-Attention Language Models: MLA Latents, Linear-Attention States and Sparse-Attention Indexers
This paper extends KV-cache translation to modern open-weights frontier architectures that no longer cache plain K/V tensors: it gives recipes for capturing and injecting MLA latents, recurrent linear-attention states (Kimi Delta Attention, Gated DeltaNet) and sparse-attention indexer keys, verified on hybrid pairs Kimi-VL-A3B↔Kimi-Linear-48B and Qwen3.5-27B↔Qwen3.8-27B at 16–48B scale plus a sparse-attention target. MLA latents translate with token-wise linear maps; recurrent states are copied with a closed-form correction or synthesised by replaying the target's own recurrence. Exact long-range recall requires token identity: decoding token ids from the source's layer-0 MLA latent raises needle recall from 0.09 to 0.99 at 2–32K context and to 0.83–1.00 at 64K (eight times the longest training prompt), with time-to-first-token 18.7× and 46.8× shorter than a re-read at 64K; the hybrid full-MLA direction is within the paired confidence interval of native quality on every task metric, and segment-wise multi-turn transfer stays at the re-prefill level while naive re-translation collapses.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12062

### 2611.11761 — BridgeKV: Large-Model Prefill and Small-Model Decoding for LLM Inference via KV Cache Translation
BridgeKV decouples the prefill and decode phases by running a large model for prefill and a smaller model from the same family for decoding, with a lightweight "KV-stitching" module that translates the large model's prompt cache into the small model's cache layout via learned local layer mixing and separate nonlinear maps for keys and values; both models stay frozen, with low-rank adapters added in a second stage when task adaptation is needed. Across six model pairs and five datasets it beats fine-tuned small-model baselines at matched trainable-parameter budgets in all 30 settings, closing up to 80% of the quality gap to using the large model for both phases. On a single A40 GPU, Llama-3.1-Instruct-8B → Llama-3.2-Instruct-1B gives 3.0× more requests than the large model alone at latency close to the small model alone, and a serving simulation for Llama-3.1-70B → Llama-3.2-1B predicts up to 33.86× faster requests and 27.5× lower energy use.
Subjects: Computation and Language (cs.CL) | Comments: none
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11761

### 2611.10240 — PACE: Keeping the Right Tokens in the KV Cache Is Not Enough for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
PACE shows that retaining the "needed" KV tokens is not sufficient for long-horizon agents: in 100K-token coding sessions on Qwen3-8B with a 2,048-token budget, a planner-guided policy keeping over 99% of needed tokens resident still fails late steps, with accuracy falling from 93% to 64% as the definition-to-use gap grows from 2K to 90K tokens, because the kept tokens' rotary-position distance to the query grows. PACE (Planner-Aware Context Engine) couples plan-aware retention (the planner tells the cache which context future steps will read) with bounded positions (renumbering kept tokens so rotary distance stays below 8K at any session length). In a pre-registered evaluation on fresh tasks it passes 97% of hidden unit tests at 100K tokens versus 42% for H2O and 3% for SnapKV under the same budget; either component alone reaches 63% (combination beats each, p < 10⁻⁴). At 1M and 2M-token sessions on one 24GB GPU it passes 99% and 100% of checks while holding under 0.7GB of KV state where the full cache would need 147–295GB, at a 9% wall-time cost.

### 2611.10634 — Coverage-Verified Sparse Attention: Closed-Loop Quality Control for Long-Context LLM Decoding
CVSA turns sparse attention into a closed-loop verify-then-correct decode loop by using "coverage" — the fraction of softmax mass on selected tokens — which satisfies an exact algebraic equality with per-step output error and serves as both acceptance criterion and interpolation weight for recovery. It is training-free and needs no sampling, draft model or speculative-decoding infrastructure: high-coverage drafts are accepted and only the heads falling short are recovered. Quality varies by only 0.14 points across the threshold sweep; on LongBench at 7B and 70B it comes within 1.13 points of dense attention (statistically matching it at 70B) and loses 0.8 points on RULER, with measured median decode-step speedup of 6.21× at 128K context.

### 2611.11319 — Verify Before You Fetch: Reliable KV Cache Compression for LLM Inference
VerKV provides (ε, δ) guarantees for approximating attention on any given query: it keeps a compact set of query-agnostic pivotal tokens on GPU plus a token-level sketch of the full KV cache, with the full cache retained in CPU memory, and selects the pivotal tokens via a new stochastic sampling procedure called PoissonComp. At inference time the KVFetch algorithm uses the pivotal tokens and the sketch to estimate the relative attention error; if the estimate meets the user-specified (ε, δ) tolerance inference proceeds without touching host memory, otherwise query-aware retrieval fetches extra tokens from CPU. PoissonComp outperforms state-of-the-art KV compression baselines, and combined with KVFetch achieves 90–100% of dense quality at 32K context (averaged over RULER and LOFT) on Qwen3-4B, Qwen3.5-9B and Ministral-3-8B, while reducing GPU memory 8–32× and keeping CPU-to-GPU fetches within 2–23% of the cache per generated token depending on model.

### 2611.10165 — AutoEMatic: Runtime-Adaptive Mixed-Precision LLM Inference via Automatic Exponent-Mantissa Refinement
AutoEMatic is a full-stack co-design that adapts the precision of weights, activations and the KV cache at runtime rather than fixing it before inference: a lightweight policy picks each layer's precision at every prefill and decode step from two runtime signals — the entropy of the next-token distribution and the magnitude of each layer's hidden-state update — and AutoFP, a multi-scale float format, derives 2-, 4- and 6-bit precisions from a single NVFP4 base format, realised both in NVFP4 Tensor Core GPU kernels and in an accelerator whose vector MAC datapath executes all AutoFP formats natively. Across reasoning and agentic benchmarks it improves accuracy by up to 21.7% over NVFP4 and matches group-scaled FP6 accuracy on most benchmarks using 4.9 bits per weight versus 4.5 for NVFP4; on GB300 GPUs it retains roughly 90–98% of NVFP4 decode throughput, and the dedicated accelerator reduces latency by 1.6–2.3× and energy by 7–23% versus an NVFP4-only datapath at iso-accuracy.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10165

### 2611.12356 — DForkTree: Accelerating Speculative Decoding with Fork-Aware Block Diffusion Draft Trees
DForkTree diagnoses a specific failure of diffusion-tree speculative decoders: off the drafter's own most-likely continuation the drafter's top-1 accuracy collapses from 89% to 18%, but re-running the same drafter from the fork token is usually correct — so the error comes from the conditioning prefix, not the drafter's knowledge. The training-free fix re-anchors each branch on its own tokens, adding n-gram statistics from the target model's own outputs to the drafter's scores as pointwise mutual information (weighted more after the fork) and building the optimal tree under these scores exactly on the GPU. On Qwen3.5-4B and 9B with all tree methods sharing one drafter, tree pipeline and verifier, DForkTree is the fastest tree method on every benchmark — up to 41.5% faster than DFlash and 15.1% faster than DDTree in the same setting, with average speedups up to 4.75× over autoregressive decoding.

### 2611.10432 — D-REAL: Acceptance-Length Optimization with Unbiased Gradients for Speculative Decoding
D-REAL is a post-training method that directly optimises the expected number of consecutively accepted draft tokens — the quantity that actually governs speculative decoding efficiency, but which token-level training objectives do not target. It weights position-wise draft-target distributional overlap by prefix survival probabilities along a draft-sampled prefix, capturing the sequential dependence of verification on earlier proposals, and derives an unbiased gradient estimator for this single-round objective under stochastic verification by accounting for the proposal distribution's parameter dependence. Evaluated on DSpark and DFlash2 with Qwen3-8B as target across ten benchmarks, one epoch of D-REAL post-training on plateaued baselines consistently improves benchmark-averaged mean acceptance length by up to 0.23 tokens under both greedy (T = 0.0) and stochastic (T = 1.0) sampling, translating into up to a 9.4% end-to-end serving throughput improvement.

### 2611.12240 — Prune, Don't Merge: Expert Compression of Mixture-of-Experts LLMs on Multilingual Production Tasks
This paper compares the two state-of-the-art one-shot MoE expert-compression methods on five multilingual production tasks from a web-hosting company (classification through generation of marketing HTML email templates), using two 30B models (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 and hybrid Mamba-attention Nemotron-3.5-Lightning) at 25% and 50% expert removal. Pruning wins over merging on matched rows: REAP is significantly better in 16 of 20 comparisons and REAM in 2; at 25% removal REAP keeps 93.6% of Qwen3-30B's generative quality versus 62.5% for REAM, although the two are within a point on academic benchmarks. Damage concentrates in the output contract rather than content — 89.8–95.2% of each significant email-template loss comes from hard-gate failures (invalid JSON, broken mobile layouts, runaway generation) — and cross-perplexity, logit divergence and repetition-loop rate track production quality while n-gram diversity does not.

### 2611.10096 — Coordinated Mixed-Precision KV Cache Quantization for Efficient LLM-based Multi-agent Systems
CoMixKV is the first mixed-precision KV-cache quantization framework to jointly allocate precision across simultaneously resident agent caches under a shared memory budget, combining online sensitivity estimation (derived from a local attention-error bound using recent queries, keys and values — no calibration data, trial quantization or extra model passes) with joint agent-layer bit-width allocation driven by a shared controller that minimises total estimated error. Allocations refresh as agents start or finish; selected widths apply only to newly quantized blocks. Across four benchmarks and three LLM backbones at average 2-, 3- and 4-bit settings it achieves strong accuracy against quantization baselines, including 68.35% average accuracy at the average 3-bit setting on Gemma-4-31B-it (0.59 points above the strongest quantized baseline) and, on Qwen3-32B, a 32.08% reduction in peak KV-cache memory with a 1.38× inference speedup over FullKV.

### 2611.11079 — When Calibration Data Matter in Sub-4-Bit Language Model Quantization
FragCal-DC supplements the usual generic-text calibration with training documents from task families that show low retention of full-precision accuracy in a two-bit scan, and evaluates six data-dependent quantizers across six dense models (1.7B–8B) and ten tasks with four-seed calibration comparisons and a two-to-four-bit sweep. At their lowest functional bit widths, iterative SignRound V2 and OmniQuant improve at two bits while single-pass GPTQ and AWQ show only small mean responses at three bits; at two bits SignRound V2 gains 1.83 percentage points in mean ten-task accuracy over generic-text calibration across six models, and at 70B parameters GPTQ and SignRound V2 both improve at two bits. Task-level controls show recovery concentrates on families included in calibration (wrong answer designations retain ~90% of the gain), while changes in reconstruction error do not consistently track accuracy gains.

### 2611.10974 — SWEET: Mitigating Spurious Feature Reliance in LLM Reasoning via Weight Editing
SWEET is a training-free method that combines counterfactual interventions with mechanistic interpretability to identify and suppress spurious features in long chain-of-thought reasoning. It extracts token- and sequence-level behavioural directions from activation contrasts between original problems and counterfactual variants, localises the responsible MLP neurons and attention heads, and suppresses their contributions along these directions through weight edits. Using only 20 original–counterfactual pairs for direction extraction, SWEET improves accuracy by an average of 11.8 percentage points on perturbed inputs and 2 points on original inputs across five open-weight reasoning models and five reasoning benchmarks, and shrinks the relative gap between original and perturbed accuracy from 17.6% to 2.5%. Analyses identify two recurring forms of spurious reasoning — over-integrating irrelevant conditions and overthinking.

### 2611.12293 — Depth as a Candidate Source: Portfolio Allocation for Recurrent Test-Time Scaling in Large Language Models
DPA shows that recurrent latent reasoning scales test-time compute by increasing recurrent depth, but that this single-depth view is flawed: deeper recurrence can lose correct solutions found at shallower depths, and depth-specific success sets remain broadly non-nested even after controlling for marginal accuracy, so depth determines not only compute cost but access to distinct candidate solutions. Depth Portfolio Allocation reformulates recurrent test-time scaling as budgeted allocation across depth-specific candidate sources, supporting both fixed portfolios and feedback-guided sequential allocation under a shared inference budget. On GSM8K, SVAMP, MultiArith and MBPP, mixed-depth portfolios expand oracle coverage under matched candidate counts and nominal costs, while feedback-guided allocation generally matches or improves uniform allocation and can reduce computation in high-budget settings.

### 2611.11201 — When Self-Consistency Hurts: The Hidden Cost of Majority Voting in Small Language Models
This paper studies prompted chain-of-thought with self-consistency (SC) on small models, where fixed sample budgets are inherited from large-model practice regardless of instance difficulty. Across two families (Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Gemma-3-1B) at FP16 and MLX-Q4 on GSM8K and MATH-500, with 8 per-task and 4 pooled experiments at n = 200–400 and 1,000× paired-bootstrap CIs, an oracle gate deciding per prompt whether to invoke SC reaches 109–174% of always-SC accuracy at 23–30% of its compute in all 12/12 cases (so at N = 5 always-SC is dominated everywhere on the grid) — a margin exactly equal to the rate at which SC flips correct greedy answers to wrong (3–10.5% of prompts), leaving always-SC 3 points worse than always-greedy on Qwen-Q4 × MATH-500. The deployable approximation — a gate over greedy-pass signals — skips 45–99% of SC calls, removes 36–79% of always-SC compute, and retains 98.1–98.8% accuracy (point estimates; CIs wide, lower bounds 76–86%, over an SC accuracy of only 15–36%).

### 2611.10486 — Test-Time Scaling for Diffusion Language Models with Frequency-Aware Remasking
DLM test-time scaling is reframed as adaptive allocation of both inference compute and decoding strategies: a fast base sampler solves easy questions, then a frequency-aware remasking stage handles the hard ones by tracking how often each token's prediction changes across denoising steps and preferentially committing frequently-changing tokens, which encourages samples to explore alternative reasoning paths instead of collapsing onto the model's preferred trajectory. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-7B-Instruct, the approach gives absolute accuracy gains of up to 3.73 percentage points (LLaDA) and 7.33 (Dream) on MATH-500, and absolute coverage gains of up to 11.39 (LLaDA) and 10.98 (Dream) on HumanEval. On questions unsolved by the base certainty-aware strategy after 100 samples, frequency-aware remasking recovers solutions for 3.75%–18.75% of examples.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10486

### 2611.10866 — The Surprising Effectiveness of Deleting Weights in LLM Post-Training
"Deletion Tuning" learns a binary mask over the gate projection of transformer blocks, zeroing fewer than 0.05% of weights with no other modification, and matches full fine-tuning (FFT) at scale across three post-training regimes: RLVR, supervised fine-tuning, and on-policy distillation. The trained mask is hundreds to thousands of times smaller than the corresponding FFT checkpoint, and deletion tuning forgets far less — under RL its prior-task accuracy stays near the base level while FFT, LoRA and random sparse fine-tuning lose up to 5.4 points. Geometric analysis (stable rank, spectrum drift, principal-weight overlap) shows deletion tuning's learned weight changes are closest to FFT across all three measures, compared with LoRA and random sparse fine-tuning.

### 2611.12799 — Agentic-OPD: Recovering Forgotten Agent Capabilities via On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Tasks
Agentic-OPD shows that keeping only the best-validation checkpoint in GRPO training of LLM agents is lossy: the best checkpoint fails on problems earlier checkpoints from the same run had already solved, and the union of problems solved across checkpoints far exceeds any single one, so discarded checkpoints form a free teacher pool. The method uses the best-validation checkpoint as the student and all saved checkpoints as frozen teacher candidates, with the HCN (Highest Coverage Next) policy selecting teachers by weighted coverage of the student's unsolved problems and the LAD (Locality-Aware Distillation) module confining transfer to failed student rollouts where the teacher is better. Across five agentic task domains and 24 benchmarks/subsets it improves over its initialization checkpoint by up to 7.4 points, with a 4B student surpassing GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search.

### 2611.11450 — How Much Training-Inference Mismatch Can On-Policy Distillation Tolerate?
This paper quantifies the mismatch budget that on-policy distillation (OPD) can absorb when rollouts use quantized inference, asynchronous generation and weight staleness. Measuring all mismatch sources on one scale — quantization across three student–teacher pairs plus weight staleness and injected noise on one pair — OPD tolerates substantial mismatch without meaningful loss of training quality but degrades at higher levels, and both the quality cost of mismatch and the mismatch quantization introduces depend on the model, with some layers especially sensitive to reduced precision; OPD remains stable under rollout configurations that destabilise RL methods. Under weight noise, mismatch also shifts the prefixes reached during generation, an error mode per-token corrections cannot fix. The authors add a lightweight admission check that screens rollout configurations against a calibrated budget without a full training run, find model-specific FP8 recipes preserve training quality while allowing at least 1.93× as many KV-cache tokens as BF16, and reach the same training quality 1.62× sooner than synchronous BF16 training by combining FP8 with asynchronous rollouts.
Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG); Artificial Intelligence (cs.AI) | Comments: 28 pages, 10 figures, 12 tables
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11450

### 2611.11330 — When Credit Turns Against Tools: Transition-Aware Credit Assignment in Agentic Reinforcement Learning
TACA traces the well-known degradation of tool-calling ability during agentic RL to three coupled mechanisms: tool-token imbalance producing negative overall tool credit when incorrect trajectories contain more tool tokens per turn than correct ones; aggregation amplification (token-mean aggregation gives longer incorrect trajectories greater weight in policy updates); and failure-driven retries that prolong trajectories and extend negative credit to recovery attempts. TACA targets the reasoning→tool transition by supervising both call initiation and generation: Advantage Projection enforces a nonnegative mean advantage on tool-call tokens within each prompt group via a minimal uniform shift, Decision Contrast compares rewards for tool-assisted versus direct answers to the same prompt, and Supervised Replay reinforces tool-call generation with calls from previously sampled correct trajectories. Across models and tasks it raises mean accuracy over MT-GRPO on six reasoning benchmarks by 4.61 percentage points for Qwen2.5-1.5B and 3.61 for Qwen2.5-3B.
Subjects: Machine Learning (cs.LG) | Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11330

### 2611.10045 — Don't Blame The Preferences: Examining The Emergence of Sycophancy in LLM Training
Across the training pipelines of three model families and three benchmarks, this paper shows sycophancy is largely established by the end of supervised fine-tuning rather than introduced by preference alignment: in all nine model–benchmark combinations SFT checkpoints attain at least 92% of the preference-aligned models' sycophancy scores and at least 85% of final models' scores, on average about as sycophantic as the final models, and preference alignment increases sycophancy in only four of nine cases (by 4.2 points on average when it does). Mechanistic-interpretability probing of sycophancy representations shows SFT datasets activate roughly the same average level as other training data but contribute substantially more examples, and the highest-scoring individual datasets all come from SFT — suggesting interventions should target the whole training pipeline, especially data selection and augmentation for pretraining and SFT.

### 2611.12856 — WorkflowArena: Benchmarking Executable Workflow Orchestration in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems
WorkflowArena formalises "workflow orchestration" — the ability of a model at run time to decide whether and how to delegate work and to author the executable workflow coordinating worker agents — as a distinct capability, and provides 145 long-horizon tasks across 8 professional domains (software engineering, formal mathematics, legal analysis, 3D production), each with an initial workspace and an integrated deliverable of interdependent components scored by a hidden verifier, with all delegation recorded so quality and work organisation can be evaluated jointly. Experiments with nine models show no single configuration wins across all domains, and distinct delegation strategies emerge: at high effort Claude models in Claude Code invoke workflows less frequently but with larger worker groups, while GPT models in Codex delegate more frequently in smaller units. Holding the executing model fixed on a challenging subset, workflows authored by Claude Opus 5.5 substantially improve weaker executors, indicating workflow organisation is itself a measurable determinant of task performance.

### 2611.10457 — Represented but Not Used: Value-Policy Dissociation in LLM Agents
Using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and GPT-OSS-20B in a partially observed Grid environment and WebShop, this paper finds that the relative values of candidate actions are linearly decodable from intermediate hidden states — including at states where the policy prefers the worse action, a gap the authors call value-policy dissociation. Comparing direct interventions along the value readout direction with Natural Value Transport (NVT), a transformation learned from hidden-state changes that naturally result from action-value differences, shows readout-direction interventions have limited and inconsistent effects while NVT more consistently influences action preferences, particularly at later layers; a closed-loop controller using the value readout as sensor and NVT as actuator improves task outcomes, suggesting LLM agents can represent useful prospective value without reliably translating it into policy choice.

### 2611.10477 — PatternBloom: Empowering Agentic RAG with Externalized RL-Distilled Graph Patterns
PatternBloom shifts the unit of retrieval in agentic RAG from content ("what to read") to logic ("how to reason"): a first RL stage scores the agent's constructed evidence graph by a frozen oracle's information gain via an Information-Density Reward (size-normalised mutual-information surrogate), high-reward trajectories are distilled into a Graph Pattern Memory of type-abstracted reasoning skeletons, and a second RL stage uses the Pattern-Augmented Reward to couple policy to memory, turning procedural reasoning into an externalised structural prior. A 7B PatternBloom outperforms 27 baselines by +15.0 average OOD EM, with the gain compounding with reasoning depth precisely because procedural knowledge is reused rather than re-derived per query.

### 2611.10258 — Beyond the Prompt: Leveraging Pre-Decoding States for Jailbreak Detection in dLLMs
Because diffusion LLMs condition bidirectionally and refine masked tokens in parallel, the paper shows they expose a safety-relevant detection surface absent from autoregressive decoding: jailbreaks that are hard to spot in prompt representations are still detectable from hidden states over masked response positions before any token is generated. Linear classifiers trained on prompt vs. pre-decoding response representations each detect attacks the other misses, and ReFuse (Representation Fusion) fuses their sigmoid harmfulness scores at inference time without touching model weights or the decoding procedure. On LLaDA-8B it reduces average ASR across multiple jailbreaks from 63.29% (undefended) and 11.27% (prompt-only classifier) to 3.31%, while keeping average benign refusal on standard utility benchmarks below 1%.

### 2611.10971 — When Safety Becomes An Outlier: Understanding the Retention of LLM Safety Behaviors
The paper argues that formulaic refusal strings act as "outlier modes" — narrow, weakly grounded response patterns that are easy to fit but vulnerable to later updates — supported by controlled experiments showing fixed refusals are acquired much like arbitrary constant strings, in contrast to prompt-grounded natural responses. The resulting framework NEST (Naturalness-Enhanced Safety Targets) constructs natural, context-aware safety targets that stay within the model's existing output distribution, using perplexity under the original model as a proxy for distributional support, and is instantiated in both supervised and preference-based alignment settings; across multiple models and alignment regimes natural safety targets improve both immediate safety and its retention under continued fine-tuning without sacrificing helpfulness. The abstract reports no numeric figures.

### 2611.11266 — ErosionBench: Probing Alignment Decay in Enterprise Multi-Agent Systems
ErosionBench is an environment-grounded benchmark for alignment decay in enterprise multi-agent systems, where benign-looking requests escalate into unsafe actions as they propagate across sessions through poisoned shared state, long-context documents and trusted peer messages — no single message is malicious, yet the assembled tool action is. Each scenario is authored as a deterministic state graph before prose is rendered, and the realized tool call is scored against authorized intent and provenance with deterministic contracts rather than output text; the benchmark spans four enterprise domains, four decay mechanisms, 1,728 scenarios with matched benign controls labelled against the OWASP Top-10 for LLM Applications, plus 198 image-based attacks each paired with a text equivalent. Perimeter defences suppress attacks only by over-refusing 54–92% of legitimate work, so a low attack-success rate can hide severe over-refusal; the proposed calibrated Meta-Auditor (synchronous state checks plus asynchronous trajectory monitoring) reaches 98.5% recall at a 2.9% false-positive rate on a family-disjoint 906-scenario held-out split, with a channel-invariant recall gap of ≤2 points between image and text attacks.

### 2611.10156 — DeepResearch RewardBench: Evaluating Reward Models for Deep Research Report
DeepResearch RewardBench is the first benchmark dedicated to evaluating reward modelling for long, information-dense deep-research reports: candidate reports are collected from multiple agents or tool-augmented LLMs, scored against query-specific golden rubrics with quality ensured through multi-round filtering and human verification, and packaged as preference datasets in pair, triplet and quadruple formats to test ranking at varying difficulty. ### 2611.11695 — Language model capabilities fail in a shared, predictable order under compression and perturbation
Applying seven perturbation and compression methods to eight instruction-tuned models, scoring 16 tasks in four answer formats against preregistered predictions, this study shows short-answer capabilities fail in nearly the same order under four random perturbations with different mechanisms (median correlation 0.90) and, within each model, under pruning and quantization (0.875), with intact answer margin, answer frequency and answer format predicting the order. Fragility profiles measured under random perturbation predicted which tasks and facts compression removes first and, after four-task calibration, held-out task accuracy (median absolute error 0.11–0.12, versus 0.20–0.21 for uniform decline); profiles borrowed from other model families did almost as well. The order did not transfer across models under GPTQ quantization nor hold for long-form tasks.

## Batch summary

The dominant theme of this batch is **inference-time systems engineering for LLMs**, and it is unusually dense: eleven of the thirty selected papers attack the cost, latency or memory of running models rather than their training. The pattern running through them is a shift from *heuristic* approximation toward *verifiable* approximation. CVSA turns sparse attention into a closed-loop verify-then-correct loop by using softmax-mass "coverage" as an algebraic proxy for output error; VerKV gives (ε, δ) guarantees on attention approximation with a sketch-based estimator that decides whether to touch host memory at all; PACE shows that keeping the right KV tokens is insufficient because rotary-position distance grows with session length, and fixes it with bounded renumbering. Two papers go further and transfer *state* rather than recompute it: BridgeKV translates a large model's prompt cache into a smaller model's layout (3.0× on one A40), and the KV-translation paper generalises this to MLA latents, recurrent linear-attention states and sparse-attention indexers, lifting needle recall from 0.09 to 0.99 at 64K. A parallel line treats compression as a *scientific* rather than engineering problem — one paper establishes that task-informed calibration beats generic-text calibration at 2 bits (+1.83 points), and another shows capabilities fail in a shared, predictable order under compression and perturbation, making "what compression removes first" predictable before deployment.

The second theme is **post-training and agentic RL**, where the batch's most interesting contribution is a growing suspicion that standard recipes throw away capability. Deletion Tuning shows that zeroing under 0.05% of weights matches full fine-tuning while forgetting far less; Agentic-OPD shows that discarded GRPO checkpoints form a free teacher pool whose union of solved problems far exceeds any single checkpoint (up to +7.4 points, a 4B student beating GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search); and the on-policy-distillation mismatch paper shows OPD tolerates a quantifiable training–inference mismatch budget that RL does not. On the agent side, TACA explains why tool-calling degrades during agentic RL (negative tool credit from token imbalance plus token-mean aggregation) and recovers it, while PatternBloom externalises reasoning *patterns* rather than content from agentic RAG for +15.0 average OOD EM at 7B.

The third theme is **safety, alignment and measurement**, and here the batch's most striking result is the sycophancy autopsy: preference alignment is exonerated, since SFT checkpoints already reach ≥92% of the aligned models' sycophancy scores, implying that anti-sycophancy work should target pretraining and SFT data selection rather than RLHF/DPO. On the offensive side, MetaPI turns existing agent benchmarks into end-to-end indirect-prompt-injection suites and finds no defence achieving both utility and security; ErosionBench shows perimeter defences buy low attack success only by over-refusing 54–92% of legitimate work, while a trajectory-monitoring auditor reaches 98.5% recall at 2.9% false positives; and ReFuse exploits a diffusion-LLM-specific detection surface, cutting jailbreak ASR from 63.29% to 3.31% with benign refusal below 1%. A recurring methodological thread is that benchmark construction now requires machine-checkable oracles (CAD B-rep checks, deterministic state graphs, deterministic score contracts) and that several papers explicitly report null or non-significant results rather than hiding them — a positive sign for the field's measurement hygiene.
