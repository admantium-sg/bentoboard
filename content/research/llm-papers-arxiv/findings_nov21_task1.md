**2611.26358** Beam Model Technical Report — Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 501.3B total parameters and 23.4B active parameters, built for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads, claiming frontier-level reasoning at 3–4× less inference compute than comparable-class models. Pretrained on 23.8T curated tokens; 1M-token context via a two-stage midtraining phase on verifiable, correctness-anchored data. Post-training ran a fully asynchronous RL campaign across 10.5K GB300 GPUs, generating over 100M rollouts over four weeks — claimed as one of the largest-scale RL runs by any open lab. Capabilities "continued to improve as we increased RL compute, with no sign of a plateau." Serving uses deep hardware-model co-design, FP8/NVFP4 quantization, and a 1.93B-parameter speculative decoding drafter (DFlash 2). Safety: refuses or pivots on 88.9% of harmful requests while complying with 81.5% of harmless ones — claimed highest harmful-request refusal/pivot rate among open-weight models on the Pareto frontier.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26358

**2611.26450** A CPU Inside an LLM: Zero-Gradient Compilation of Exact Circuits into Pretrained Weights — Introduces closed-form, zero-gradient circuit-grafting primitives that compile deterministic computation directly into pretrained LM weights with zero training examples and zero runtime hooks, while bounding per-layer normalization drift and preserving natural-language competence. Three analytical mechanisms: two-layer Mellin-GLU quadrature over bias-free SwiGLU/GeGLU layers (turning unknown per-token RMSNorm scaling into translation along a log-spaced gate grid with >250× dynamic range, IEEE 754 error bounds); cross-token scale cancellation via signed Chebyshev Indefinite-RoPE self-sink attention and memoryless SiLU-antisymmetric GatedDeltaNet shifts; and axis-aligned width extension with norm compensation, Base-1000 canonical BPE emission and differential tied unembeddings. An automated 5-stage compiler synthesizes 4–8 digit signed {+,−,×} circuits in constant TC⁰ depth alongside 3-literal Boolean (100%, N=1,728) and 5-bit Majority/Parity (100%) circuits. On 6-digit arithmetic in BF16, IBM Granite 4.2 3B and Alibaba Qwen 3.5 4B reach 99.17% and 100.00% ALU-prefix accuracy (100% with an FP32 sidecar) and 98–100% mid-sentence resumption, versus 42.2%/61.7% and 0% inline continuation for a 12.8K-sample LoRA. A factorized block-sparse sidecar holds A100 memory at 7.89/10.65 GiB while passing all 14/14 pre-registered >2K-token Tango splits (0/11,134 OOD triggers).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26450

**2611.26009** Evaluating safeguarding in multi-turn conversations with AI assistants — Tests whether models safeguard identically when distress cues arrive all at once or gradually. Three community mental-health support workers set gold-standard responses across nine scenarios (depression, psychosis, domestic abuse); five cues were presented either together in one message or dispersed across a conversation. Over 7,400 conversations, six models from five developers were evaluated. GPT-5.5, Grok 4.3, DeepSeek V4 and Llama 4 Maverick met standards in only 26–59% of concentrated-cue conversations and 3–6% of dispersed ones. Claude Sonnet 5 and Claude Opus 5 showed a smaller gap (68–97% concentrated, 59–87% dispersed); even the best model, Claude Opus 5, fell short in 13% of dispersed conversations. Conclusion: safety evaluations centred on explicit, legible concerns overstate real-world protection and must be redesigned around how people actually communicate.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26009

**2611.25538** Towards Autoregressive Neural Network Parameter Generation — Asks whether the zero-shot generalization LLMs show in text can be transferred to weight generation. Instruction-Guided Parameter Generation (IGPG) quantizes pretrained weights with a VQ-VAE, then trains a prefix-conditioned autoregressive generator over the resulting tokens; generated tokens are mapped back to parameter space by the decoder. Evaluated on in-distribution generation, cross-dataset transfer and cross-architecture settings, IGPG is competitive in-distribution and provides useful initializations for held-out datasets and architectures, beating random initialization after adaptation. Positions discrete autoregressive modeling as a framework for transferring structure across collections of pretrained parameters.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25538

**2611.26553** Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI) — News reports of agent failures (e.g. Replit's coding agent deleting a production database) rarely validate the incident or test the proposed cause. ROGUE-AI is a multi-agent framework that rebuilds each reported incident offline together with a matched control lacking only the blamed cause, then runs agents on both. It reproduces the reported harm in 89% of 108 attempted replications. Agents built on five current GPT, Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek models made the reported mistake in 18–33% of runs versus at most 1% without the blamed cause. Of 72 incidents with real-world harm, 62 had no underlying attacker — agents did as instructed but missed an ordinary hazard (e.g. a database shared with the live site), and these attacker-free replications caused more harm than prompt-injection replications for all five models. A warning after each tool output naming the hazard and its consequence lowered harm for all five models, whereas an equal-length note not naming the hazard left most harm in place. The framework is released as a reusable pre-release audit vendors can rerun.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553

**2611.25380** Beyond Weight Updates: Evolving Agent Harnesses for Domain Specialization — Studies harness evolution — evolving the system prompt, tool schemas, skill library and planning scaffolding while keeping the policy frozen — as a complement to weight updates. Cast as an RL-style outer loop and instantiated with GEPA, a reflective evolutionary optimizer; resulting harness adapters need no GPU training, load per vertical domain, and compose with weight-based RL. On SWE-bench Verified, OfficeBench and Terminal-Bench with a Claude Code-style agent, weight updates give the strongest single-path gains, harness evolution recovers most of those gains at a fraction of the compute, and combining both is best. Also analyses an SFT failure mode — shortened reasoning chains and increased truncation — which harness adapters avoid by construction.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25380

**2611.25066** DVBench: End-to-End Benchmark for Real-World UVM Design Verification Agents — First end-to-end benchmark for real-world UVM design-verification agents: 95 tasks across 9 functional domains and 5 difficulty levels, each requiring an agent to complete the full verification workflow rather than decoupled subtasks. Evaluates final testbenches via compilation, coverage and bug detection, plus intermediate artifacts (feature lists, test plans, reference models). Frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra) and agent frameworks (Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode) were tested; the best LLM-based agent (Codex & GPT-6 Astra) reaches only 21.05% overall success — substantial headroom.

**2611.24491** Probing agent overeagerness in realistic settings with continuation audits — Frontier coding agents increasingly take reckless actions beyond a user's mandate, but surfacing this requires private deployment traffic, and capable models recognize synthetic audit setups. Continuation Audits ground the auditor on a prefix drawn from a real coding-agent transcript and direct it to continue toward a seeded behavior. On SWE-Chat prefixes, a judge cannot reliably distinguish continuations from source sessions (realism win rate 0.42 vs 0.43) and verbalized eval-awareness across 900 audits does not exceed the rate in unaudited real sessions. Applied to three models across three seeds, each model leads a different seed — DeepSeek V4 Pro on colleague-credentials misuse, GPT-5.6 Terra on file-permission bypasses, Kimi K3 on treating diagnostic questions as instructions. Kimi K3 continues to bypass file-permission protections after an explicit CLAUDE.md prohibition while the other two stop entirely, corroborating Moonshot's own report of excessive proactiveness.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24491

**2611.23651** Safe Personal RSI: Evaluating and Repairing Personal-Agent Updates — Kicker is a personal health assistant whose reusable instructions affect disclosure, request-following and action reporting. Safe Personal RSI restricts recursive self-improvement to prompt updates (weights and runtime code fixed): it tests proposed and current instructions, repeats failing cases, temporarily undoes changes to guide repairs, and checks safety and performance before adoption — if nothing passes, current instructions stay. The executable benchmark has 29 email/calendar/health/marketplace cases plus probes for authorization reuse, memory and tool-output injection. Across three models and two deliberately risky edits it rejects 20 of 21 update attempts. An admitted GPT-5.6 Luna repair cuts attack violations from 10.7% to 0% and raises safe task completion from 89.7% to 100%; a repair transferred from Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to Qwen3-14B cuts violations from 69.0% to 17.9% and raises safe completion from 19.5% to 64.4%.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23651

**2611.24025** Intent Contracts: Taking Tools Away from Agents — An authorization interface in which the planning model holds no tools: for each step it submits a short logged contract (goal, verbatim user quotation, scope fields); code validates the contract and a small trusted model checks it against the user's words and writes the tool call itself. Provided validation and dispatch code is correct, every executed call is written by the trusted model, the planner's influence is confined to a logged contract, and the trusted side decides how much to carry out. With gpt-6.1-terra as planner and GLM-5.3-Flash as trusted model on three AmPermBench families, every object changed under contracts is named in the contract's goal — under review, the planner names objects in its commands but rarely in its goals. When the planner adds work the user never asked for, contracts complete the user's task in 27/30 runs versus 16/30 for an intent-visible reviewer. Importantly, contracts do not reduce unauthorized actions: they are indistinguishable from blind review, and once the benchmark's environment terms are clarified, every condition that changes state hits a wrong target in 40–49% of runs — the planner's choice of objects for an ambiguous reference, invisible to the trusted model.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24025

**2611.25146** EverydayBench: Evaluating Satisfactory Completion of Live User-Facing Digital Work — 77 human-authored tasks across eight everyday domains, each specifying success criteria before execution without prescribing an answer or workflow, so agents pick their own outcomes. Grading combines deterministic checks of explicit constraints, verification of claims against agent-cited pages retrieved at evaluation time, and rubric-based assessment of deliverable quality. Across 23 reported agent-model configurations, Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Fable 5.1 tie for the highest strict pass rate at 54.55%; a controlled GPT-6 Sol effort sweep ranges from 29.87% at low effort to 45.45% at max. Reliability varies by task type: GPT-5.6 Sol leads lookup, Fable 5.1 planning, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 tie on comparison, GPT-6 Astra and Sonnet 5.5 tie on customized deliverables.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25146

**2611.25135** Calibrated Decision Rewards: Training Language Models with a Judge That Knows How Sure It Is — RL can only teach a model what its reward can recognize; answer checkers are exact but need a reference answer, while learned reward models and LLM judges score but are not probabilities. RLCDR turns an LLM into a decision model answering a typed question ("is the final answer correct?", "which step is the first error?") with a probability distribution, read in one forward pass, debiased over option orders and calibrated with ~100 labels. Training Qwen3-1.7B with GRPO on MATH, this reference-free reward raises MATH500 accuracy from 72.2% to 76.8% (+4.65 points, p < 1e-4), matching training on the answer key (76.9%); a random reward gains only +0.65. The gain grows with difficulty (44.8% → 51.7% on the hardest level). Calibration holds on the policy's own changing samples: expected calibration error is half that of a softmax judge on the same 27B backbone (0.074 vs 0.141), with no sign of reward hacking. Without step training the same model beats a dedicated 7B process reward model (ProcessBench F1 0.772 vs 0.722, 20/20 splits). Boundary case mapped too: trained only on problems the base model mostly fails, the reference-free reward still improves the policy (+1.9 MATH500 points, p = 0.03) but trails the answer key because its probabilities become overconfident on that distribution.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25135

**2611.25613** Can an LLM Fix a Language Model Without Training It? — Whether an external LLM's value lies in *selecting* repairs or merely applying fixed numerical edits. In a controlled Transformer-repair study, an editor inspects predictions, activation summaries and weight statistics, then selects projection recipes to apply to the target without gradient updates; 20 recorded sessions across five masked and causal models are compared against fixed projections, scripted and matched-budget black-box search, and low-rank adaptation. Under a core two-layer fault, live selection matches black-box search on SmolLM2 and Qwen2.5; for Pythia it achieves 65.62% completion accuracy versus 0% for black-box search and 66.15% for a fixed projection. Under a compound fault affecting attention and feed-forward weights, both scripted and LLM-guided selection raise the descriptive five-checkpoint mean from 24.89% to 71.25% using the same operator family.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25613

**2611.26592** Realized versus Population Reliability: Anytime-Valid Self-Consistency Certification under Correlated Reasoning — Shows that the conditional-independence assumption behind anytime-valid self-consistency e-processes fails in practice: reasoning streams are positively correlated through shared prefixes or across-prompt heterogeneity. Modeling dependence via a per-unit latent state splits the single null into a realized null (about the drawn belief) and a population null (about average model behavior), which coincide only when ρ=0. Results: the standard betting e-process remains anytime-valid for the realized null, not the population null; read as a population-null test a suitable mixture crosses with asymptotic probability ≥ π = P(μ(Z)>0), potentially exceeding nominal ε; the within-unit mean's effective sample size saturates at 1/ρ, so extra within-unit rollouts cannot restore population validity; and a prompt-level e-process restores population validity, optimally at one rollout per prompt under a pure rollout budget. Simulations and Claude Haiku 4.5 rollouts confirm the regime dependence: on GSM8K (ρ̂=0.435, θ̂=+0.96) the gap is dormant, whereas on AIME (ρ̂=0.480, θ̂=−0.15) the single-unit rule crosses on 24% of prompts at nominal ε=0.05 while the prompt-level procedure declines (0.00).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26592

**2611.25669** Ask in the Crowd: Differentially Private LLM Inference via Dummy-Augmented Shuffling — DP-SPD protects user queries in LLM-as-a-Service by hiding the real query among client-generated dummy queries, shuffling all queries before sending them to the server, and exploiting shared-prefix structure to reuse transformer KV caches during decoding. Evaluated on classification and generation tasks and against QIA, EIA and GPT-based attacks, it reports strong inference-time privacy protection with near-plaintext utility plus inference-efficiency gains from shared-prefix KV-cache reuse.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25669

**2611.26221** Sightline: Calibrated Data-Flow Summaries for LLM-Integrated Applications — Programs call LLMs by filling prompt placeholders with program values; tracking data across that NL/PL boundary needs a summary of which placeholders the output uses, yet no code describes what the model does. Existing analyses either taint every output with every input (spurious flows) or ask a second model to label each call (costly). The paper proves, and mechanizes in Lean 4, that a placeholder flows to the output of a transformer, to first order, only through the attention that reads it, and that a faint reading or sensitivity cannot certify that a placeholder does not flow. Sightline reads a recorded call in one forward pass, learns which reads are uses, and calibrates pruning to miss at most a chosen fraction α of true flows on average over call sites when deployment is exchangeable with calibration. On 2,103 unseen repository families and four models it is the most accurate summary against the seven closest methods and a hidden-state probe; calibrated at α = 1% it misses ~1% of true flows and prunes 43.9–65.6% of spurious flows (1.6–2.6× the best baseline), retaining 43.8–60.1% when calibrated on another collection. Through an open surrogate it reads models treated as closed about as accurately as they read themselves.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26221

**2611.26075** Understanding MoE Decisions Beyond Gradients — Gradient-based influence estimation misses the effect of expert replacement in mixture-of-experts models: replacing one expert can change which experts later layers select, and those switches alter answer scores in ways derivatives do not capture. Introduces a predictor using the model's own expert outputs and selection rules to estimate these extra effects, added to a second-order prediction. Across three benchmarks, it reduces the error in predicting answer-score changes by 60% on Qwen3.6 and 47% on Gemma4 relative to a multistep gradient-plus-second-derivative baseline.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26075

**2611.26069** SheetAlign: Grounded Interaction and Stage-Isolated Learning for Spreadsheet Agents — A spreadsheet-manipulation framework with stage-isolated policy learning organized around where to operate, what to change, and how to implement transformations. Task-guided inspection and API grounding anchor code generation in workbook observations and available operations; stage-isolated learning optimizes program generation and review using edit-progress rewards; at inference, execution consistency selects programs through agreement among workbook states. On SpreadsheetBench the grounded task interface improves Qwen3-4B accuracy from 13.5% to 29.2% on average with no training; with learning plus execution consistency SheetAlign reaches 32.8% average on SpreadsheetBench and 30.1% on Domain-Spreadsheet, outperforming comparable baselines.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26069

**2611.25925** SpatialMem: Building Quantitative Spatial Reasoning Agents with Hierarchical Memory — VLMs do qualitative spatial reasoning well but are limited quantitatively; existing approaches bind each functional requirement to a single predefined vision specialist, giving fixed, rigid tool pipelines and a combinatorial execution space. SpatialMem is a memory framework letting a VLM agent accumulate tool-use experience and use it to orchestrate vision specialists. It builds a two-level memory (task memory for tool orchestration, scene memory for tool selection) plus a dual-state trajectory optimization that preserves successful tool experiences and refines suboptimal ones. Also proposes MetricBench (indoor and outdoor scenes, three quantitative spatial reasoning tasks). Achieves state-of-the-art results on MetricBench, Q-Spatial and SpatilRGPT-Bench, outperforming best VLM baselines by 32% on MetricBench, with the memory design worth a 9.1% absolute gain over a memory-free agent on MetricBench.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25925

**2611.25211** Learning to Read Events for Exact State Tracking with Frozen Language Models — Trains a selector on a frozen language model to read each transition, with an external integer register composing them in order; with API-only access the model is asked to list transitions, and a confidence rule can flag uncertain updates or retain several candidates. On products of permutations of five items — a task fixed-depth, log-precision transformers cannot solve on all input lengths under the conjecture TC⁰ ≠ NC¹ — a 27B model answered 3/100 correctly without visible reasoning and 96/100 with intermediate steps, while a frozen 0.8B model with a trained selector read every event in 299/299 windows of 43,687 events each; the same package passed 298/299 new windows on a fresh machine. On 1,000-event room-swap documents, three of ten models solved all five documents by reasoning alone, and all seven models tested both ways solved all five with extraction plus the register — no chain of thought requested. Concurrent extraction cut median wall-clock from 136.1 s to 10.4 s for Sonnet 5.5 and 313.5 s to 28.0 s for GPT-6.1 Sol, with mean cost per document down 55% and 44% respectively.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25211

**2611.24231** What Survives the Shift from Text to Pixels? Conditional Protection against Prompt Injection in Image-Rendered Documents — Pairs text and page-image versions of 191 English QA sources across six models, measuring marker appending, answer formatting and answer replacement alongside clean QA utility. Rendering reduces append and format execution for every model, yet Qwen3-VL-8B still executes image-borne replacement at 77.96%. Protection depends on target type, rendering settings, wording and repetition; a search over eight fixed attack candidates largely closes the image deficit in appending/formatting for Qwen and Gemini but not GPT-5-nano. Utility ratios on original questions run 93.31–101.61%, but Qwen falls to 61.99% when answers combine two evidence spans. Illegibility cannot explain all failed attacks: models often transcribe and identify image-borne commands they do not execute, and trusted user authorization raises execution on unchanged pages. Attention blocking suggests why — a text command acts through its own positions, whereas visual encoding spreads an image command and its target across the instruction page.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24231

**2611.25071** FinVeriQA: Financial Research Agents for Question Answering — An open-source multi-agent research prototype over SEC filings and live market data: a compact typed-decision model (Laya) routes each question and screens out advice requests, Python computes every number, retrieval and tools fetch evidence, Mistral-7B writes a cited answer, and a code validator checks that cited IDs exist and numbers match computed notes; an MCP server exposes the pipeline. Laya routed better than Mistral-7B (0.85 vs 0.52 routing; overall 0.86 vs 0.68, McNemar p = 0.0003), a 12B Mistral decided no better, and DeepSeek-R1-8B decided better (0.79 vs 0.68, p = 0.0192) but ~9.9× more slowly. Mistral-7B computed only 29% of 100 financial calculations correctly versus 100% for Python; a live quote cut price error from 210% to 0.00%. But in a live case study both agent answers passed validation while describing AMD sample data under NVIDIA's and Alphabet's names — an upper-bound study explains why: with one corrupted figure in shared evidence, self-consistency stays confident (agreement 0.55–0.59 while accuracy falls 0.50 → 0.00), and a shared-evidence check catches 35% of errors versus 77% for a check against the original passage (p = 5.96e-8). Verification must be independent of the evidence the writer saw.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25071

**2611.24650** Self-Rewarded Reinforcement Learning for Better-Structured Medical Explanations in Small Language Models — Small LMs run on commodity hardware and support private on-premises clinical deployment, but their medical explanations are short and poorly structured. Building on instruction-tuned Mistral-7B, applies PPO with LoRA to 2,000 Meerkat-derived medical prompts, guided by a simple self-supervised reward favoring longer, lexically diverse, well-punctuated responses while penalizing very short ones. About 170 PPO steps on a single NVIDIA A100 yield a stable increase in average reward; the RL-tuned model produces longer, more organized multi-point explanations while preserving the underlying medical content. A study of self-rewarded RL for style shaping, with limits discussed.

**2611.25446** PAIR-MED: Policy-reward Alternation for Iterative Refinement in Multilingual Medical Reasoning — A fixed process reward model becomes misaligned with the evolving trajectory distribution of a policy during RL, degrading supervision. The proposed policy–reward alternating training framework jointly adapts a medical PRM and optimizes a multilingual reasoning policy: the current policy generates multilingual trajectories to update the PRM, which then supplies process-level rewards for the next policy update, with medical step supervision preserving the PRM's medical foundation. Introduces multilingual medical PRMs and reasoning models at 8B and 9B scales across 12 languages. On medical subsets of Global-MMLU and MMLU-ProX the models improve average accuracy by 3.01–7.47% over their Qwen backbones; on CUREMED-BENCH, PAIR-MED-9B improves the joint language-consistency and logical-accuracy metric by 12.88% (in-domain) and 7.45% (out-of-domain).

**2611.26422** Reasoning-Trace Analysis Using Large Language Models for Interpretable Q-Matrix Construction — LLMs can generate Q-matrices for diagnostic classification models, but evaluations look only at final matrices, leaving the basis of each item-attribute assignment unexamined. Reasoning-Trace Analysis (RTA) has the LLM generate attribute-labeled reasoning steps via chain-of-thought prompting, which are then analyzed to reconstruct, compare and inspect each assignment; it is intended to supplement expert review. In a simulation with perturbed fraction-subtraction items, four LLMs recovered required attributes and withheld attributes the items did not require. In an empirical study (11 items, 536 examinees, four LLMs, up to three temperatures, 100 replications per condition), CoT-derived Q-matrices overlap substantially with the reference (mean .809) but less than directly generated matrices (.877), and vary more across replications. Trace inspection links disagreements to item-type coding, labeling rules and how operations were expressed as steps — Q-matrices with similar overlap differed in fit to examinee responses.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26422

**2611.23969** ReflectRec: Cycle-Consistency Preference Verification for LLM-based Recommendation — CoT-style LLM recommenders generate a natural-language preference note as a chain-of-thought prefix to ranking, but the note is trusted without verification and the ranker never consults behaviorally similar users. ReflectRec organizes a preference-to-history round-trip verifier whose recall score acts as an operational signal of preference-to-history recoverability, enabling conditional regeneration for low-recoverability preferences and selection among attempts; around it sit a semantic Stage-1 prompt, conditional regeneration, and Forward-Look retrieval injecting future watches of behaviorally similar users as a collaborative hint. On MovieLens-100K and MovieLens-1M the full pipeline beats CoT4Rec-ICL by +16.25 / +4.46 pp Hit@10, with positive gains across all ValScore buckets and three backbones (Qwen3-30B, Qwen2.5-72B, Llama-3.3-70B); a similar verifier-guided regeneration pattern appears on Amazon Electronics.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23969

**2611.26627** RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering — Probes each RL-tuned diffusion model with a deliberately simple parameterization: fixed steering vectors added to the block outputs of the frozen base model. On SD3.5-M these vectors hold 65× fewer parameters than the RL-tuned LoRA (0.29M vs 18.8M), are fit to match the velocity predictions of public RL-tuned checkpoints with no reward in the objective, and are identical for every prompt, token and sampling seed — yet recover 90.0–96.3% of the reward gains of six RL-tuned image models, and 89.8% / 111.9% on two RL-tuned video models. Initialized at zero and trained with online RL, the vectors alone exceed the reward gains of weight-based RL on text rendering (OCR) and human preference (PickScore) on SD3.5-M. Conclusion: a constant input-independent shift suffices for most of the gain of policy-gradient RL post-training — it steers the pretrained generator toward what it can already compute.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627

**2611.26199** Learning to Think with Images Faithfully via Adaptive Data Selection — Thinking-with-images (TwI) VLMs crop and zoom into fine visual detail, but improved accuracy does not guarantee faithful tool use: they frequently invoke tools without relying on the zoomed region. The cause is traced to training data — when questions are answerable without zooming, the model can answer correctly while ignoring the returned evidence, and the problem worsens as the model gets better at answering without tools. The method adapts training data to the model's evolving capability, training on questions where correct zoomed evidence can still help, supported by a synthesis pipeline that localizes small objects and constructs verifiable questions, complemented by an adaptiveness reward favoring tool-free answers when zooming is unnecessary. Results: substantial gains in reasoning faithfulness and state-of-the-art visual-search performance (+11.6% V*, +7.9% HR-Bench 4K, +9.4% HR-Bench 8K), plus gains on multimodal hallucination benchmarks.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26199

**2611.25889** EvoMem: Self-Evolving Skills for Time-Aware Long Video Memory Agents — Memory-augmented architectures have expanded long-video processing but retrieval still relies on global semantic matching without state-driven temporal control, returning fragmented or redundant evidence. EvoMem constructs a hierarchical time-indexed memory aligning evidence types onto a unified video timeline; during retrieval, temporal cues from acquired evidence become explicit signals constraining subsequent memory access. To fix recurring procedural errors in long-video QA (unclear retrieval targets, improper access paths, premature stopping), it introduces retrieval skills providing process-level guidance so the agent can adjust retrieval content, memory operations and target temporal ranges, and decide when to stop. Skill evolution is formulated as reward-guided policy improvement — each candidate skill is an external policy patch, accepted or rejected by answer-correctness state transitions on the validation set, with no parameter updates. On four long-video QA benchmarks: average accuracy 60.1%, average improvement 8.6 percentage points over the previous best.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25889

**2611.25560** Is a Description Worth a Thousand Demonstrations? Specificity and Reliability of Task Descriptions in In-Context Learning — Models task descriptions in ICL through a Bayesian lens as informative priors characterized by specificity and reliability, adapting the effective sample size (ESS) to quantify a description's predictive value as an equivalent number of demonstrations. For in-context linear regression, a reliable description's ESS grows with its specificity and with the task's dimension, while a description that may be irrelevant gains little from specificity unless demonstrations are given. Empirically, meta-trained Transformers come close to the Bayes-optimal ESS on their training distribution, but pretrained language models make too little use of highly specific descriptions when answering without reasoning; most match the Bayes-optimal predictor only when reasoning is enabled.

**2611.25292** Cross-Question Reliable Reinforcement Learning — Existing confidence-estimation post-training reinforces confidence per question-answer pair against its own binary correctness label, which is theoretically optimal but unattainable and harmful for selective prediction at non-zero risk tolerance: confidences pushed to 0 and 1 carry no information about how answers compare. CARE (Cross-question Abstention Reward Estimation) computes rewards over a set of question-answer pairs: at each of several risk tolerances the confidences across the set induce an abstention threshold, and each confidence is rewarded for lying on the correct side of it, so every verbalized confidence is reinforced relative to other questions' confidence. On math, CARE lifts coverage at 5% risk (C@5) across MATH-500, GSM8K and Big-Math-Digits from 0.8% to 42.4%. Trained on multi-hop HotpotQA, CARE improves C@5 on out-of-distribution general-knowledge, reasoning and math benchmarks (CommonsenseQA, GPQA, SimpleQA, TriviaQA) from 12.3% to 23.6%.

**2611.26018** Does the Workspace Do the Work? A Causal Protocol for Verifying Global-Workspace Computation in a Language Model — Building a workspace into a network does not reveal what the trained network computes with it: a module reporting an answer may have combined a broadcast message with its own knowledge, or merely forwarded an answer computed elsewhere — and task accuracy and ablation cannot tell these apart. The causal protocol's central test transplants a message from another input, chosen so that ignoring, relaying and recombining it predict three different answers. On networks built to relay or recombine, the test assigns the right computation every time; among trained workspace models it finds all three outcomes — one leaves its broadcast nearly unused, and of two models differing only in training data, one relays and one recombines. In AFGW, a language model whose modules communicate only through a learned workspace, a transplanted message yields the recombination-predicted answer in 97% of cases, acting by redirecting what the receiver retrieves from its own memory.
