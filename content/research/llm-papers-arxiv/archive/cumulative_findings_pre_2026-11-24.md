## Trend

The five-day window (2026-11-19 → 2026-11-23) splits cleanly into **one axis that barely moved and one that moved a lot**, and the split is between *measurement* and *everything measured*.

**1. The boards froze while the field did not.** Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index homepage table was byte-identical across 11-22 and 11-23 — 47 rows, identical slugs, identical Intelligence Index values, zero added, zero removed, zero changed — while its Decision Index moved only by *addition* (Bison 31B at 75.8803, #1 among decision models but #2 overall behind Claude Haiku 5.5 (Max) 76.4922) and by a single changelog entry (Ling-3.2-air scored at Intelligence Index 23.3878). BenchLM over the same window published a new snapshot (`2026-11-23-1a6fc6525ad498ed`, methodology `bench-align-v6.0-2026-11-19`) whose one substantive move was **Gemini 4 Neon 86.3 → 91.3 (+5.0), rank 4 → 2** — larger than any model-release effect of the week. BenchLM's own ordering policy ("an Estimated row backed by fewer than two source families never ranks above a row within 2 points below it") means rank and score must be read together. The recurring conclusion of the last several cycles holds: **the leaderboard is a snapshot event far more often than it is a capability event**, and a single-board delta is low-confidence.

**2. The community's centre of gravity moved from models to *institutions and mathematics*.** Hacker News AI share stayed essentially flat across the window — 22.9% (11-19), 29.5% (11-20), 27.6% (11-20) / 25.4% (11-21), 28.9% (11-22), 27.5% (11-23) — while the *content* rotated hard. Anthropic's IPO ($83/share, ~$1.5T) and its $4T-wipeout aftermath; the SEC's Nvidia accounting probe; **Nvidia reinstating Jensen Huang as CEO with reported government input** (1,673 points, the window's dominant AI-tagged item) and TSMC taking a 0.03% stake in Anthropic for US$500M; a Riemann-hypothesis proof claim; an agent swarm attempting P vs. NP; a Lean formalisation progress map. Meanwhile the largest AI thread of the week was "Anthropic employee claims general proof of the Riemann Hypothesis" (1,374 / 931) — **unverified, with a stated-but-unaudited Lean archive**. One thread put the constraint precisely: "You couldn't have proved the Riemann hypothesis on a $200/month Claude/GPT plan" — the binding limit on frontier mathematics has moved from model capability to **sustained inference budget**.

**3. Open-weight supply kept accelerating; open-weight *measurement* stayed stuck.** The window's releases were dense and mostly strong-licensed: **GLM-5.4** (z.ai's first Loop Transformer flagship, ~750B, 28 of 78 layers running twice per forward pass), **Kolibri 1.1** (Aleph Alpha, 75B MoE / 3B active, 1M context, Apache 2.0, sparse attention), **Ling-3.2-air** (inclusionAI, MIT, native multimodal MoE), LingSante-3.1-flash (560B / 25B active, 1M context, medical), Tencent WeMM-Ecom quants, Apple DepthPro2, Base Labs Bison 1 / Groundhog 1, Sarvam's 105B claimed to run offline on one 96 GB GPU, and community sub-3-bit work (NariQ at 2.5–3.0 bits/weight matching BF16 on long-horizon agentic tasks; a 0.5-bit MoE in 27.5 MB). Yet the **measured open-weight ceiling did not move**: Kimi K3.1 Pro sat at 46.4526 throughout, ~18 points behind the proprietary frontier. Supply grew, the ranking did not.

**4. The paper layer's target is the instrument and the agent's plumbing.** The 232 papers of the five cycles are dominated by work that treats measurement, memory, routing and decoding as the object under test: *Who Grades the Agent?* (privilege separation for coding-agent benchmarks), *Your Judge Inherits Your Prompt* (in-context label bias in LLM-as-judge is directional, not symmetric), *Gumbel Substitution* (removing sampler drift from RL without trusting the sampler), *Decision Models Are General-Purpose Process Reward Models*, *Cleave* (severing guardrails and auditing spectral coupling in open-weight LLMs), *Speculative Decoding Is Less Speculative Than You Think*, *MemOnce* (a 1313× smaller memory-state cache at 1M context), *Model Carving* (a network that prunes its own sublayers, 34% fewer FLOPs per token), and *Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights* — which crossed from arXiv into r/LocalLLaMA as a tracked topic. **Self-modification is now a headline topic in the local-inference audience, not just a paper abstract.**

**5. The local-inference community wrote itself a measurement standard.** Inkling 2 Mini dominated r/LocalLLaMA for a week (1680-upvote release thread; ~200 tok/s decode and ~6,700 tok/s prefill on 2×3090; a 5090-only NVFP4 build at 613 tok/s decode; a community reverse-engineering of the loop mechanism). The sharpest thread of the cycle is a **meta-rules proposal** (721 upvotes, 112 comments): "when you say 'I can run X at Y' you also have to say what quant/bpw/size". Headline throughput numbers had stopped being comparable, so the community imposed reporting discipline on itself — a strong sign that the *measurement layer is being rebuilt by users, not by vendors*. The parallel thread arguing inference engines have become too fragmented is the same complaint aimed at tooling.

**6. Cost-per-task is becoming a first-class axis.** Two threads treat money-per-task rather than tokens-per-second as the primary metric, BenchLM now publishes cost/task alongside index scores and encodes an ordering policy around estimated rows, and AA's Decision Index quotes per-task cost ($0.000345/task for Bison 31B). Meanwhile GLM-5.4's reception split **along capability axes rather than quality axes** — "great at coding, but absolutely garbage at writing" (268 upvotes) — i.e. the complaint is that "Generalized Coding" has not yet generalised.

**The unifying reading.** Three weeks of trend entries keep circling one idea: the object of study has moved from the model's score to the validity of the process that produced it. This window makes it concrete at four levels simultaneously — the benchmark (AA frozen vs. BenchLM's +5.0), the funding and export-control layer (Nvidia's board, TSMC's stake, Anthropic's IPO), the cost model (cost/task, sustained inference budget as the real limit on AI mathematics), and the community's own reporting rules (the quant/bpw meta-rule). The uncomfortable conclusion is unchanged and now better supported: **the models and the open-weight supply are moving faster than the instruments that rank them.** The one signal that *did* move all week — Gemini 4 Neon +5.0 on BenchLM — arrived with no frontier launch and no AA movement, which is the cleanest demonstration yet that our dashboards lag reality in both directions.
