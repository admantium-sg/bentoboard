## New Findings (July 17 submissions — not covered in prior days)

### 1. Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak
**arXiv:2607.14147** (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CR, cs.LG) — July 14, 2026
- A single prefill ("Sure, here is") strips the refusal from aligned LLMs — works across four models and three families (1.5B–14B)
- Key finding: harm representation stays intact — linear probe reads harm as high as on refused prompts (0.91–0.98), but behavioral refusal drops to chance
- Causal localization: first half of response suffices to break refusal; second half is nearly inert
- Mechanism: prefill's grip is generic autoregressive conditioning, not safety-specific suppression
- Three causal probes converge: restoring harm direction in early window partially re-engages refusal; injecting refuse-state reverses jailbreak (74%); knocking out early attention to prefill selectively collapses harmful continuation
- Safety implication: monitor reading the untouched prompt-side representation is immune to response-site attacks, but the failure surface is local and refusal is structurally shallow
- Code: https://github.com/collapseindex/breaking-refusal
- **Significance:** First mechanistic account of why prefill attacks work — refusal is a response-side, early-token computation, not a deep semantic property

### 2. Implicit Reasoning Steering via Concept Chaining
**arXiv:2607.14242** (cs.CL) — July 15, 2026
- Concept Chaining: generates short connection paragraph linking question entities to a target option through 1–2 intermediate concepts
- Continuing pretraining on these connection paragraphs shifts model answer preferences on multiple-choice questions
- Indirect natural-looking text can systematically steer predictions while being substantially less inferable than direct paraphrases
- Key insight: reasoning brittleness is not merely an evaluation artifact — it creates a practical channel through which latent biases can be amplified by ordinary-looking text to covertly redirect model decisions
- **Significance:** Demonstrates that implicit reasoning steering is a real vulnerability — subtle contextual text can bias decisions without explicit instructions or triggers

### 3. KV-Cache Grafting: Byte-Exact Verified-Knowledge Flywheel
**arXiv:2607.14431** (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.PF) — July 15, 2026
- Makes a frozen small language model more capable AND cheaper simultaneously without weight changes
- Verified knowledge deposited once as byte-exact KV-state artifact; restored later via graft into fresh inference context
- Restore is bit-exact: grafted logits are byte-for-byte identical to fresh computation (SHA-256 equality), zero KL divergence, 100% argmax agreement
- AIME 2025: frozen Gemma-4-12B moves from 80.0% → 93.3% with verified solution library graft
- Recurring problems: 8 problems base model never solves within 401,026-token budget are answered from cached verified solutions in 61 total decode tokens (~8,700x less energy)
- Same byte-exact store widens usable context from 32,768 to 2,854,766 tokens at zero extra accelerator memory
- **Significance:** Major efficiency breakthrough — verified knowledge can be stored and reused with byte-exact fidelity; enables verified-knowledge flywheel for small models that outperforms larger siblings

### 4. Reasoning Graphs for Robust LLM Authorship Attribution
**arXiv:2607.14905** (cs.CL, cs.AI) — July 16, 2026
- Prior work on LLM text detection focuses on surface-level linguistic features — susceptible to paraphrasing and backtranslation
- Goes beyond linguistic surface: extracts and analyzes reasoning structures in LLM-generated texts
- Graph neural network approach leveraging reasoning graphs from argument mining pipeline
- Outperforms Longformer baseline by up to 27 percentage points under obfuscation attacks (paraphrasing, backtranslation)
- 19 percentage points improvement on texts generated by unseen model versions
- **Significance:** Reasoning structure (not surface features) is the robust signal for LLM detection and authorship attribution — critical for attribution in the presence of obfuscation
