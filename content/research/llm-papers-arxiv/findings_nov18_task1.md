## A. Reasoning, chain-of-thought, and test-time compute

### 2611.22148 Long-Context Generation Is a Sampling Problem (When You Turn Up the Heat)
Tests 29 open-weight models (1B–122B) across ten benchmarks plus needle retrieval out to 1M tokens (SWE-bench Verified coding agents, long CoT reasoning, long-form writing, structured output, long-context reading), isolating the *sampler* as the variable. Claim: samplers that keep a small, stable candidate-token pool as temperature rises do not degrade, while samplers admitting low-probability tokens collapse. At temperature 2, coding agents running top-p/top-k/Top-H resolve **0 of 100** GitHub issues, whereas top-nsigma resolves **46–62** and wins every issue the two samplers disagree on across three Qwen3.5 models. Over all benchmarks top-nsigma keeps a median **100%** of its temperature-1 score vs **26%** for top-p. Mechanism evidence: in one Llama-3.1-8B story, temperature 1→2 grows top-p's pool from 4 to **51,845** tokens while top-nsigma stays at **3**. Across 133 sampler×temperature combinations, pool size predicts judged quality at Spearman **-0.77**; picking the pool at temperature 1 and applying temperature afterwards stops Top-H/min-p/p-less from collapsing. At the default temperature 1 most samplers perform about the same. Code and data released.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22148

### 2611.22778 A Full-Ledger Counterfactual Audit of Adaptive Reasoning
Introduces the Full-Ledger Counterfactual Value Audit (FLCVA), which compares an immediate answer against four fixed-length continuations from the same frozen prefix under one answer procedure, and charges every signal a policy uses. On GSM8K and MATH-500, a random 256-token continuation recovers a wrong answer in **26.6%** of Qwen3-8B and **18.9%** of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B cases while damaging a correct answer in only **1.4%** and **1.9%**. Correctness labels from independently sampled continuations predict a separate continuation's outcome in both models. In offline replay, using those labels to allocate compute improves DeepSeek performance, but the Qwen3 gain is **inconclusive**; under full cost accounting, tested lightweight myopic controllers mostly recognize current errors and add little continuation-specific value beyond that shortcut. A Llama-8B replication and action/cost ablations probe robustness. Explicitly a measurement paper: it separates *measuring* continuation value from *predicting* it from *using* predictions to allocate compute.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22778

### 2611.22301 MCTS Is Counting the Wrong Thing: Learning Exploration Counts for LLM Reasoning
Argues that standard UCT's independent per-branch visit counts let different phrasings of the same reasoning strategy repeatedly collect exploration bonuses; theory shows this count fragmentation raises cumulative regret. Proposed Learned Pseudo-Count MCTS (LPC-MCTS) changes only the exploration statistic, adding a lightweight Coin-Flipping Network head on the policy's hidden representations to estimate representation coverage so related branches share exploration evidence. Across 5 math-reasoning and 7 search benchmarks, LPC-MCTS consistently beats strong MCTS baselines. Holding the trained policy fixed and swapping in LPC-MCTS counts at inference lifts mean@16 accuracy by **+3.79 pp** on Qwen2.5-7B and **+2.71 pp** on Qwen3-4B over 5 math benchmarks; training *and* inference together yields **+4.96** and **+4.37 pp** over standard MCTS.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22301

### 2611.22234 The Fragility of Reasoning-Importance Attribution for Pruning Language Models
Stress-tests the assumption underpinning attribution-guided pruning of reasoning LLMs: that "reasoning importance" can be faithfully measured. Across four small reasoning models the measurement is fragile in three ways, each of which changes pruning decisions. (1) Scoring final-answer tokens saturates in every tested CoT model (answer-token NLL of **1e-4–1e-3**, because the model states its answer inside its own reasoning); guiding head pruning with this target costs **27.6** and **32.2 accuracy points** relative to scoring reasoning tokens on the two models run with five seeds, while perplexity barely moves and even reverses sign at 7B. (2) Importance is not localizable to single units — single-neuron effects sit at the measurement floor, their cross-split ranking is noise, and they cancel: a group's ablation effect disagrees in sign with the summed effects of its members in **90%** of groups. Reproducible importance emerges only at groups of ~**100 neurons**. (3) Contrastively isolated "reasoning-specific" importance is not separable from generic importance, and protecting it is worse than protecting raw importance. A pre-registered hypothesis (distillation-induced concentration predicts pruning cost) is **not supported** and is reported as unresolved. Resulting rules: score reasoning tokens, attribute at coarse granularity, evaluate with accuracy not perplexity.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22234

### 2611.23124 Get Hint-Augmented GRPO Done Right Through Activation Steering
Notes the failure mode of Hint-Augmented GRPO (HA-GRPO): when a training question exceeds the base model's capability, all rollouts get zero reward and there is no gradient — but no hint is available at inference, creating a training/inference context mismatch. ASH (Activation Steering for Hinting) reinterprets hinting as *steering behavior* rather than difficulty reduction: during training the model sees hinted and plain questions while a trainable steering vector is optimized to widen the margin between generation probabilities for the hinted vs plain version (capturing the question-agnostic component of the shift). At inference the vector is injected directly into activations, so no hint is needed. Across Qwen3-1.7B/4B-Base and Llama-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct, ASH consistently improves pass@k on AMC, OlympiadBench, AIME24, AIME25, Minerva and GPQA-Diamond, improving average pass@8 of Qwen3-1.7B-Base and Qwen3-4B-Base by **2.60%** and **2.21%**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23124

### 2611.22033 Thinking Harder Doesn't Lead to Better Choices: Reasoning Effort Increases the Confidence but Not the Correctness of LLM Shopping Agents
Benchmarks ten frontier models on three product-choice benchmarks built from WebShop, Amazon SQID and Wayfair WANDS. Across all three, increasing thinking effort does **not** meaningfully increase decision quality, while the same change raises stated confidence at a statistically significant level — i.e. extra thinking buys confidence, not correctness. Most models are poorly calibrated at every effort level, and errors are strongly shared: when one model errs, several others tend to err on the same task, and on multi-model failure tasks many pick the *same* wrong product.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22033

### 2611.21799 Chain-of-Thought Controllability Can Reduce Chain-of-Thought Monitorability
A safety-side result: SFT on mathematical reasoning traces rewritten in diverse personas induces broad CoT *control* even though the training contains **no monitor-evasion examples**. On covert side-task evaluations, the gpt-oss-20b "model organism" produces traces with less monitorable evidence than the base model. Weighted by side-task-success counts, the CoT monitorability metric g-mean² falls from **0.94** (base) to **0.68** (organism with no hiding instruction) to **0.22** (explicitly instructed to hide). In intervention evaluations explicit hiding often drives monitorability near zero; the effect replicates on a Kimi-K2.6 organism in agentic settings, though hidden objectives needing extended reasoning or longer trajectories stay more monitorable than single-step side tasks. Targeted transparency prompts substantially recover monitorability, sometimes exceeding the base model.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21799

### 2611.21946 Conserved Verdicts, Drifting Commitments: Identifying Harmful Overthinking
Formalizes overthinking via two quantities at the same point in a chain: the model's *commitment* (probability that the answer it would give now is correct) and its *verdict* (probability that the answer it will finish with is correct). Shows the verdict alone — what final-correctness probes estimate — cannot make this comparison, since as a forecast of a fixed outcome it has no predictable drift. The commitment-verdict gap equals expected remaining change in answer quality, i.e. the accuracy gained by stopping. Measured on MATH-500 with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B: at all **379** points where the model holds a correct answer (selected without regard to how the chain ends), *continuing* loses **3.6 points** on average (95% CI [2.2, 5.2]), concentrated in a few problems. Across six reasoning models from three families on MATH-500 and OlympiadBench with budgets up to 16,384 tokens, declining accuracy curves come from *which chains remain*, while chains still reasoning keep or improve their answers; an oracle stopping rule gains at most **+1.8** and **+2.5** points at 1.5B and 7B.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21946

### 2611.22103 From Sorry to QED: Refining LLM Math Proofs via Angelic Execution
MathEye converts imperfect LLM-generated Lean proof scripts into fully verified Lean proofs using *angelic proof checking* — an optimistic execution model that replaces failing fragments with placeholders while preserving the argument's structure, isolating exactly which proof obligations the LLM fails to discharge. Because obligations are logically interdependent, MathEye runs residual analysis over a proof dependence graph (symbol-level data flow) and formulates obligation selection as a **MaxSAT** optimization, picking a minimal-cost set whose resolution completes the whole proof; the mathematician is asked only about those essential obligations, in ordinary mathematical language. On **30 real-world benchmarks** across analysis and algebra, MathEye solves **93.3%** of problems while reducing required user interactions by **55%**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22103

### 2611.22161 What RL on Expert-Built Tasks Teaches Nemotron 3.5 Super
Post-trains NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super (120B total, 12B active) with RL on five expert-built task datasets, one at a time (real-world software engineering, complex constraints, long-horizon tool use, professional chart understanding, enterprise document reasoning). Each run trains a fresh rank-32 LoRA with CISPO on Tinker, **no supervised stage**, and each adapter is compared against the base model on identical tasks/harness/settings. On twelve headline benchmarks (8 built by other groups, 4 by Surge AI), gains run from **+4.5 pp** on IFBench to **+13.8** on GDP_pdf (10.4% → 24.2%); SWE-bench Pro V2 86.9% → **92.1%**, Terminal-Bench 2.1 63.9% → **70.3%**, MultiChallenge 58.3% → **66.6%**, AutomationBench 14.8% → **27.5%**, Chartography 39.4% → **50.6%**. The document adapter, trained *without* API tools, also gains +8.0 pp on AutomationBench. Side-by-side reading shows distinct failure-mode fixes per adapter (tool-use adapter finds data/rules before acting; coding adapter writes scratch test files; constraints adapter carries earlier turns; chart adapter measures marks in code).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22161

### 2611.19042 Understanding the Role of Reasoning Data in Pretraining
Asks how pretraining should balance atomic facts against multi-step reasoning traces, given that traces cover only a subset of available facts. Builds a controlled setting where every fact yields an atomic training signal but only a subset appears in reasoning supervision, so generalization to atomically-seen-but-never-traced transitions can be measured. For a linear transformer, derives the weight on atomic signals minimizing held-transition squared error, whose leading-order optimum **increases with reasoning-chain length** and **decreases with the fraction of facts held out** of reasoning traces; trends persist in a full transformer layer (MLP + softmax attention + cross-entropy). Validated in controlled multi-hop reasoning and mathematical pretraining.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19042

## B. Prompt engineering, ICL, and instruction following

### 2611.22605 Simplicity Goes Far: Auditing Prompt Optimizers with Demonstration-Conditioned Prompt Search
A methodological audit of automatic prompt optimization (APO): recent methods stack policy-gradient updates, trace reflection and evolutionary search, and all share a generate-and-select step that is rarely controlled for, so reported gains may conflate optimizer mechanism with sampling budget. The authors isolate that step as Demonstration-Conditioned Prompt Search (DCPS) — the APE recipe repurposed as a mechanism-attribution control (frozen generator conditioned on randomly sampled demonstrations, scored on validation data, best kept; no learned update, reflection or evolution). DCPS **replaces PPO in StablePrompt**, matching classification accuracy within **0.5 pp** at roughly **one-third the measured cost**; in DSPy-based compound AI systems it **matches GEPA's macro accuracy** on GPT-4.1-mini while using **55% fewer rollouts at 32% lower measured API cost** on average. Task-dependent pattern: proposal+selection recovers much of heavier optimizers' performance on structured/instruction-following tasks, while extra mechanisms retain advantages in some open-ended and reasoning-heavy settings. Recommends benchmarking APO against generate-and-select controls and reporting rollout counts, token usage and dollar cost.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22605

### 2611.22382 RICoT: Recurrent Interleaved Chain-of-Thought for Page Stream Segmentation
Reframes page stream segmentation (splitting a multi-page file into documents) as a sequential process over the whole stream rather than boundary classification over neighboring pages. RICoT is a prompting framework that, within a sliding window, reasons about each page and commits its boundary decision before moving on, and across windows carries forward a compact record of what it has read, keeping per-call cost bounded. Introduces **FinancePSS-205** (205 real-world de-identified financial page streams, to be released publicly). On FinancePSS-205, RICoT matches or beats pairwise and windowed prompting with the same backbone for three open-weight LLMs; on a TABME test subset RICoT with GLM-5 reaches document F1 **0.847** and boundary F1 **0.915**, above published zero-shot results. A 2B student trained on a larger teacher's decisions (with and without page-level reasoning) performs comparably in both modes and comes within **0.05 document F1** of the strongest prompted model.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22382

### 2611.21251 The Price of Choosing Examples: Context Overfitting in Adaptive In-Context Learning
Formalizes the gap between the usual ICL assumption (examples sampled before the learner is chosen) and deployed reality (examples retrieved, filtered, re-ranked, edited and repeatedly tested after seeing the query), as adaptive data analysis via a conditional stochastic-kernel calculus. Introduces *context capacity* κ (mutual information / approximate max-information between the data store and the final prompt transcript) and proves a finite-sample context generalization bound of order **√(κ/k)** for any frozen in-context learner and any adaptive pipeline of capacity κ, with a matching lower bound (constant exact when log k ≪ κ ≪ k). Derives two PAC-Bayesian retrieval rules (first-order Gibbs charging data-dependent gates their prior mass; self-normalized second-order). On Banking77 with 64 retrieval recipes and two model families, the certificates hold in all **24,000** resampled selection events, and the Gibbs rule certifies risk below zero-shot error in **>99%** of validation draws using 64 (Qwen2.5-7B) / 128 (GPT-4.1-mini) reused validation queries. Instantiated for Bayesian linear in-context regression with an order-statistics analysis of query-aligned top-k Gaussian retrieval. Core message: longer/more relevant contexts improve risk only when the selection procedure is stable enough for selected-context evidence to transfer.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21251

### 2611.19767 Logical AI OS: A Training-Free Governance Layer Outside the Model for Instruction Following
Measures (rather than proposes) a training-free governance layer placed outside the model: it reads the request as rules, mechanically checks the answer, repairs it when a request is not kept, and returns the original answer when the repair fails. On four 4-bit Qwen-family models (3B, 7B, 14B, and one 7B reasoning model) over **329 Chinese prompts**: (1) answers keeping every request the layer read rise significantly (all comparisons **p<0.0001**) by **34–66 of 329** under the corrected checker and **52–92** after a length-control fix; a generic redo message brings no significant gain. (2) Exploratory: on 90 repair-triggered prompts, two AI annotators judged the layer's output acceptable where the bare model failed more often than the reverse (**49:7** and **18:12**, the latter not significant). (3) Costs: **43–66% more generation time**; whole-answer rewriting makes a sizeable share of usable answers unusable. (4) Failures: rule-based reading reaches ~**70%** and **84%** of a two-annotator ceiling on unseen phrasings; unsatisfiable requests make the reasoning model loop; model self-reports are unreliable (**18 of 25** "fixed" items were not fixed). The paper also reports calibrating the measuring instrument itself, noting over half of the original checker's "violated" verdicts were wrong.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19767

### 2611.20071 The Silent Cost of Conversation History in LLM Reasoning
Pre-registers analyses before computing outcomes. **Study 1** (48 sharded math tasks): retaining a model's own earlier replies lowered final success by **0.21** for GPT-4o-mini and **0.29** for Mistral-7B (Holm-adjusted **p=0.026** and **p=0.004**); Llama-4 Scout showed the same direction but not significant. **Study 2** introduces CDR-Stream, **100-turn** state-tracking dialogues with an exact answer per turn and per-model difficulty calibration: with full history accuracy fell from ~**0.6 to ~0.1 within twenty turns** at matched difficulty, whereas a solver-verified state summary kept it **0.52–0.59** higher (Holm-adjusted p<0.001). Consistency and logical coherence fell far less than accuracy, and stability rose. No tested process metric (readiness measure, calibrated single-stream drift detector) gave robust model-general early warning; a single 100-turn stream had power only for very large drops. Recommendation: keep task state outside the transcript and monitor across sessions.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20071

## C. Efficiency, quantization, serving

### 2611.22215 Communicate the Change: Efficient Expert Parallelism via Activation Compression
EchoMoE retrofits existing sparse MoE models to cut the two synchronous collectives per MoE layer (dispatch + combine), which have become the bottleneck as compute outgrows interconnect bandwidth. Because transformer blocks update the residual stream incrementally, EchoMoE uses an earlier layer's hidden state as a reference and communicates only the *compressed difference* to the current state, refreshing references periodically. Requires ~**8,500x fewer training tokens** than full pretraining. Reports **58%** less inter-node communication volume, **+22.6%** throughput, average performance within **3.8 percentage points** across **18 benchmarks**, and up to **2.38x** inference throughput over bandwidth-constrained Ethernet; it also tolerates quantization of the communicated representations without measurable quality loss. Releases an implementation plus a vLLM inference integration.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22215

### 2611.22296 SpecSearch: Towards Hardware-Aware Efficient Speculative Decoding
Speculative decoding's optimum depends jointly on workload, GPU and memory: on an RTX 5090 the SOTA DFlash drafter reaches **3.50x** AR throughput at low concurrency but only **0.76x** at high concurrency with short outputs. SpecSearch is a hardware-aware search engine jointly optimizing decoding strategy, draft length and precision per workload–GPU pair, using an analytical cost model to find feasible configurations, then calibration measurements across GPUs to train a Jensen-based predictor that picks high-throughput configurations on **held-out GPUs without deployment-GPU calibration** (LoRA-adapting a frozen Qwen3-4B plus a linear correction layer). Across RTX 5090, H100, L40 and AMD MI300X: full calibration averages **2.01x** AR and **1.36x** speculative-decoding throughput (vLLM); the zero-calibration predictor reaches **1.30x** SD, retaining **97.36%** of full-calibration throughput.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22296

### 2611.22334 BQ-LoRA: Binary-Quantized Low-Rank Adapters as Implicit Regularizers for Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
Questions whether the FP16 adapter precision in QLoRA is necessary: replacing it with a single binary bit per adapter parameter (plus a learned per-layer scale and a Scaled Straight-Through Estimator) cuts adapter storage **13–16x**. Two variants — NormScale (norm-preserving scale, clipped STE) and LearnThresh (learnable binarization threshold) — unified as BQ-LoRA. Theory: bounded entrywise norm, a typical-set spectral norm bound **Θ(r)** sharper than worst case, a full-rank near-isotropic update, and a Rademacher-complexity generalization bound of Õ(s_max α √(d_out/(rn))) — FP16 LoRA has no comparable a priori norm or isotropy control. Results: on Qwen2.5 (1.5B–72B), BQ-LoRA exceeds QLoRA's benchmark averages at every scale by **0.8–3.5 points**; on LLaMA-2 (7B/70B) it is competitive but not uniformly ahead (LoftQ at 7B, QLoRA at 70B). On Qwen2.5-7B the binary adapter preserves the 4-bit base WikiText-2 PPL within **0.06** (9.86 vs 9.80) while QLoRA worsens it by **0.59**. Training memory matches QLoRA, step time +<4%. Deployment study BQ-SGMV extends Punica's SGMV kernel with a binary inner-product primitive: an A100-80GB holds **15.6x** more adapters in HBM.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22334

### 2611.22128 TempQuant: Learning Adaptive Temperatures for Quantized Language Models
Quantization "warms" the model on average (measured via generation entropy), and temperature matters much more for a quantized model than for its full-precision counterpart. TempQuant learns the quantized model's temperature and top-p to bring its next-token probabilities closer to the full-precision model's. Global variant (one setting per requested temperature) finds nearly the same temperatures that tuning on *labeled* answers would, without labels; per-token variant is motivated by the finding that tokens whose rankings flip under quantization tend to need warming while ranking-preserved tokens need cooling. Headline: on 3-bit Qwen3-4B, global TempQuant raises MATH accuracy from **34%** (the configuration Qwen recommends for its quantized releases) to **51%**. The paper argues model cards should not assume quantized models should be sampled with full-precision settings.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22128

### 2611.22019 Computed Permutation Trellis Coding: Near-Optimal Quantization from Two Multiplications and a 64-Byte Table
CPTC is a low-bit tensor format for write-once/read-many data (e.g. LLM weights). Per-value formats like INT4/FP4 waste bits against the information-theoretic ideal; trellis codes recover this by joint encoding but need either huge lookup tables or hash-restricted decoding. CPTC's decoder multiplies state by two coefficients and adds the top bits of the products to index a **64-byte table**; because the sum is non-uniform, rarely-used table ends hold values far into the tails. One decoder serves bit widths **1 through 6**, reproduces standard integer and floating-point formats exactly, and comes within **0.07 bits** of the ideal at 4 bits — matching a random-permutation trellis code whose table is thousands of times larger, and exceeding every QTIP codebook. In LLM weight quantization without fine-tuning, CPTC has the **lowest KL divergence to the unquantized model at every tested bit width** on Llama 3.1 8B and 70B Instruct, beating QTIP codebooks by up to **27%**. The decoder is built into the AWS Trainium 4 accelerator, running at the throughput of native FP4/FP8 for a negligible fraction of the systolic array area.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22019

### 2611.22721 HADVQ: Hessian Aware Directional Vector Quantization
Ultra-low-bit LLM quantization is hard because aggressive compression distorts parameters and disproportionately harms a small number of highly sensitive channels. HADVQ: random preconditioning to regularize the weight space; polar representation with decoupled quantization of directions and magnitudes; a structured codebook based on the **E8 lattice** for directions (compact yet expressive); and detection of extremely important channels via a proxy Hessian, preserved at an extra cost of only **0.16 bits**. Reports improved accuracy across the extremely low-bit regime, attributing gains to the combination of geometric structure, lattice codebooks and selective channel preservation (abstract gives no per-benchmark numbers).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22721

### 2611.22186 Is 4-Bit Quantization More Than Noise to a Chat Model's Safety Behaviour?
Pre-registered study of three 3–4B chat models, with paired tests, blind human labels and an 8-bit null. Finds two 4-bit shifts of **opposite sign**: with a compliant reply prefilled, Qwen2.5-3B continues into harmful content more often, while on held-out jailbreaks Llama-3.2-3B complies **10.3 points less** often. Matched perturbations separate the two — shuffled and Gaussian versions of the real rounding error average near zero, while scaling the real error (from its mirror image to twice its size) changes behavior near-linearly. At **half** the real error Llama's likelihood is almost unchanged yet it already complies **5.8 points less** often; llama.cpp's Q4_K_M at the same bits/weight moves neither model. Conclusion: refusal under 4-bit quantization depends on the *specific rounding error*, not bits-per-weight or likelihood — so each build must be compared with its original rather than assumed equivalent.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22186

### 2611.23035 Storing the Gemma 4 K=V Cache Once: Exact Global-KV Memory Savings in a Paged Serving Engine with Unchanged Attention Kernels
Notes that Gemma 4 31B's global attention layers make the value a normalized key projection and the key a scaled value (with RoPE on a quarter of dimensions), which the Gemma 4 report says cuts the global KV cache by up to **37.5%** — yet open serving engines still store a full key and a full value (1,024 values per KV head per token). The authors store one **640-value** row instead, exact in real arithmetic, and read it through two overlapping strided views via an *unchanged* paged attention kernel. In a vLLM prototype with the 16-bit 31B model, outputs are bit-identical to an emulation of the same arithmetic on the stock cache (2 of 432 final answers differ from the stock engine, within its run-to-run noise) and steady decode KV memory per request falls by a factor of **1.34–1.40** at 32K–64K tokens. On a prefill/decode pair of two A100s with NIXL transfer and CPU offload, the compact engine ran **1.38x** the stock decode tokens/step for 16K-token prompts and **1.24x** for 8K; a block-pool model predicted both ratios within 1%. Eager decode throughput rose **1.30x** and **1.11x**. Caveats: the eager step is 4–9% slower at equal batch, and the method does not yet stack with an 8-bit KV cache at default scale.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23035

### 2611.22280 XM-Sparse: High-Throughput Sparse-Attention LLM Serving over Scale-up Memory Pools
Analysis shows that even with scale-up memory pools, decode throughput stays bounded by HBM capacity because hot KV buffers and the fully resident indexer key (K-index) cache occupy substantial HBM — and the two caches have distinct access patterns. XM-Sparse is the first sparse-attention offloading system over scale-up memory expansion: it offloads both caches aggressively but matches each with an I/O mechanism suited to its pattern (TMA-based retrieval fused with attention in a tile-level pipeline for dynamically selected KV entries; SM-efficient GPU-initiated DMA prefetch, coordinated with latency-critical work, for predictable K-index accesses). Implemented atop **SGLang**; on DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5.3 with an emulated scale-up pool it delivers up to **4.98x** and **4.52x** higher decode throughput than PCIe-based ECHO and HiSparse, and up to **1.34x** over a naive integration on the same platform, across 16K–1M contexts.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22280

### 2611.21852 The Hidden Cost of MoE Diffusion Decoding: Profiling and Fixing the Sampler
Profiling single-user decoding of a 26B diffusion MoE on a bandwidth-bound edge accelerator (NVIDIA GB10) finds that after weight quantization the *logits sampling/stopping pipeline*, not the expert GEMMs, is the single largest consumer of decode time — because diffusion decoding processes S times as many logit entries per output token as autoregressive decoding (S = denoising steps). Two lossless removal tiers: a bit-exact tier (byte-identical output) and an accuracy-parity tier using a fused single-pass entropy kernel with Gumbel-max sampling. With a W4A16 expert kernel the full stack reaches **2.4–2.7x** end-to-end speedup over bf16 at unchanged accuracy on GSM8K, HumanEval and MATH on a single 273 GB/s device. Confirmed on a second independently built diffusion MoE (LLaDA2.0-mini: pipeline **28.8%** of decode, fused W4A16 **3.3x** at accuracy parity), and the effect disappears on a dense diffusion model (**3.6%**).
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21852

### 2611.21917 Lost in a Few Heads: Diagnosing and Repairing Block Selection in Sparse Attention
Uses exact attention-mass selection at the shipped budget as an oracle to show the selector *under-ranks the needle* rather than running out of budget. A calibration probe finds "blind heads" that attend to the needle while a mean-pooled key summary dilutes its decisive token. Four InfLLM-V2 checkpoints and NOSA-8B lose multi-key retrieval in the *same three* blind heads. Fixing them — full attention in those heads, or a **4-bit scorer** that keeps the shipped budget — recovers most of the loss with no training: on MiniCPM4.1-8B at 64K, either fix lifts multi-key retrieval accuracy from **54.1 to 97.5%**, level with full attention in every head. In production engines, full attention in those heads retains **84–89%** of the TTFT that sparse attention saves at 32K–128K context. The same small set of blind heads carries the loss for SeerAttention-R, NSA and DashAttention, whereas MoBA has many such heads; on Qwen3-8B with SeerAttention-R gates, full attention in **18 of 288** KV heads lifts multi-key retrieval from **29.8 to 97.7%**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21917

### 2611.19455 Text Speeding Itself Up: Recency and Restraint in Session-Local Drafting
A careful negative-ish result on training-free speculative drafting. Compares a recency-weighted, backing-off, confidence-gated *session memory* drafter against prompt lookup decoding (reference earliest-match form and a most-recent-match variant), with paired per-prompt bootstrap intervals. On general, medical and legal text with a Mistral-7B verifier: recency accounts for the speed — most-recent lookup gains up to **0.09** in speedup over earliest-match on 500-token generations, while session memory's weighting and backoff add **nothing measurable** on top, at **30–40x** the drafting cost. Energy tells a different story: unselective drafters are **14%** faster than plain decoding yet use as much energy per token, while abstaining drafters save **3–4 joules per 100 tokens**; a three-token minimum match length on prompt lookup matches the confidence-gated session memory. Outputs are token-identical to greedy decoding in **94%** of generations, with every divergence at a numerical near-tie. Under a repetition penalty, long-generation speedups shrink from **1.4–1.7x** to **1.1–1.2x**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19455

### 2611.20119 CECAR: Cache & Expert Co-Aware Routing Accelerates On-Device Inference of MoE LLMs
Identifies three observations for on-device MoE: cache hit rates are fundamentally bounded even under Belady's optimal policy; those bounds are insufficient for low-latency inference; and prior binary cache-presence-aware routing induces routing instability. CECAR jointly considers expert routing and cache management, using an ML-based cache policy to predict each expert's expected reuse distance and prioritizing routing/execution toward experts with smaller predicted reuse distance. On Qwen3-30B-A3B, CECAR achieves **12.5 tokens/s** on a consumer GPU with only **16 GB VRAM** (non-resident experts fetched from SSD), a **4.5x** speedup over conventional MoE inference, exceeding baselines that keep the whole model resident in DRAM.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20119

### 2611.21974 Beyond Bitwidth: Deployment-Constrained Mixed-Precision Quantization for Multilingual LLM Inference on NVIDIA Blackwell
Deployment-flavored quantization study on Qwen3-8B / NVIDIA B200 / vLLM, covering INT2/INT3, GPTQ, NVFP4 and mixed precision, with native packed-execution verification across all **144 fused linear modules** and preregistered quality gates on Korean, English, numerical and reasoning items. Packed INT2/INT3 execute but **all seven tested INT2 grids degenerate** on generation; isolated module-restoration effects are strongly **non-additive**, Korean and reasoning sensitivity rankings disagree, and a promising INT4/INT8 candidate fails independent final evaluation. The passing configuration (16-bit activations, 98 NVFP4 weight-only modules, 46 INT8 modules, INT8 output head) meets all gates on the untouched FINAL-81 set (5,168 items): **5.917 GiB** weight-loading memory (limit 5.98), Korean teacher-correct retention **0.904** (gate 0.90), TPOT ratio **0.939** vs BF16; it improves paired accuracy over NVFP4 W4A4 by **+1.4 pp** (Bonferroni-adjusted [+0.2, +2.6]) versus **+3.4 pp** on the selection set — a selection-vs-holdout gap. Caveats: the comparison changes both activation precision and INT8 protection, so the isolated allocation effect is not established; final checkpoints were not retained and bitwise rebuild reproducibility is unverified.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21974

### 2611.22206 TAILROLL: Tail-Aware Rollout Acceleration for RL-Based LLM Post-Training
RL post-training is bottlenecked by rollout: heavy-tailed responses leave engines idle while each step waits for the longest response. TAILROLL separates *imbalance waste* (uneven placement, decode contention) from *straggler waste*, and combines stateless in-flight migration (moving decode requests between engines at chunk boundaries, resuming with one re-prefill under a freeness-based, cost-aware tail-balancing policy) with sufficiency-gated termination (stopping a response once its GRPO group has enough completed samples for advantage estimation, masking the partial output from the loss). Across ten configurations it reduces rollout bubble ratio by **21–74%** and cross-engine KV-cache imbalance by **44–79%**; end-to-end it cuts rollout time **24–34%**, full step time **21–22%** on long-CoT RL, and p95 request latency **28–37%**, while raising generation throughput by up to **33%** with comparable or higher final reward.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22206

### 2611.22947 The Kernel Reality Check: Benchmarking and Distilling Efficient Attention at Scale
Systematic evaluation of kernelized (O(N)) attention against softmax. Two headline results: (1) FlashAttention achieves **lower memory and faster wall-clock** than SOTA kernelized methods during training and in vision transformers at most sequence lengths — with inference-time memory in causal LMs the only clear exception — contradicting the core drop-in-replacement assumption; reported efficiency gains also often fail to reproduce against modern baselines. (2) Quality: current kernelized methods leave **2–10 point** gaps vs softmax under a unified distillation protocol, and model scale alone does not close them. The gap is shown not intrinsic to kernelization — LARA++, a principled extension approximating softmax via importance sampling with adaptive proposal distributions, recovers up to **99%** of softmax accuracy — but the computational overhead remains substantial, "leaving the efficiency wall intact". Code released.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22947

### 2611.22238 Context Has a Price: Adding and Removing Context for LLM Coding Agents under Prefix Caching
A cost-measurement study over **>20,000 runs** of open models on SWE-bench Verified, SWE-bench and Loc-Bench, accounting separately for uncached input, cached input and output tokens. Three findings: (1) retrieved code reduces read and search commands by **18–34%** but *increases* cost by **33–42%** when cached input costs one tenth of uncached input, with no measurable improvement in resolution; (2) removing old observations can either save money or increase it — the same approach saves **19–36%** without a cache discount but increases cost by **38–116%** with a tenth-price cache; (3) shorter prompts do not guarantee cheaper runs, because agents compensate by taking more steps. Also finds concurrent experiments can share cached prefixes and distort cost comparisons. Recommendation: evaluate context-management methods using both token prices and the agent's subsequent behavior.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22238

### 2611.18859 Exstill: The Most Efficient Single-Chip Processor for the Next Token
A silicon-and-model co-design study. Gate-level measurements at 5 nm place the arithmetic floor for a Qwen3-1.7B token at 16K context at **0.89 mJ**, yet an NVIDIA B300 spends **391–1,120x** that. Fixing one model architecture in the datapath with weights still loadable and holding weights plus each conversation's KV cache on-die, a pre-silicon 5 nm design brings an unchanged Qwen3-1.7B token to **3.4x** its floor; co-designing the model with the circuits at fixed quality lowers the floor to **0.29 mJ** and the token to **0.52 mJ** (1.8x the new floor, less than the baseline model's arithmetic alone), while on-die capacity grows from one user to twelve. Against a measured B300 serving the same twelve users, the design decodes each user's tokens **110x** faster at **690x** lower energy. Also introduces Polyglot-DC (1.7B and 4B) co-processors specialized per job via distillation and task-specific training.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18859
