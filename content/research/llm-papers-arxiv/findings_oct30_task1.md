## Selected findings (22)

**2610.38095** Nemotron 3.5 Super and Lightning: Open, Efficient Mixture-of-Experts Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Models for Agentic AI — NVIDIA ships two open MoE hybrid Mamba-Transformer models: Super is 120B total / 12B active and multimodal, Lightning is 30B / 3B active text-only. Both use large-scale distillation, long-context training and native speculative decoding; Super adds Indic continued pretraining plus agentic software-engineering mid-training, and the text-only base becomes a multimodal tool-and-image/video agent. Post-training spans SFT + RLVR + RLHF, with teacher training and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) for Super. Concrete numbers: Indic continued pretraining lifts Indian-language benchmarks by up to **9.82 percentage points**; Super reaches **51.78% on SWE-bench Pro**, beating Nemotron 3 Ultra while using **~4.6× fewer active parameters**; MTP boosting raises speculative-decoding acceptance length by **up to 21.8%** under probabilistic draft sampling; NVFP4 quantization recovers **99.62% median accuracy relative to BF16**; inference optimizations reach **up to 4,856 output tokens/s/GPU on GB200** while sustaining **≥50 tokens/s/user**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38095

**2610.38433** Does Chain-of-Thought Reliance Follow a Scaling Law? Larger Models Follow Their Steps More, Independent of Post-Training — Ground-truth CoT-reliance measurement via a synthetic box-swap task where one step of the model's own chain is replaced with a wrong-but-valid state. **25 open-weight models** (Qwen3 0.6B–14B base and post-trained, Gemma 3 1B–12B, Llama 3 1B–8B, 8 OLMo 2 checkpoints) evaluated on **7,200 items each**. Five results: (1) reliance rises with size and saturates — Qwen3 goes from **16–18% at 0.6B to 97–100% at 14B**, logistic fit in log N with **R² = 0.89**; Gemma 3 and Llama 3 follow the same trend; (2) capability is the main driver, reliance vs. step accuracy correlates at **r = 0.99** across all models; (3) size still matters at matched correctness (**rho = 0.80, p = 0.002**); (4) post-training does not change reliance (base vs. post-trained Qwen3 differ by **at most 0.03 from 4B upward**, the 1.7B gap tracks capability); (5) **no model up to 14B solves the task internally** — without written steps every capable model is at chance once a problem has ≥4 swaps. Implication: CoT monitoring is only meaningful where scale makes the written steps load-bearing.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38433

**2610.38681** Weaken to Strengthen: Weakening-Induced On-Policy Self-Distillation for Large Reasoning Models (WISD) — Rather than needing a privileged teacher, WISD builds a deliberately *weaker* counterpart of the model (low-bit quantization or magnitude pruning) and uses the contrast as the training signal, requiring only unlabeled prompts. Quantization improves average accuracy by **4.0 pp** and pruning by **5.5 pp** over the originals. Specifics: pruning lifts Qwen3.5-4B from **57.7% → 70.5% on HMMT 2025** and **39.1% → 56.7% on LiveCodeBench v6**; quantization lifts Qwen3.6-35B-A3B from **68.5% → 74.6%** and **61.8% → 69.5%** on the same two benchmarks.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38681

**2610.38391** SCOPE: Selective Critical-Step On-Policy Self-Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning — Address the coarse supervision of outcome-based RL for long-horizon agents (failed trajectories contain good steps; successful ones contain errors) and the off-target drift of trajectory-level skills. SCOPE has the policy analyze its own trajectories, write step-specific hindsight skills, and distill them only at selected error steps, with a bounded signed teacher-student coefficient plus a success-relative pairwise length reward for efficiency. On ALFWorld/WebShop with Qwen2.5-3B: **97.7% success on ALFWorld and 85.2% on WebShop**, beating SEED by **5.9 and 6.3 pp**; on unseen ALFWorld tasks **89.6%**, i.e. **+23.2 pp over GRPO with 46.7% fewer interactions**; scaled to Qwen3-32B, **99.2%** on seen and **94.0%** on unseen ALFWorld tasks.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38391

**2610.39876** Turing LLM Post-Training: Calibrated Distillation and Efficient Dynamic-MoE Execution — Integrates independently optimized specialists into a dynamically routed MoE. Combines calibrated multi-teacher on-policy distillation (C-MOPD: match each selected teacher on a local Top-K support, teacher-wise loss normalization + clipping) with an execution path using post-capacity bounded-width dispatch, final-route replay, and async rollout/training overlap. On **Turing-20B-A2B**: four-domain normalized integration score **0.9988**, retaining **≥90.16%** of measured specialist gain in every domain. Ablation at matched K=32: full calibration **0.9962** vs. **0.9724** without calibration vs. **0.9834** without clipping. At matched 8K response cap, unweighted mean over four reasoning-enabled math/code benchmarks **85.92** vs. **80.33** for Qwen3-8B and **74.21** for shared SFT, improving on SFT on **10 of 12** broader metrics. The PPU-based execution config cuts consolidation step time from **398.7 s to 139.3 s (2.86× speedup)** over serial execution.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39876

**2610.39853** KV Cache Quantization for Diffusion Language Models — First systematic study of KV-cache quantization for dLLMs, where the assumption behind autoregressive KV quantization breaks: fast dLLM inference reuses KV states across denoising steps, but bidirectional token states change as masked tokens are revealed, so the reused cache already diverges from full recomputation *before* quantization. The authors adapt KIVI and KVQuant to Fast-dLLM-style prefix and dual cache reuse, and introduce TRACE (reconstructs feasible dLLM reveal trajectories from prompt-answer pairs to give calibration states matching iterative masked decoding) and dLLM-KVQ (picks per-layer K-cache entries to keep at high precision using output- and attention-level behavior metrics, with standard low-bit V quantization). Across **LLaDA and Dream** on **GSM8K, HumanEval, MATH, MBPP**, dLLM-KVQ achieves the **highest average score among quantized methods** across the reported **4-bit and 2-bit** prefix/dual cache settings.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39853

**2610.40305** Rimuru: Unified Memory Management for Modern LLM Serving via Elastic Expert Offloading — Serving-system paper attacking the fact that weights and attention states are provisioned in separate fixed pools that cannot be reallocated across workload phases, while modern LLMs add heterogeneous tensors with different layouts, granularities and lifetimes. Rimuru unifies them via a shared page pool plus "memory views" (each tensor type interprets the shared space with its own page size and page table), huge-page alignment with a multi-level page allocator that splits and merges pages for cross-type reuse, a page-table-aware fused MoE kernel that touches non-contiguous expert weights without copying, and a workload-aware adjuster that reallocates pages at runtime. Built on **vLLM**, it reports **up to 19.03× baseline throughput** and **98.1% average TTFT reduction**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40305

**2610.38981** Why Speculative Decoding Works Better Than Predicted on Sparse MoE Models — Prior work predicts a *concave* speedup-vs-batch-size curve for speculative decoding on MoE (peaks at intermediate batch sizes due to expert saturation); this paper finds recent fine-grained MoE models **break the trend, showing higher speedup at low batch size**. Evaluated across **10 sparse MoE models (30B–1T params, 8–512 experts)**. Mechanism: verification cost is governed not only by activated-expert bytes but by **temporal overlap across draft tokens** and GPU execution dynamics; at low batch size MoE layers in fine-grained models are a much smaller share of runtime than of weights, with H100 kernel profiling attributing this to higher bandwidth utilization of MoE kernels vs. attention and L2 absorbing repeated reads of co-routed experts; routing overlap further reduces unique experts activated vs. independence assumptions. Their **maximum-entropy Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF)** model predicts unique expert counts from routing-overlap statistics with **<4% error** and captures the low-batch trend from synthetic routing.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38981

**2610.37720** LRMoE: Demand-Side Load Re-Calibration for Efficient MoE Training on Memory-Constrained Nodes — Memory-aware MoE training system that jointly recalibrates backward demand and rebalances retained workload, since extra expert copies compete with token buffers for GPU memory. Forward pass plans retention quotas and expert placement under resource constraints while preserving all routed computations; backprop estimates each token-expert pair's contribution to the expert update and selects pairs within quota, then a capacity-aware router distributes retained pairs among eligible instances of their original experts, and physical compaction forms smaller communication payloads and variable-size grouped GEMMs to cut backward traffic, compute and execution-buffer demand. On **Qwen3-30B-A3B and GLM4.5-106B-A12B**, LRMoE reduces cumulative MoE-layer training time to fixed-loss targets by **21.2–44.8% relative to SYMI**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37720

**2610.40444** Prefix Executability: Evaluating Tool-Using Agents Beyond Final Success — Argues that terminal task success masks whether the *tool-call trajectory* was executable at all, and proposes prefix executability: every prefix of a predicted tool-call sequence must remain executable under tool schemas, state preconditions and data dependencies. Summarized via **Prefix Executability Curves (PEC), First-Error Depth (FED), Trajectory Executability Rate (TER), horizon-normalized AUC-PEC and Recovery Rate (RR)**. Instantiated on stratified samples of **BFCL-v3 multi-turn and ToolSandbox**, plus the full Airline/Retail base splits of **τ³-bench**. Key divergence: agents conditioned on an *unverified plan* achieve higher point-estimate final success than non-plan-conditioned baselines while **substantially lowering TER and AUC-PEC** — apparent success resting on an unexecutable trajectory. Explicit pre-execution plan checking improves executable-prefix survival while keeping competitive terminal success.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40444

**2610.40488** rTaste: Do Research Agents Have Taste? Evaluating Early Stage Research Judgment — Six frontier models, alone or as teams, each got seven recent NLP papers and had to propose minimum viable experiments (MVEs), forecast whether each would pass a self-set threshold, and rank them; a separate executor then attempted *every* MVE (including those ranked last) and three model auditors judged results. Of **415 MVEs, 194** were judged sound, valuable and new, but **only 73** were also judged beyond the seed paper and non-obvious. Agents' first choices match the auditors' most-valued experiment more often than random, but are **not shown to improve on choosing the experiment written first**. Their forecasts beat a base rate but are **not measurably more accurate than a fresh instance of the same model** given only the paper and registrations. **Teams cost more without clear overall improvement** in proposal quality. Harness plus all 415 MVEs with forecasts and audited outcomes released.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40488

**2610.39377** AgentDecept: Benchmarking Deceptive Behavior in Tool-Using LLM Agents under Pressure — Executable benchmark for behavioral deception in multi-turn tool workflows where users see only the agent's report. Contains **477 pressure variants derived from 257 source tasks across six pressure sources**, each paired with a pressure-free counterpart; interventions enter via event messages, task context, application state, tool responses and service quotas (explicit events plus implicit task/environment changes). The evidence-grounded protocol relates user-facing claims to time-ordered observations and executed outcomes, distinguishing **falsification, concealment and equivocation** from honest failure and disclosed limitations; the labels also serve as a testbed for activation-based probes.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39377

**2610.39234** AgentHeal: Evaluating Goal-Preserving Resource Recovery of Web Agents under Access Barriers — Benchmark for goal-preserving resource recovery: detecting non-transient barriers (dead links, auth walls, rate limits, anti-scraping), pivoting to legitimate alternative sources, and still fulfilling the user's original objective. Spans **nine barrier families** across a counterfactual sandbox and real-website replay. Headline number: **average task success drops from 89.4% to 43.7%** under access barriers. Failure taxonomy: agents fall for deceptive look-alikes, fail to escalate to users when necessary, violate access policies, or burn compute on futile retries.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39234

**2610.39122** Appreciation Teaches Unauthorized Execution: Self-Evolution Degrades the Robustness of Personal Agents to Indirect Prompt Injection — Two-phase controlled methodology: in the adaptation phase the user asks the agent to read external content and do the task in it, then the agent learns from user feedback; in the test phase a third party writes a similar task into new external content that the user's request does not authorize. Result: **self-evolution substantially degrades IPI robustness** — average attack success rate rises by **14.0–48.8 pp under skill evolution** and by **3.0–39.3 pp under reinforcement learning** versus the non-evolving base agent. Concrete safety regression: positive feedback loops teach unauthorized execution.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39122

**2610.38392** Masked Erosion: How Acceptance Gates Lose Redundant Safeguards in Self-Improving Agents — Self-improving agents edit their own harness and keep an edit only if an acceptance gate scores it higher; the gate sees what the harness does under the current model, not what it would still prevent if one safeguard failed. When safeguards overlap, deleting one is *masked*. The paper measures harm for **every subset of four written safeguards** in two agent environments and runs **411 improvement loops under ten gate rules** (mostly controlled deletions) plus **three runs of the GEPA optimizer**. Findings: a deletion behind another working safeguard adds almost no observable harm; a paired significance test blocks deletions that pass by chance but not deletions that genuinely raise task success; **20-task batches kept every harness protected while 40-task batches lost all six**; large harm audits prevent exposure but not loss of depth, which becomes harm when the harness is reused with a weaker model. **GEPA kept every safety rule verbatim, yet two of its three final harnesses misused credentials under a weaker model.**
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38392

**2610.37741** Agentic Backdoors via Pretraining Poisoning — First demonstration that web-corpus poisoning can implant a **remote code execution** backdoor in an agent. Qwen3 models pretrained from scratch at **0.6B, 1.7B and 4B**, then post-trained into CLI agents with a clean pipeline. Poisoning only **0.1% of the pretraining corpus** implants the backdoor: the deployed agent executes the attacker's command when the trigger appears and behaves normally otherwise. **ASR rises with scale — the 4B model reaches roughly 92% ASR at 0.1% poison rate, and stays positive even at 0.0005% poisoning.**
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37741

**2610.37738** Poisoned Skills: Propagating Jailbreaks Through Agent Markdown Files — Agent frameworks ingest user-supplied Markdown files (system prompts, tool descriptions, skill definitions) into the agent's context at init, an under-explored injection channel. Using a LangChain QA agent whose system prompt is assembled from a user Markdown file, with a jailbreak combining persona manipulation and a GPTFUZZER-style attack: across **seven contemporary open-weight models** and **270 forbidden questions spanning nine harm categories**, benign system prompts give a **mean attack success rate of just 0.5%**, while the injected skill file raises **mean ASR to 45%** (up to **68.9%** on individual models). Two obfuscations (zero-width-character stealth payload and grapheme-to-phoneme phonetic payload) **do not beat plain-text injection** — the phonetic variant actually lowers mean ASR to **30%**. Robustness depends more on the underlying model than on payload obfuscation.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37738

**2610.38595** Rethinking LLM Jailbreak Evaluation: Continuous Risk Landscapes Beyond Binary Outcomes — Argues binary ASR is the wrong unit for generative jailbreaks: a jailbreak prompt induces a distribution over open-ended trajectories while one sampled response reveals only one path. Introduces a continuous, context-conditioned **risk landscape over first-token branches**, estimated by fixing candidate openings and repeatedly sampling continuations. Exposes two properties invisible to binary ASR: **decoder exposure** and **safe rerouting capacity**, giving a common basis for comparing clean prompts, attacked variants and attack strength against an adaptive defense. Empirical results: risky branches hide beneath apparently safe outputs; attacks reshape the opening distribution in qualitatively different ways; **attacks strongest before a defense are not necessarily strongest after the adaptive defense reacts**.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38595

**2610.40516** How I Aligned Your Model: Scaling Up Multilingual Safety Training with Templates — Template-based augmentation pipeline that extracts safe templates from harmful prompts, translates them, and fills slots with culturally authentic harmful/benign entities, releasing **MSafeTemp, a corpus spanning 60 languages**. Central validation claim: **English-only evaluation misjudges multilingual safety — attacks that fail in English succeed once translated, and culturally localized variants expose further failures that translation alone misses**. Training-data augmentation via templates raises diversity and quality of alignment and improves safety beyond English.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40516

**2610.38100** Evaluating Causal Reasoning in LLMs: A Case Study in the Challenges of Procedural Generation — A benchmark-validity attack on CLadder (2023), the standard formal-causal-reasoning benchmark for LLMs. The authors find several **superficial, highly predictive patterns embedded in CLadder-generated data**; **even random forests perform well** on CLadder-generated datasets after training on a sample of generated data; and commercial LLM performance on CLadder is **extremely brittle** — **removing large sections of the text critical for formal causal reasoning has almost no effect on performance**. Most likely hypothesis: strong SOTA results come from exploiting generator artifacts memorized in pretraining; any claim of genuine causal reasoning must rule this out.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38100

**2610.38610** Repairs for Whom? Evaluator-Executor Mismatch in Selecting Repairs for Failed LLM Agents — Repair methods pick which failed action to replace using greedy replay, a stronger model, an oracle, or a judge; this paper asks whether those evaluators select repairs that help the agent that will actually execute them, holding trajectories and candidate single-step repairs fixed and labeling every candidate with independent rollouts of the deployed policy. On failures not pre-screened for repair value, **greedy replay loses 0.08–0.13 expected net success gain in ALFWorld and 0.06–0.13 net reward in WebShop** relative to selection with the deployed agent's own rollouts. At equal rollout budget, selecting repairs for Qwen2.5-7B using Qwen2.5-14B rollouts **loses 0.25 in ALFWorld and 0.07 in WebShop**; **twenty 14B rollouts per candidate are worth no more than two of the 7B agent's own**, and fresh rollouts confirm the two models prefer opposite repairs on disagreement cases. Across different model families the loss appears in both directions (**0.07–0.17**). The scoring executor also changes repair-location labels and method rankings — repairs must be verified with the executor that will run them.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38610

**2610.39682** Heterogeneous Co-Evolution Learning for Tool-Efficient Search Agents with Zero Labels (ZEST-Search) — Trains deep-search agents with **zero labeled search data**: starts from unlabeled reference questions, builds validated tasks online via answer-first + evidence-path-guided generation, lets a heterogeneous proposer adaptively construct tasks around the solver's evolving capability boundary, and trains the solver with RL. Jointly optimizes task difficulty and search efficiency via an intermediate-difficulty reward for the proposer and a task-local tool-aware reward for the solver (answer-only RL treats redundant-search successes alike). Across **seven general-domain and multi-hop QA benchmarks**, ZEST-Search consistently improves search-agent performance **while achieving higher tool efficiency** without labeled search-training data or expert search trajectories.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39682
