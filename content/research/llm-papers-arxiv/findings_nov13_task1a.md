### Read Once, Answer Many: Parallel Prompting for Shared-Context LLM Inference
arXiv: 2611.15130v1 | cs.CL
When many independent questions target the same long document, prefix caching removes redundant prefill but each decode step still re-reads the document's KV cache once per question, so decoding stays memory-bandwidth bound. The authors decode all P questions as P independent streams of a single request, with a stream-id attention mask evaluated inside the attention kernel so each answer attends only to the document and its own question; the implementation adds roughly 80 lines to three SGLang files, requires no dense mask tensor, and keeps a fixed decode shape that can be CUDA-graph captured. With CodeLlama-7B, 8k-16k token documents and 64-256 questions per document, the authors report 3.7x speedup over stock SGLang with FlashInfer on an A100 and 5.9-6.0x on an RTX A6000, faster in all 144 runs (2 GPUs x 2 releases x 6 workloads x 6 input sets). Code: https://github.com/jlab-nlp/parallel-prompting. The caveat is that the method only pays off in the shared-prefix multi-question regime and was benchmarked on a single 7B model family, so generalization to larger models or to mixed-length question batches is untested.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15130v1

### Early Signals, Strong Decisions: Prefix-Guided Sampling for Parallel Test-Time Scaling
arXiv: 2611.14965v1 | cs.AI
Parallel test-time scaling (TTS) improves reasoning by generating and aggregating multiple reasoning paths, but internal confidence is usually consulted only after low-quality paths are fully expanded, wasting compute. This paper argues that confidence matters most for questions near the decision boundary, where multi-sample aggregation can amplify even weak confidence signals derived from short prefixes, and introduces Prefix-Guided Sampling (PreG), which generates short prefixes, ranks them by internal confidence, and completes only the most promising candidates. The authors add a theoretical analysis showing that under a fixed compute budget PreG widens the effective decision boundary margin, and report consistent gains over standard parallel TTS across benchmarks together with significant token-efficiency and compute reductions. A caveat inferable from the abstract is that the reported evidence is aggregate: it reports that the method "consistently outperforms" without stating per-benchmark deltas or the absolute accuracy figures, and the gains depend on internal confidence scores being calibrated enough to rank truncated prefixes.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14965v1

### The Illusion of Diversity: Aligning LLM Exploration via Effective Entropy
arXiv: 2611.15493v1 | cs.LG
RLVR pipelines commonly use token-level or sequence-level entropy as an exploration signal, but such "blind" entropy cannot separate genuine reasoning diversity from hallucinated degeneration. The paper introduces Effective Entropy, a validity-aware entropy computed only inside the subspace of valid (reward-correct) trajectories, and VEPO, a Validity-aware Entropy Policy Optimization method that plugs this term directly into RLVR training, inducing an adaptive attraction dynamic toward under-explored valid trajectories, which the authors argue matches the regime of large autoregressive models where sequence probabilities are tiny. On six mathematical reasoning benchmarks with 1.5B and 7B DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen models, the authors report consistent aggregate accuracy gains over GRPO and competitive repeated-sampling success, with a matched-correct-count evaluation finding more distinct strategies on commonly solved MATH500 problems, backed by paired statistical tests and three-seed training runs. The main caveat is scale: all evidence comes from distilled 1.5B/7B models on math-only benchmarks, so it is unclear whether validity-aware entropy still helps on frontier-scale models or on non-verifiable domains where no validity mask exists.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15493v1

### Is the Importance Ratio Necessary for Stable Reinforcement Learning in LLMs?
arXiv: 2611.15404v1 | cs.LG
GRPO-style RL for LLMs computes both old-policy and current-policy likelihoods to form importance-sampling ratios, which costs real training time. This paper proposes Likelihood-Gated Policy Optimization (LGPO), which enforces a soft trust region via likelihood-based gating and eliminates old-policy likelihood computation entirely, arguing that the ratio correction is not what provides stability - the trust region is. The authors show that ratio-based clipping can silently never activate in fully on-policy training, where the importance ratio stays at 1, and that in that regime GRPO fails while LGPO remains stable. Where GRPO is stable, LGPO matches stability and peak performance while cutting training time by roughly 18% on average versus an implementation that recomputes old-policy likelihoods. The caveat is that the trust region is enforced through a different mechanism, so the speedup is measured against a specific baseline implementation, and the abstract reports no numbers for the fully on-policy failure case beyond a qualitative claim that GRPO fails.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15404v1

### Trait Hitchhiking in LLM Post-Training
arXiv: 2611.14683v1 | cs.LG
RL post-training is credited with capability gains, but it also perturbs behaviors and internal properties the reward never targeted. This paper formalizes the evolution of "traits" during RL post-training and recovers the Price equation from evolutionary biology, then derives a mathematical model of "trait hitchhiking": non-targeted traits being amplified or suppressed as training redistributes probability mass across completions. Evaluated on language models trained with GRPO, the authors report that the Price-update estimates closely track the sampled trait dynamics, identifying hitchhiking as a concrete mechanism by which RL can silently amplify undesirable traits. The main caveat is that the abstract reports no quantitative effect sizes for how large the hitchhiking effect is in practice, and the evaluation appears restricted to GRPO-trained models, so generality to PPO or DPO-style post-training is not established.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14683v1

### Abstention Is Not Threshold Compliance: Stated Scoring Rules in Open Language Models
arXiv: 2611.14661v1 | cs.CL
The paper probes whether models actually comply when a prompt states a scoring rule with an explicit correctness threshold t, testing 12 checkpoints across three post-training stages of four fully open 3-8B recipes (plus exploratory models up to 32B). Adding a format line limiting replies to an answer or "I abstain" raised the mean abstention rate from 20% to 91% at t = 0.75, yet within that format raising t from 0.5 to 0.9 moved abstention by only -0.8 percentage points, and without the line a higher threshold even lowered abstention; up to 40% of a checkpoint's items received decisions no compliant policy with threshold-independent beliefs could make. Post-hoc calibration on the evaluation distribution beat the abstention format at t = 0.5 but fell below it at 0.75 when calibrated on TriviaQA and applied to PopQA, and threshold-conditioned training on OLMo 3 7B did not follow unseen thresholds consistently. The key caveat is measurement: the work measures stated-rule compliance, not raw accuracy, and its conclusions about P(True)-style self-assessment signals are shown to depend on the grader and on answer drift between prompts.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14661v1

### When Is Proof-Step Verification Worth Its Cost? Operating Requirements of Certify-or-Decline Verification for Open-Weight Mathematical Reasoning
arXiv: 2611.14794v1 | cs.AI
The paper evaluates a certify-or-decline pipeline that rewrites a solver's answer into typed proof steps and asks LLM judges to certify or decline, across open-weight models on GPQA, AIME-2025 and a MATH-500 subset. Certification raises answer-key-match precision by 31 percentage points on the strongest GPQA configuration and 46 points on AIME, but answers only 35% and 21% of attempts respectively; on near-ceiling MATH-500 the gains are at most 5 points for a 13-27 point coverage loss. An output-token-matched self-consistency selector reaches 82.9% precision at 35% coverage versus the verifier's pooled 87.6% (paired difference +4.8 points, 95% interval [-8.2, 18.5]), and in a diagnostic sample 52 of 88 completed problems ended in invalid proof construction before ever reaching a judge. The caveat is that external-LLM acceptance of rendered certified chains is only 61.0-67.6% against 87.6% answer-key match, and the study has no human or formal-kernel ground truth, so the audits measure consistency rather than proof validity.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14794v1

### Termination Lag: Coding Agents Solve Early but Stop Late
arXiv: 2611.14960v1 | cs.AI
The paper characterizes "termination lag" - the number of agent steps between its first correct patch and the end of the run - by recording and testing every distinct repository patch produced during execution, across five models, two agent frameworks and three SWE-bench datasets. The authors find that 38-82% of successful runs already contain a correct solution at the initial source-code edit, yet post-solution steps still account for a median 35-37% of the run for Qwen3-Coder and 54-60% for Laguna-XS.2 on SWE-bench Verified and Multilingual, mostly spent verifying work and exploring the repository. A notable failure mode is that in 1-8% of cases the continued activity leads agents to undo an earlier correct patch, leaving the final solution wrong. The caveat is that the study measures behavior rather than proposing a fix, and its agent set (two frameworks, five models) is narrow, so the size of the lag may differ for long-horizon or tool-heavy tasks outside SWE-bench.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14960v1

### Verify by Doing: Co-Evolving Agent Execution and Evaluation without External Feedback
arXiv: 2611.15276v1 | cs.AI
Agent self-improvement normally needs feedback about task success, which is often unavailable at test time; the method here, Verify by Doing (VbD), co-evolves Actor and Evaluator harnesses within a single task while keeping model weights fixed. The Evaluator actively inspects artifacts and environment state with tools, and its findings ground an internal signal used for output selection and for updating both the execution and the verification procedures; proposed Evaluators face comparative review on common candidates, while Actor updates must be supported by their own fresh outputs. Across AutomationBench, tau^3 Banking and selected GDPval tasks, VbD reports the highest overall scores among methods operating without external feedback, with gains of 3.3, 4.1 and 2.3 percentage points over the strongest baselines on the main backbone, and ablations supporting the contributions of active verification and Evaluator evolution. The caveat is that gains are modest in absolute percentage points and the abstract notes that selection from fixed candidate pools already recovers successful outputs without new generation, so much of the benefit may come from better selection rather than better execution.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15276v1

### Knowledge in the Harness, Reliability in the Weights: Teacher-Free Co-Evolution of an LLM Agent
arXiv: 2611.15268v1 | cs.CL
The paper asks whether a small open model can close both the policy-knowledge gap and the consistency gap with frontier models using only its own deployment logs, without a stronger teacher. Starting from a Gemma 4 12B agent that reads its domain policy from on-demand skills, each round serves a fresh task batch, the base model diagnoses failed conversations and writes rules into the skills (a rule survives only if it passes code checks, counterexample self-review, a paired retest and a noise-calibrated validation gate), and GRPO then trains a LoRA adapter on complete multi-turn tool-using conversations. On the retail and airline domains of tau^2-bench without training or selection on official tasks, the agent improves over its starting point by 12.5 and 19.5 pass^1 points and by 18.4 and 22.0 pass^4 points, exceeding the benchmark's reference agent (same model, whole policy in system prompt) by 11.8 and 8.0 pass^1 points and 22.8 and 16.0 pass^4 points. A 2x2 ablation shows the rules rescue never-solved tasks while the trained weights make already-solved tasks reliable; the caveat is that everything is measured in customer-service dialogue domains with one 12B backbone, and the abstract notes the gap to three frontier models is only mostly, not fully, closed.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15268v1

### Fewer Expert Transfers Without Changing Routing: Expert Eviction for MoE Offloading
arXiv: 2611.15842v1 | cs.LG
MoE serving can keep most expert weights in CPU memory and copy experts to GPU on router request, so the paper measures the caching trade-off directly in a harness performing real timed CPU-to-GPU copies for OLMoE-1B-7B on an L40S. The authors find that every tested policy that freezes the expert set to decode faster than an LRU cache raises next-token loss by at least 0.067 nats, while a policy that leaves routing decisions untouched but evicts the expert whose next use lies farthest in the future copies 2.1x fewer experts than LRU at no loss in quality and within 3.5% of the theoretical minimum number of copies. The catch is that the rule needs future requests: the next sixteen tokens of demand recover 96% of the reduction, whereas a rule based on counts of past requests recovers only 27%. The caveat is the scale of the evidence: one small MoE (1B-7B) on one GPU, with no evaluation at production model sizes or under real multi-tenant load.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15842v1

### Mask-Pattern Position Augmentation: Training-Free Long-Context Extrapolation for Diffusion LLMs
arXiv: 2611.15029v1 | cs.LG
Diffusion LLMs decode by iteratively unmasking tokens under bidirectional attention, but their RoPE still limits them to the pretraining window; prior training-free extrapolation (LongLLaDA) transplants autoregressive NTK scaling and applies one static, length-only transform at every position and denoising step, ignoring the mask pattern that is unique to diffusion decoding. The proposed Mask-Pattern Position Augmentation (MPPA) linearly interpolates indices into the pretrained window and adds a per-position, per-step offset derived from cumulative mask density, restoring rotary phase resolution that interpolation collapses and shrinking as tokens are committed so late steps see near-clean interpolated geometry. On LLaDA-8B-Instruct at twice its 4096-token window, the authors report perplexity dropping from 19.51 under standard decoding to 3.12 at 8192 tokens - below the in-window figure - with 100% passkey retrieval and unchanged needle-in-a-haystack accuracy. The caveat is that this is explicitly a preliminary 2x result on a single 8B model, so behavior at 4x or beyond, or on other diffusion backbones, remains untested.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15029v1

### Outside the Purview of the Reward: Capability Drift in Multilingual Post-Training
arXiv: 2611.15717v1 | cs.CL
The paper documents how RL post-training can improve rewarded behavior while silently damaging capabilities users still need, tracking two GRPO branches from a shared supervised checkpoint in a multilingual mixture-of-experts model. The released branch gains 36.07 points on MATH but loses 19.32 F1 points on XQuAD-Indic, while XQuAD-English improves under the same scoring rule; an independently trained sibling with Indic instruction following and language-aware shaping recovers the regressing Indic scores and preserves MATH but does not restore script compliance and trades off competition mathematics and code. In a later round, judge training is followed by a sharp extraction decline from the Indic branch, which reward-free distillation from an earlier checkpoint mostly repairs. A MATH score decomposition attributes most of the measured gain to increased boxed-answer emission rather than better accuracy among boxed answers. The caveat is scope: this is a two-branch case study on one MoE with a specific recipe, so it documents a mechanism and its repair rather than a general law of capability drift.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15717v1

### A Closer Look at Language Model Introspection: Probing Information and Reporting
arXiv: 2611.15267v1 | cs.CL
The paper questions whether introspection tasks are as demanding as they appear, by training a simple linear classifier to identify which sentence in a model's context had its activations perturbed, and comparing that probe against the model's own zero-shot answers across pretraining and injection strengths. With an injection four times the typical activation norm at layer 0 and probe readout at layer 24 of OLMo 3 32B, the supervised probe reaches 98.3% accuracy after 8.4 billion training tokens while the model's own zero-shot answer stays at chance (11.1%); at the released base checkpoint the accuracies are 99.1% and 89.6%. The authors also show that location is already decodable in randomly initialized 7B and 32B networks, so the architecture can support such traces without pretraining. The caveat is interpretive rather than empirical: since the target information sits close enough to the surface for a linear probe, successful reporting of localization need not imply broader access to the model's internal computation, and it says nothing about faithful explanation of reasoning.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15267v1

### ShadowBench: Exposing Lexical Anchoring and the Illusion of Forgetting in Large Language Models
arXiv: 2611.15925v1 | cs.CL
The paper argues LLM factual knowledge is lexically anchored - models hold the facts but struggle to retrieve them without explicit name tokens - and introduces ShadowBench, a shortcut-resistant benchmark measuring Latent Entity Association via a Dual-Trait Association task. Across all model scales, including frontier GPT-5.4 and Claude-Sonnet-4.6, removing lexical anchors causes performance drops of over 20% (the "Shadow Gap"). Applying the benchmark to machine unlearning, the authors find state-of-the-art methods (Gradient Difference, NPO) achieve only superficial lexical erasure: unlearned models fail direct queries but their new Latent Entity Leakage Rate (LELR) metric shows the model reconstructing the "forgotten" entity in its internal reasoning traces in over 74% of popular-entity cases, and niche entities retain 60-90% shadow accuracy via implicit attribute correlation despite 0% LELR. The caveat is that the benchmark's synthetic DTA construction is a proxy for real-world retrieval, so the size of the Shadow Gap on naturally worded queries could differ.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15925v1

## Cross-paper themes
- **Evaluation rigor and metric skepticism dominate**: 5 of 15 papers (Abstention Is Not Threshold Compliance, When Is Proof-Step Verification Worth Its Cost, Termination Lag, ShadowBench, A Closer Look at Language Model Introspection) explicitly argue that a headline number is misleading and propose decomposition of the metric - separating abstention rate from threshold responsiveness, precision from coverage from proof validity, first-correct-patch from final-patch correctness, lexical erasure from latent leakage, and probe accuracy from self-reported accuracy.
- **RL post-training is being audited, not just tuned**: 4 of 15 papers treat GRPO/RLVR as an object of study rather than a tool - Trait Hitchhiking (Price-equation account of untargeted trait drift), Is the Importance Ratio Necessary (LGPO drops the IS ratio and keeps stability), The Illusion of Diversity (validity-aware entropy), and Outside the Purview of the Reward (capability drift across MATH/XQuAD-Indic in a multilingual MoE).
- **Inference-time compute allocation is the recurring efficiency lever**: 4 of 15 (Parallel Prompting for shared-context decoding, Prefix-Guided Sampling for parallel test-time scaling, Verify by Doing's evaluator-driven selection, Expert Eviction for MoE offloading) find large wins by changing *when* or *how* compute is spent rather than shrinking models - 3.7-6.0x decoding speedup, token-efficiency gains, 2.1x fewer expert copies.
- **Agent harness and runtime are treated as first-class objects**: 3 of 15 (Verify by Doing, Knowledge in the Harness, Termination Lag) separate the model from the harness, and all report that agent behavior is dominated by harness design - selection, verification, stopping policy and skill storage - rather than by base-model capability alone.
- **Interpretability is moving from description to intervention**: 2 of 15 (A Closer Look at Language Model Introspection, ShadowBench) combine probes or metric-level audits with a check on whether the measured information actually drives behavior; both report cases where decodable or present information does not translate into reliable, faithful behavior.
- **Diffusion LMs are treated as first-class LLM architectures**: 1 of 15 (Mask-Pattern Position Augmentation) attacks position-encoding issues specific to diffusion decoding rather than adapting autoregressive tricks, reporting perplexity 19.51 -> 3.12 at 2x the pretraining window.
- **Small, open models are the dominant testbed**: 7 of 15 evaluate on models of 0.6B-32B (distilled Qwen 1.5B/7B, OLMo 3 7B/32B, Gemma 4 12B, OLMoE-1B-7B, CodeLlama-7B), which keeps compute honest but means most headline gains are unverified at frontier scale.
