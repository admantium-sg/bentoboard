### LazyWeights: Decode-on-Demand Edge LLMs
arXiv: 2611.17108v1 | cs.LG
The authors keep standard GPTQ-style quantized weights but observe that the integer codes are far from uniformly distributed, so they Huffman-code them losslessly in interleaved lanes and decode them on demand in a small unit placed between DRAM and L2, letting GPU kernels run unchanged while DRAM moves only coded bytes. At 1% and 3.5% perplexity budgets the coded Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B and SmolLM-1.7B models are 5.7-31.4% smaller than the best llama.cpp format, and a bit-exact 0.21 mm² ASIC decoder unit (1 GHz, 7 nm) cuts Jetson Orin latency and energy per token by up to 36% and 38% (54% and 44% with prompt-lookup speculation). On a Jetson Nano the scheme needs 58-64% less time and 53-57% less energy per token than CPU llama.cpp and streams Mistral-7B 6.6× faster at 3.89 bits per weight, and the decoder is also bit-exact on a Zynq UltraScale+ FPGA. The main caveat is that the gain is bound to Huffman-friendly code distributions in already-GPTQ-quantized models; the hardware unit is a research ASIC, so the speedups are not yet reproducible on commodity hardware.

### SignRot: LLM Quantization with Massive Outlier-Aware Sign-Adjusted Rotation
arXiv: 2611.16502v1 | cs.AI
Rotation-based quantization methods (e.g. QuaRot, SpinQuant) mitigate activation outliers with randomized Hadamard transforms, but a massive outlier dominating a token's activation fragments the post-rotation distribution into empty bins, and the down-projection input position requires the rotation at inference time, which precludes learned dense matrices like SpinQuant's. SignRot identifies massive-outlier channels and constructs a sign-adjustment vector that unifies the signs of the corresponding Hadamard row, collapsing the bimodal profile into a unimodal one, paired with asymmetric activation quantization whose zero-point absorbs the remaining mean shift. Applied to QuaRot on LLaMA3-70B the authors report a WikiText2 perplexity of 5.88 and average zero-shot reasoning accuracy of 72.73, surpassing SpinQuant, at the cost of a single calibration forward pass and only element-wise sign flips at inference with no extra matrix multiplication. Code: https://github.com/HYU-ARK-Lab/SignRot The approach is only a drop-in for Hadamard-based schemes, so methods with learned rotations or no online rotation do not benefit.

### KVPath: Connecting Key-Value Representations Across Transformer Layers
arXiv: 2611.16072v1 | cs.LG
Building on evidence that a token's key-value states across neighbouring layers are correlated, KVPath treats the KV cache as a path through depth: a full-dimensional base KV is established at the first layer and each later layer produces a low-dimensional delta KV that updates the previous layer's KV, so only the base and the compact deltas must be stored. Two variants are proposed — KVPath-Residual (residual addition) and KVPath-Rolling (rolling updates over feature dimensions) — and at matched parameter counts KVPath reduces KV-cache storage by up to 68% for grouped-query attention and 55% for multi-head latent attention while maintaining or improving model performance. The caveat is that the delta formulation must be trained with the same scheme it will be cached under, so retrofitting it onto an existing checkpoint is not free, and the gains are expressed as storage ratios rather than end-to-end wall-clock speedups.

### Towards Green Text Generation: Memory-Based Speculative Decoding
arXiv: 2611.16714v1 | cs.CL
Olifant is a draft model built as a memory-based classifier that predicts the next token from a four-token window in under 0.3 ms on CPU, requiring no GPU, no backpropagation and no accelerator memory, and the authors measure throughput and energy under two verifier regimes on consumer hardware. Without a KV cache the draft is nearly free: a domain-matched Olifant reaches 38.1% acceptance on EU legal text, giving 2.43× throughput and 2.51× lower energy per token than the neural verifier alone; with a KV-cached verifier, Olifant still yields 1.25× throughput at the lowest energy in the study (93 J per 100 tokens, 14× below the uncached baseline), whereas a TinyLlama draft halves throughput and triples energy despite high acceptance. The authors also show KV caching decouples time from energy so the energy-optimal draft length sits below the speed-optimal one, though the acceptance rate is domain-dependent and 38.1% is far from the regime where speculative decoding pays off strongly.

### Symmetry in Time: A Representation Theory of Linear Recurrent Memory
arXiv: 2611.17162v1 | cs.LG
Using representation theory, the authors formalise why diagonal state-space models fail at state-tracking: the state update is a linear representation of the monoid of input sequences, and an SSM tracks a group at every sequence length exactly when that group is a quotient of the group generated by its transition matrices, so diagonal real- and complex-valued SSMs can only track commutative groups and the transition matrices of any order-tracking SSM must split into independent blocks over the reals, complexes and quaternions. Acting on that prediction they introduce HyperSSM, which adds quaternion channels while keeping linear-time inference and parallel training, and trained only on final answers it solves the group tasks of recent state-tracking benchmarks exactly with at most five state numbers, including S5 with 32 generators where previous SSMs are at chance, and generalizes to sequences 25× longer than those seen in training. The learned weights are also directly interpretable, recovering gyroscope calibration, imperfect qubit-gate rotations and protein-backbone geometry, though the demonstration is confined to synthetic group tasks rather than language modelling itself.
### Turning Multi-Agent Communication into Token-Level Credit with On Policy Self Distillation
arXiv: 2611.16206v1 | cs.AI
OPSD4MAS converts future multi-agent communication into fine-grained token-level credit: an external model extracts facts from trajectories and assesses their relations for coarse filtering, then a fine-grained leave-one-out filtering estimates each target token's influence against agent-wise outcome advantages, and the resulting fact-conditioned policy signal is combined with GRPO through a linearly annealed joint objective. On mathematical multi-agent tasks the authors report an improvement in pass@4 over GRPO of up to 20.00 percentage points, with ablations supporting the need for complete filtering and frozen-history injection diagnostics confirming fact utility. The caveat is that the credit signal depends on an external model's fact extraction and relation judgement, so its quality and cost scale with trajectory length. Code: https://github.com/Camel-Prince/OPSD4MAS

### Privileged Information Does Not Guarantee Privileged Guidance: Auditing On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Models
arXiv: 2611.16064v1 | cs.CL
This audit decomposes on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) — training a student on next-token targets from a teacher that additionally sees a privileged worked solution — into four stages (Source, Encoding, Transmission, Expression) across three Qwen3 scales on mathematics. The authors find that although the teacher with the solution solves hard problems and recovers from student errors, it does not reliably recognize its own errors and its token targets do not favour correct trajectories; repeated training makes successes within the student's reach more frequent rather than unlocking new ones, and learned corrections are not reliably expressed in free generation. Varying solution availability separately from teacher/student thinking modes shows the largest gains occur when a thinking-mode teacher supervises a non-thinking student, and problem-only teachers often match or exceed privileged-solution teachers — a pattern the authors call "reasoning arbitrage". The key implication is that accuracy gains alone do not demonstrate transfer of privileged reasoning; the caveat is that the decomposition is only demonstrated on math with Qwen3-scale models, so generality to other domains is unproven.

### StateAlign: Internalizing State-Aware Decision-Making for Reliable Multi-Turn Tool Agents via Reinforcement Learning
arXiv: 2611.16304v1 | cs.LG
StateAlign tackles the observation that stronger tool-use RL can raise tool hallucination: agents sometimes execute when they should ask for information, retrieve it, or decline an infeasible request. The method combines explicit task-state construction (available information, tool capabilities, task progress) with state-contrastive RL, where token-level contrasts compare the same generated reasoning and actions under paired states with different action requirements, while outcome rewards still drive task completion. Across five base models from three families the authors report gains on BFCL v4 multi-turn, ToolSandbox and When2Call; on Qwen3-8B, BFCL v4 multi-turn accuracy rises from 45.5% to 55.2% and no-tool hallucination on SimpleToolHalluBench drops from 62.2% (outcome-only training) to 56.1%. The caveat is that the token-level contrast objective depends on constructing matched state pairs, which is expensive and not obviously available for arbitrary tool environments.

### Detection Is Not Early Warning: Evaluating Token-Level Alarms on Language Model Failures
arXiv: 2611.16803v1 | cs.LG
PREWARN introduces an evaluation protocol for any failure mode with an annotated onset: each monitor's threshold is calibrated on validation data to an application-chosen error rate, then the monitor's alarms are scored relative to the onset, so a monitor that only flags a failure after it has begun is separated from one that warns in advance. Applied to repetitive degeneration in three open models, the authors show response-level metrics (e.g. AUROC) saturate — a length-only rule scores almost perfectly — and that probes which AUROC ranks as equal warn very differently under PREWARN; probes warn before onset on most degenerate responses of two of the three models, while low-rank adapters below the probe more than double the share of pre-onset tokens flagged, largely by firing long before the onset, and no untrained baseline warns much more often than random alarms. The caveat is that the case study covers only degeneration in three open models, so it is unclear how the protocol transfers to other failure types. Code: https://github.com/swiss-ai/degeneration-probe, released probes: https://huggingface.co/collections/marcodene/prewarn-degeneration-probes

### The Value of Thinking: Learning When to Reason in Hybrid Decision Models
arXiv: 2611.16961v1 | cs.AI
Instead of shortening or skipping explicit reasoning, this paper introduces "zero-or-T thinking": a single frozen backbone either answers from implicit computation in one forward pass without emitting tokens, or performs full autoregressive reasoning costing T tokens. A lightweight self-routing block estimates the value of thinking — the expected utility of the slow mode over the fast one — from what the implicit thinking has already produced, and the slow mode runs only where that value exceeds a per-request price λ; for episodic agentic tasks without per-decision labels, the authors add Calibrated Decision Policy Optimisation (CDPO), an RL recipe trained from episode-level returns. On JevBench, HDM-4B scores 0.857 versus Jev's 0.851 (+0.7%) at a median 0.179 s against 0.652 s (−72.5%), and on MiniWoB HDM-27B cuts token cost by 80.1%. The caveat is that the routing block must learn a value estimate, and the gains reported are on relatively small models (4B, 27B), so scaling behaviour is untested.
### Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards
arXiv: 2611.17287v1 | cs.LG
The paper formalizes one-sided verifiability (OSV): cheap checkers can reliably falsify a solution but cannot certify positives, so verified positives are merely non-falsified candidates and standard RLVR can directly reward shortcuts that pass partial checks. Negative-Only Policy Optimization (NOPO) treats verified positives as unlabeled and updates the policy only by suppressing trusted verified negatives, combining a reference-relative negative-suppression loss with a group-level adapter that scales each update by the proportion of non-falsified responses. Across unit-test, constraint-check and LLM self-verifiers, NOPO improves pass@k over baselines on code, logic and math benchmarks when oracle rewards are unavailable, and the gains persist under inference-time scaling and cross-benchmark transfer. The caveat is that discarding positive signal wastes verified-correct samples, so the method trades sample efficiency for robustness in this regime — and the reported gains are relative rather than tied to absolute pass@k numbers in the abstract.

### Sable: A Small-Field Sumcheck Argument for Bit-Exact Inference of Large Language Models
arXiv: 2611.14083 | cs.LG
Sable is an argument system and single-GPU prover for the statement that a committed, integer-quantized transformer produced a given token sequence from a given prompt, bit for bit: it fixes an integer function with no floating-point step, batches zerochecks and LogUp-GKR lookups into one sumcheck per domain, and commits to all witness tensors and weights in a single multilinear commitment (Reed-Solomon codes, BLAKE3 Merkle trees, mixed-length BaseFold opening), streaming the model layer by layer on one GPU. On an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell it proves all 64 layers of Qwen2.5-32B in 92.5 s for 4,096 tokens and 398 s for 16,384 tokens, and covers Qwen3-32B and the MoE Qwen3-30B-A3B (121.8 s and 84.7 s at 4,096 tokens), at a proof cost of 41–50× serving time (6.4 cents per 4,096-token request at public GPU prices) and 40–64× less time per parameter and token than zkLLM. The authors also report that the integer model matches BF16 greedy tokens at 86.3% of positions on WikiText-2 and stays within 1.6 points on MMLU and 1.7 points on GSM8K for Qwen3, with a compiled-core verifier checking a proof in 19–56 s on one CPU core; the caveat is that the proof is not zero-knowledge (hence open-weight models only) and the 13.7% token mismatch rate shows the integer model is not behaviourally identical to BF16.

### Understanding Mixture-of-Experts Language Models Beyond Expert Boundaries
arXiv: 2611.13975 | cs.CL
Using mixed-domain calibration data and a new Routing-Aware Magnitude Profiling (RAMP) measure, the authors shift the debate over MoE expert specialization from router-level to neuron-level analysis, finding domain-specialized neuron sets with 58–74% overlap across multi-source samples of the same domain versus only 15–24% across domains. Tracing neuron importance through pre-training and post-training across three fully open MoE families shows that neuron importance keeps reorganizing between pre-training checkpoints even though expert-level rankings are comparatively stable, while post-training largely preserves neuron rankings. Building on this, RAMP-based Pruning (RAMPr) selects neurons across experts for balanced multi-domain capability retention: at 75% neuron retention it preserves 91–98% of original average performance across six models, and at 50% retention it cuts resident GPU memory by 36–43% while improving batched prefill throughput by 17–39%. The caveat is that the analysis covers only three fully open MoE families, so conclusions about closed or denser expert layouts are not established.

### Free Lunch Uncertainty: Sequence-Level Rényi Entropy from Particle Decoding for Adaptive LLM Reasoning
arXiv: 2611.13414 | cs.CL
The paper shows that Sequential Monte Carlo decoding for power sampling already estimates the normalizing constant of the power distribution, which yields sequence-level Rényi entropy (SRE) over the whole completion distribution at zero marginal cost, with a guarantee at every particle budget; a Rényi chain rule extends the readout to stepwise entropies of any order, and the authors prove that orders above one are tied to the most likely token at each step (explaining why they track log-probability), while the order-1/2 member accumulates the log of per-step search effort. That member is the best zero-cost selective-prediction signal in 10 of 13 settings, with MATH500 AUROC 0.022–0.044 above log-probability for all four models and within length quartiles, and a two-pass policy matches 64-particle accuracy on MATH500 with 34.2% fewer particles over three seeds. The caveat is that the signal depends on running multiple particles, so it trades a single-sample pipeline for particle-level compute even though the marginal cost is zero.

### Can KV Cache Be Reused Across Model Updates? A DeepSeek-V4-Flash Case Study
arXiv: 2611.14051 | cs.LG
The authors study direct KV-cache reuse across four model updates, with a detailed case of DeepSeek-V4-Flash to Flash-0731, and find that caches can retain useful task information without faithfully reproducing the updated model's behaviour: retrieval accuracy varies from near-native performance to large losses across updates, though with source caches already available, reuse gives median time-to-first-token speedups of 2.10–5.08× on three longer-prefix QA benchmarks. They introduce Functional Cache Divergence, comparing next-token distributions under reused versus native caches, finding direct reuse preserves native behaviour better on natural-text continuation while compatibility degrades with increasing prefix length in single-token retrieval tasks, and that agreement on the first generated token does not imply agreement in answer content or stopping behaviour. The caveat is that the study is centered on one model family and one upgrade path, so the conclusion that task utility and behavioural fidelity are distinct criteria may not generalize across architectures.

## Cross-paper themes
- **Serving and inference-efficiency work dominates (6/15)**: LazyWeights (edge decoding units), SignRot (FP4/outlier-aware rotation), KVPath (inter-layer KV deltas), Olifant (memory-based speculative decoding), the KV-cache-reuse study (2.10–5.08× TTFT) and Free Lunch Uncertainty (particle-decoding entropy readout) all attack the cost of running models rather than their accuracy, and most report hardware- or throughput-level numbers rather than benchmark deltas.
- **Quantization is attacked from two directions (2/15)**: SignRot focuses on activation outliers and rotation math for W4A4, while LazyWeights assumes quantization is already done and instead exploits the non-uniformity of the resulting integer codes via lossless Huffman coding — both report perplexity-preserving gains over llama.cpp/SpinQuant-style baselines at 4-bit granularity.
- **Measurement skepticism and evaluation rigor recur (4/15)**: the OPSD audit finds "reasoning arbitrage" (privileged solutions add little beyond a thinking-mode teacher), PREWARN shows response-level AUROC saturates and hides when a monitor fires, the KV-cache paper separates "task utility" from "native-behaviour fidelity", and Free Lunch Uncertainty shows most zero-cost uncertainty proxies are dominated by one Rényi order.
- **Multi-agent and tool-use RL is the dominant application of RLVR (3/15)**: OPSD4MAS (token-level credit from inter-agent communication), StateAlign (state-contrastive RL to reduce tool hallucination) and NOPO (one-sided verifiability for RLVR) all rework the reward/credit plumbing rather than the base optimizer.
- **Architecture-level theory work is present but narrow (2/15)**: the Symmetry in Time representation-theoretic account of diagonal SSMs (with HyperSSM's quaternion channels) and the KVPath path-formulation both derive structural necessities from how information must be carried across depth or time, and both validate mainly on synthetic state-tracking tasks rather than language modelling.
- **Interpretability work is moving from probes to timing (2/15)**: PREWARN reframes failure detection as a temporal-alarm problem with calibrated thresholds, and the MoE study shifts specialization analysis from router-level expert choice to neuron-level magnitude profiles (58–74% intra-domain vs 15–24% inter-domain overlap) — both worry about what a metric measures, not just its value.
- **Selective prediction and adaptive compute recur as an application thread (3/15)**: Free Lunch Uncertainty (zero-cost sequence-level entropy), the hybrid decision model's zero-or-T thinking (80.1% token-cost cut on MiniWoB) and NOPO's use of non-falsified positives all propose mechanisms for deciding when to spend more compute or accept less certainty.
