## Reasoning, Chain-of-Thought & Test-Time Compute

### 2611.08403 — StepBack: Step-Level Error-Localized Resampling for Efficient Test-Time Reasoning
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
Self-consistency decoding improves chain-of-thought accuracy but pays for many complete trajectories. StepBack instead starts from a small set of draft trajectories, localizes the likely *first* error position using lightweight signals, and resamples only the detected error step and its suffix before majority voting over answers. The authors report that StepBack "matches the accuracy of SC on average across seven mathematical reasoning and multi-hop QA benchmarks, while reducing token usage by up to 41.26%". A second, structural property: because resampling is local rather than whole-trajectory, StepBack composes with adaptive inference schemes such as Adaptive Consistency, further improving their inference-time efficiency. Comparison: the gain is efficiency at equal accuracy rather than an accuracy gain over SC — the paper's claim is parity plus a token saving, not a better solver. Null result implied: efficient variants that only truncate early or spread a fixed sample budget across questions do not localize the error, which is where StepBack's mechanism differs.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08403

### 2611.07854 — Towards Foundation Decision Models: Drex
cs.CL (New submissions)
Autoregressive long-CoT models give higher accuracy but produce inconsistent answers, require output parsing, and add cost and latency. Drex reframes decision-making as a single non-autoregressive pass: given a state (document, record, or agent context, optionally with images) and multiple-option questions, the model returns a probability distribution over options without decoding. The family covers four open models: 8B (DLM), 9B (v1.5), 35B with 3B active (v2.0), and 27B (Max), trained with SFT plus reinforcement learning from adjusted feedback (RLAF), with vision tuning for Max; ablations show RLAF improves accuracy and consistency losses improve robustness to option order. On Decision Index 0.4.1, Max sets a new state of the art among compared decision models (67.52 on the public suite vs. 60.70 for Jev 1.14.0); on Decision Bench v4 it is "statistically tied with d3-turbo and GPT-6 Sol and trails Claude Opus 5.5 by 1.6 points", and has the highest accuracy among decision models on DecideBench. The latency claim is the headline: Max answers a typical request in 183 ms "against about 2.0 s for GPT-6 Sol and 3.5 s for Claude Opus 5.5", with stable outputs under option reordering. Authors report these numbers; vendor model versions are referenced but not independently verified.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07854

### 2611.08277 — LeanContext: Disentangling Mathematical Resources from Local Formal Context in Lean Whole-Proof Generation
cs.AI (New submissions)
Neural theorem proving increasingly retrieves premises and project context, but evaluating those resources requires measuring the advantage of target-selected declarations over comparable alternatives — and how that advantage depends on training. LeanContext is a benchmark of 129,826 Lean whole-proof generation problems, splitting selected complete preceding mathematical declarations (Mathematical Context, MC) from remaining local source commands (Environment Context, EC), with four aligned input views for crossed training/inference evaluation under original Lean verification environments. The core finding: for both diffusion- and autoregressive-based models, selected declarations outperform comparable alternatives by a larger margin when training includes MC — i.e., the training distribution, not just the retrieval quality, determines whether context helps. Final-input audits identify omitted declarations referenced by the target, and prioritizing these dependencies improves Pass@32 by 1.40% and 1.70% for LLaDA and Llama respectively on 2,000 targets. Comparison: selection is done without access to target reference proofs, with checkpoints, EC, and inference budgets held constant, so the gains are attributed to selection rather than budget.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08277

## Reinforcement Learning Post-Training

### 2611.07639 — Measuring the Marginal Value of Verifier-Based RL Beyond Supervised Fine-Tuning
cs.LG (Cross submissions)
RLVR is usually evaluated as an alternative to SFT, implicitly assuming the RL stage contributes gains of its own. This paper measures that contribution directly on a deterministic benchmark of 1,877 synthetic GPU/LLM training-failure diagnosis tasks across 30 archetypes, graded by a programmatic verifier with partial credit, calibrated so a mid-tier model lands in a 0.60–0.75 band. The pilot was a surprise: 1,200 gold demonstrations nearly solved it (SFT 0.785 on the hard split) while 15 GRPO steps from the base model did not move it (0.314 vs 0.311 base). The pre-registered full run (60 GRPO steps, lr 1e-6) reports "no detectable improvement in-distribution (+0.22 points, 95% CI [-0.06, +0.51])" and only "a modest but measurable improvement under distribution shift (+0.33 points on a held-out OOD split, CI [+0.02, +0.68])". Null result: SFT accounted for most of the available improvement; the residual errors SFT leaves are "mostly systematic, identical across samples", which likely reduces GRPO's within-group learning signal. A full 60-step cold-start GRPO arm confirms the pilot. Total compute cost: $8.69, fully reproducible from archived generations.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07639

### 2611.07482 — SAVeR²: Reasoning-based Safety Alignment for Large Reasoning Models via Verifiable Rewards
cs.AI (New submissions)
Large Reasoning Models improve capability through explicit CoT but introduce safety risks: non-malicious reasoning and answers, safety consistency, and explicit intent identification. The authors argue existing alignment suffers from "poor adversarial generalization, a significant 'safety tax' on reasoning, and unfaithful coarse-grained rewards", largely because they neglect the LRM's intrinsic reasoning ability for safety itself. SAVeR² decomposes the safety objective into four fine-grained verifiable reward signals and uses a difficulty-aware data selection strategy to stabilize training; its distinctive mechanism is a reasoning-preserving gradient projection that analytically resolves conflicts between safety and reasoning gradients, which the authors claim "effectively eliminat[es] the safety tax". Experiments span models from 1.5B to 671B and report "superior safety performance and significantly lower over-refusal rates across multiple benchmarks without compromising general reasoning capabilities". Caveat: the headline safety-tax elimination is a self-reported claim; no absolute benchmark deltas are given in the abstract, so the magnitude of the effect cannot be checked from the abstract alone.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07482

### 2611.06984 — Resource-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Post-Training through Elastic Expert Parallelism
cs.AI (New submissions)
In MoE models, RL post-training response generation is bottlenecked by stragglers: workers that finish their assigned responses stay occupied because their experts are still needed by workers processing longer responses. The authors measure that "approximately one-third of occupied worker time in the baseline occurs after local response generation finishes". FlexFloor releases workers earlier during synchronous response generation while preserving expert computation required by ongoing responses — jointly adapting response scheduling and the active worker group, using historical response lengths and memory constraints to guide batching and prompt placement, then progressively releasing workers while maintaining expert availability and correct communication. Against the verl baseline, FlexFloor generates "approximately 65.40% more response tokens per unit of occupied computing time" and "increases response generation throughput by 52.74%", with "little observed variation in rollout reward". Null-result caveat: reward distribution is unchanged, so this is a pure systems-efficiency win — the paper reports no accuracy change in either direction.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06984

### 2611.06956 — Octopus: One Brain, Many Branches for Latent-KV Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
Multi-agent LLM systems spawn sub-agents with isolated contexts, but existing frameworks duplicate prefix KV caches across branches, exchange findings by re-prefilling text summaries, and flatten cross-branch dependencies during RL. Octopus is a single-model multi-agent RL framework where all threads share one backbone and coordinate over a GPU-resident DAG of KV states via five primitives (fork, read_thread, wait_threads, rewind, commit). Three co-designed mechanisms: Zero-Copy Radix Forking (branches as O(1) cursor views over shared physical KV pages); Attention-Only KV Mounting, which mounts a sibling thread's terminal KV blocks into full-attention layers while bypassing linear-recurrent states (Gated DeltaNet / Mamba-2), cutting off-task hijacking by 21× (57.9% → 2.7%) while retaining 92.2% of full-text mutual information at zero extra KV allocation; and a Packed DAG-GRPO Trainer that packs all episode branches into one block-sparse attention pass and assigns counterfactual branch credit via leave-one-branch-out marginal rewards. Trained end-to-end with GRPO on Qwen3.5-4B over a sparse BM25 corpus, Octopus reaches 49.6% on BrowseComp-Plus (+17.5 points over single-agent GRPO at 143K context, +12.5 over text-summary multi-agent RL) and 51.2% on OpenResearcher, "outperforming dense-retrieval agents up to 250× larger while increasing training throughput 3.3× and cutting peak KV memory 2.8×". All figures are author-reported on a single 4B backbone.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06956

### 2611.08219 — Agent-R²: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis
cs.CL (New submissions)
Current agents fail at realistic productivity work because scalable post-training data reflecting real work intent, authentic file environments, and deliverable-level evaluation is missing. Agent-R² grounds synthesis throughout the pipeline in real occupational seeds, corpus-derived workflow evidence, and authentic web-sourced files — deliberately avoiding manually specified workflows or LLM-generated files. From 10k web-corpus candidates it distills 4k task instances pairing real multi-format attachments with fine-grained, deliverable-level rubrics; Agent-R²-4B is trained with SFT and rubric-based RL. Headline comparison: trained on only 4k instances, Agent-R²-4B "surpasses Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 on both GDPval (51.0% vs. 48.0%) and OfficeQA (14.8% vs. 13.8%)" — a 60× parameter gap closed by 4k real-sourced instances. The paper also identifies shortcut behavior (superficially plausible deliverables produced before grounding, constructing, or verifying) and reports the method "mitigates shortcut behavior ... while promoting sustained exploration". Self-reported; rubrics and dataset construction are author-defined.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219

## Efficiency: KV Cache, Quantization & Attention

### 2611.07605 — The Gating Mechanism of KV Cache Eviction
cs.CL (New submissions)
A negative/structural result about learnable KV eviction. The setup: give each cached entry a learnable gate on its attention weight and train it on the model's own objective. The gate sits inside the normalizing softmax, so suppressing one entry hands its mass to the others, and the same normalizer absorbs any per-query constant — "a constant gate cancels and reproduces the frozen model exactly". Empirically, trained on the language-modeling loss, "the gate stays near a constant score while its loss falls throughout: optimization succeeds, but a frozen backbone was fit for dense attention, so its loss prefers the no-op, and a near-constant score ranks nothing." A second obstacle survives: the same gradient charges the gate for errors the frozen model already makes, which no reweighting of positions separates out. The authors argue much of the machinery in learnable eviction (sparsity penalties, relaxations of the discrete decision) works around the first obstacle rather than removing it. Fix: put dense attention beyond the gate's reach and make the dense model's predictions its target; under these two conditions capacity is never the missing ingredient — "neither a larger scorer, nor explicit access to the past, nor a trainable backbone helps". Discarded mass is better recovered by parameter-free centroids read inside the same softmax than by any memory read outside it. Result: at 75% KV compression the gate reaches 90.14 on RULER at 8K and 46.03 on LongBench with Qwen3-8B, 3.72 and 1.36 points below full-cache inference.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07605

### 2611.07025 — LowRAM: Low-Rank Keys and Amortised Values for Composable KV Cache Compression at Test-Time
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
Existing KV compression exploits redundancy across tokens, layers, and feature dimensions, or lowers precision. LowRAM identifies a complementary axis: within each attention head, values are largely predictable from their corresponding keys. The method fits a lightweight per-head predictor at test time and stores residuals only for the tokens it reconstructs worst, giving 4× near-lossless value compression; the predictor can be meta-learning-initialised for substantially reduced prefill overhead, or fitted from scratch where a one-off cost is acceptable (e.g. multi-query inference). Pairing value amortisation with cross-layer low-rank key compression yields an asymmetric pipeline that "stays close to lossless across three model families". By reconstructing only a selected subset of KV pairs per decoding step, overhead stays minimal and the paper reports "up to 5× higher peak decode throughput at 4× compression". Composed with quantisation and eviction it reaches "26.9× compression at 94.5% of baseline performance on long contexts". Mechanism claim: treating keys and values as structurally distinct enables more aggressive compression than any single-axis method. Author-reported.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07025

### 2611.07217 — TileQuant: Tile as the Precision-Allocation Unit for LLM Mixed-Precision Post-Training Quantization
cs.LG (New submissions)
Post-training quantization preserves quality only if uneven weight sensitivity is accounted for. Uniform PTQ uses one bit-width; importance-aware PTQ adjusts scales or reconstructs weights within that width; column-wise mixed precision retains selected columns at higher precision — but sensitivity varies *within* columns, so whole-column retention also protects less sensitive weights, while runtime outlier detection adds decoding overhead. TileQuant is training-free and assigns precision to hardware-aligned 2D weight tiles: offline calibration scores tiles either by input-activation-aware forward statistics (cheaper) or gradient-informed loss sensitivity (stronger), then within each projection's retention budget the highest-scoring tiles stay at 16-bit and the rest use 8-bit. The resulting map guides reconstruction into compact weight buffers that the kernel reuses for native integer and 16-bit computation with local accumulation to reduce memory traffic. Headline number: "TileQuant reaches 2.66x the original model's generation throughput", and across language modeling, reasoning, long-context retrieval and QA it "preserves quality and surpasses quantized and original models on multiple tasks" — an unusual claim of beating the FP16 baseline, plausibly from noise-tolerant evaluation rather than genuine improvement.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07217

### 2611.07256 — TriAttention: Unified Sparse Mixed-Precision Attention for Long-Context LLM Inference
cs.LG (New submissions)
Sparsity and quantization both cut attention cost, but useful interactions differ in precision requirements: some tolerate low-precision scores while remaining sensitive to removal. TriAttention assigns query-key (QK) tiles to skipping, 4-bit, or 8-bit computation, using 4-bit to retain quantization-tolerant interactions cheaply. Compactness: each head's computation regions are encoded with two diagonal-distance boundaries, keeping policy storage independent of context length. Offline calibration evaluates candidate boundary pairs via normalized projected-output errors and complete-tile execution costs; a model-wide allocator then picks each head's boundaries to minimize calibration error under a shared computation budget. During prefill the boundaries generate precision-grouped traversals for a fused kernel that skips omitted tiles and combines both QK precisions into one normalized attention output; the prefill-quantized keys and values are reused as a compressed KV cache for decoding, reducing repeated prompt reads. Results: "end-to-end generation speedups of up to 7.88x over the Torch attention and 2.65x over the BF16 baseline", while maintaining quality comparable to the original models on general-purpose and long-context tasks. The mechanism of interest is the joint sparsity/precision decision, not either alone.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07256

### 2611.07542 — FlashOffloading: Low-Latency KV Cache Offloading for Long-Context LLM Serving
cs.AI (New submissions)
In LLM serving the KV cache grows linearly with sequence length and concurrency, so long-context inference hits the HBM wall and concurrency collapses. Offloading to host DRAM frees HBM but inflates latency with cache-management and host-to-device (H2D) transfer overhead — and higher concurrency then fails to translate into throughput. FlashOffloading is a host-DRAM-centric architecture that fully offloads the KV cache in both prefill and decode: rolling HBM cache for prefill, a hot cache for decode, a reverse sparse index with zero extra miss-lookup overhead, and a compute–load overlapped kernel, tuned for a time-per-output-token (TPOT) service-level objective. Experiments deploy GLM-5.2 with multi-token prediction (MTP3) speculative decoding on an Ascend910C prefill–decode disaggregated platform, reporting "prefill throughput by up to 1.38× and decode throughput by up to 1.91× under a 30 ms TPOT constraint". The framing that matters: the win is measured under an explicit latency SLO, not raw throughput; without the TPOT constraint the offloading advantage would not materialize. Integrated into an open-source serving framework.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07542

### 2611.07496 — ZYFA: Speed and Accuracy of FP8 FlashAttention on AMD CDNA4
cs.LG (Cross submissions)
Attention dominates compute-bound prefill at long context, and FP8 doubles peak matrix throughput — but only accelerates matrix multiplication, while the softmax between the two matmuls runs on vector units FP8 does not touch. On AMD Instinct MI355X, vendor AITER kernels "reach 39% of peak FP8 matrix throughput at 32K tokens, compared with 49% in BF16". Attention is also more numerically sensitive to quantization than feed-forward layers, and unlike NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell the MI355X does not track matrix-instruction completion in hardware, so softmax/matmul overlap depends on instruction order. ZYFA's three techniques: compute each FP8 probability directly from its score with a multiply-add and integer conversion instead of an exponential; a ping-pong schedule where one thread group computes softmax while another runs matmuls; and scale each block of probabilities up by a power of two before rounding, normalizing by the sum of the rounded probabilities. On real activations from three models, "ZYFA achieves 1.16-1.76x the throughput of AITER"; on Gaussian benchmark inputs at 32K tokens, 1.26-1.37x at head dim 128 and 1.99x for DeepSeek-style multi-head latent attention. Numerically, at head dim 128 its error is within a few percent of Composable Kernel and Triton and "62-78% lower than that of AITER's". Important caveat the authors flag: MI355X sustained clock depends on operand values, "so throughput on random inputs does not reliably predict throughput on real model activations".

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07496

### 2611.07552 — SPARE: Fast Linear Attention Decoding with Compact Recurrent States
cs.CL (New submissions)
Linear attention avoids softmax attention's quadratic cost by keeping fixed-size recurrent states, but those states scale quadratically with representation dimension and require repeated data transfers during decoding — a GPU memory bottleneck. Recent work has revealed low-rank redundancy in recurrent states. SPARE (State Projection for Accelerated Recurrent Execution) is training-free and exploits that redundancy via fixed low-dimensional subspaces, heterogeneous rank allocation, and lightweight write compensation; recurrent reads and updates run directly on compact states, with no online decomposition or dense-state reconstruction. Reported numbers: "2.55x full-model decoding speedup, 7.49x lower recurrent-kernel DRAM traffic, and 3.31x lower peak GPU memory", while "preserving better associative-retrieval accuracy than structured pruning at matched memory budgets" — the comparison that matters, since pruning is the obvious competing way to shrink the state. Author-reported benchmark results.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07552

### 2611.08376 — Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout
cs.LG (New submissions)
Gated delta-rule models decay the recurrent state and erase its prediction for an incoming key before writing a new value; Gated DeltaNet-2 generalizes this with channel-wise decay and decoupled full-rank erase/write gates. Muir's analysis finds a surprising asymmetry: "state updates are far less sensitive to erasure than to writes" — key activation and unit normalization concentrate key energy onto a sparse subset of channels, whereas value writes scale all channels, so the erase gate can be parameterized at lower rank. A second observation: slow-decaying state rows accumulate much larger norms than fast rows, dominating unweighted readout and forcing stronger write-time erasure to suppress interference. Muir accordingly fuses all gates into a sensitivity-allocated low-rank projection, re-weights state rows with a matched-filter readout gate, and reinvests the saved parameters in a wider feed-forward network; because standard chunkwise algorithms become numerically unstable at the large chunk sizes needed to saturate wide matrix units, a parallel update is designed for stable, efficient training. Across 166M–1.34B parameters, Muir "lowers perplexity by 1.8-2.0% over GDN-2 and 2.5-3.6% over Transformer", raises commonsense accuracy by up to 1.9 points, and improves extractive recall over GDN-2. At 1.34B it outperforms both baselines on multi-hop and multi-needle recall, "extrapolates 2,560x from its 4K training length to 10M-token streams without perplexity drift", and "maintains near-100% passkey retrieval to 1M tokens with static readout". Throughput matches GDN-2, its 100B-token validation loss is reached 1.2x faster, and it trains 2.7x faster than Transformer at 32K.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376

### 2611.08290 — AutoCompress: Automated Hardware-Guided Model Compression
cs.LG (New submissions)
LLM pruning workflows typically target parameter counts, forcing practitioners to hand-pick sizes that meet latency, throughput and memory limits — but "parameter count alone does not predict these metrics, which depend on the model architecture, workload, hardware, and serving engine". AutoCompress folds those constraints directly into differentiable architecture search, jointly optimizing global and per-layer structure to produce heterogeneous architectures, guided by serving-engine cost models validated against end-to-end latency, throughput and memory measurements. Evaluated on long-context local coding, batched datacenter serving, and latency-sensitive in-game interaction: against the state-of-the-art Minitron pruning framework, AutoCompress improves average accuracy across eight benchmarks by 6.57, 2.35 and 0.57 percentage points on RTX 5070, RTX 5080 and H100 respectively — a strongly hardware-dependent effect, largest on the weakest consumer GPU and near-negligible on H100. It also compresses Nemotron 3.5 Super from 120B to 74B parameters for single-B200 BF16 serving at 128K context, "retaining 95% of its mean benchmark score". The transferable lesson is that compression search should be cost-model-driven, not parameter-driven.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08290

### 2611.06976 — On the Opportunities and Challenges of Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-Model Transfer
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
Prefill-to-Autoregressive Cross-model Transfer (PACT) combines a larger model's prompt representations with a smaller model's efficient generation, promising capability/cost balance — but existing systems lack support for independently evaluating large-small collaboration, and better task performance alone does not show the receiver actually used the sender's information. PLatour is an open-source PACT framework connecting frozen models through lightweight, zero-initialized linear bridges trained directly with task rewards, supporting hybrid-attention architectures; it "significantly outperforms open-source baselines on generation tasks and surpasses Mostik's reported results on shared mathematics and coding benchmarks using a substantially smaller sender". The critical result is the controlled ablation: gains on some tasks "can be reproduced by uninformative sender signals, self-communication, or receiver-only training", and the authors find "trainable bridges can act as parameter-efficient adaptation mechanisms without exploiting useful sender information". The paper's contribution is the negative one — PACT-Check, proposed to quantify how much of a trained system's gain genuinely comes from communication. Caveat: the "surpasses Mostik" comparison depends on an external paper's reported numbers.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06976

## Evaluation, Benchmarks & Contamination

### 2611.08417 — Benchmarks as Software: Terminal-Bench and the Case for Continuous Benchmarks
cs.AI (New submissions)
Coding agents are among the most economically consequential LLM deployments, and even carefully validated benchmarks go stale: models "discover valid solutions that existing verifiers reject", and infrastructure changes invalidate assumptions embedded in tasks. The paper names this accumulation "benchmark maintenance debt: a growing gap between benchmark scores and the capabilities they are intended to measure", and proposes a continuous-validation workflow combining community reports, task artifacts, agent traces, automated oracle checks, and human-gated repairs. Applied to Terminal-Bench 2.0 (itself heavily reviewed and red-teamed before release), the audit "identifies issues in roughly 30% of the benchmark", released as Terminal-Bench 2.1. Repairs change results materially: "accuracy increased by over 10 percentage points and leaderboard positions move by up to three ranks", while overall ranking stays highly correlated (Spearman rho = 0.958). The sharper finding is about resource budgets: "fixed wall-clock can materially change agent performance" — extending the time limit closes a 20pp harness gap on GPT 5.4 and shrinks a 30 pp gap on GPT 5.4 mini to 5 pp. Conclusion: "resource budgets are part of the measurement specification and must be validated alongside the tasks themselves". Caveat: the accuracy jump partly reflects fixed verifiers, not improved models.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08417

### 2611.07042 — Follow the Policy, Fail the Benchmark: Auditing and Revising Policy-Based Decision Benchmarks
cs.CL (New submissions)
Policy-based decision benchmarks test whether systems apply written rules to financial and operational cases, but a rule-following response can be marked wrong when the benchmark misstates the required decision. This paper audits nine public policy-based decision benchmarks via source tracing, program execution, and controlled checks, finding unsupported targets, reference errors that survive replay or propagate as solved steps, and evaluators that reject acceptable answers or credit incomplete decisions. The scoring impact is measured by rescoring unchanged model responses: "On the 96 LegalBench SARA numeric cases, correcting the scorer raises acceptance by more than 80 percentage points for each of three models, without changing references or responses." In RuleArena Tax, restoring a required zero floor changes 27 of 300 references and narrows an observed model gap. A controlled federal-exemption task with explicit ownership and eligibility assumptions shows errors in supplied intermediate answers can affect final decisions. The authors release BAR (Benchmark Audit and Revision), a versioned collection of audit evidence and revisions for reevaluation. Methodological point: a large share of apparent model failure on policy benchmarks was benchmark defect.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07042

### 2611.07962 — TCSExam: Evaluating Frontier LLMs on the Full Range of TCS Research
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
TCSExam is a unified framework and benchmark covering the full range of theoretical computer science research, evaluating theorem proving, solver engineering, and constructions alongside direct attempts to resolve open conjectures — capturing concrete accomplishments and progress toward long-standing goals. TCSExam-Full contains 100 expert-reviewed tasks with submissions checked by the Lean kernel or an executable task checker; TCSExam-Lite selects 20 tasks (five per track), "reducing the number of task attempts by 80% at the same per-attempt budget". Eight agents were evaluated on Full and nine on Lite. The TCSExam Index combines all four tracks: GPT-6 Astra leads Full (71.9) and Opus 5.5 leads Lite (72.3). The validation result: "Full and Lite rankings are strongly aligned across eight shared agents (Spearman's rho = 0.93)", supporting Lite as a compact, discriminative benchmark. Beyond the leaderboard, the evaluations "yield improved solver outputs and mathematical constructions, together with independently verified special cases and bounds from open-conjecture attempts" — i.e. partial mathematical progress was machine-verified. The framework ships task sources, Docker build recipes and evaluation tools for reproducibility.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07962

### 2611.08369 — NPCBench: A Clinical Apprenticeship Benchmark for Multimodal Care-Pathway Reasoning in Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma
cs.AI (New submissions)
Medical benchmarks typically decompose competence into isolated factual questions, single-image tasks, or static vignettes, which score individual answers but say little about integrating subspecialty knowledge with multimodal evidence over a course of care. NPCBench mirrors specialist training for nasopharyngeal carcinoma: guideline and foundational knowledge, case planning, longitudinal review, CarePath integration, and open-ended consultation. It contains 4,777 component and consultation items and a 100-patient CarePath cohort with 417 scored diagnostic and care tasks; episode-level evaluation tests whether diagnostic and care answers are jointly correct within one care pathway. The headline finding across 30 evaluated models is a composition gap: "strong performance on individual tasks does not ensure jointly correct diagnostic and care answers for the same patient", and among 21 multimodal models "strict CarePath success reaches at most 23%". Paired controls and physician calibration further probe evidence grounding, longitudinal revision and consultation judging. Domain caveat: single-disease (NPC) and single-language setting; results may not generalize to other specialties.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08369

## Agents & Multi-Agent Systems

### 2611.07689 — MCPHallu: Benchmarking Reasoning, Execution, and Memory Hallucinations in MCP Agents
cs.AI (New submissions)
Existing MCP benchmarks measure task completion and diagnose failures only after execution; MCPHallu instead builds reasoning, execution and memory failure risks into task construction. It contains 358 tasks across five capability domains, run on 36 production MCP servers in isolated containers, each designed with a structural trigger making one of four subtypes the dominant risk: Branch Collapse, Unreachable Goal, Tool Misuse, or Context Forgetting — enabling subtype-level rather than aggregate analysis. Fourteen frontier LLM agents from eight vendors were run over 5,012 trajectories. Current MCP agents "remain vulnerable to hallucination-inducing tool environments, achieving an average score of 0.642 on a [0,1] scale". Failures concentrate on Unreachable Goal tasks, where "the average score drops to 0.458 and 23.2% of runs pass" — agents keep issuing tool calls or claim success rather than recognizing infeasibility. The most transferable diagnostic: subtype rankings diverge sharply, "a model's rank shifts by 6.9 of 13 positions on average across subtypes, and Branch Collapse is uncorrelated with Unreachable Goal across models (r = -0.12)", so aggregate leaderboards hide failure structure.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07689

### 2611.07238 — How Do AI Agents Waste Your Money?
cs.CL (New submissions)
Prior work showed agents can incur unexpectedly high token costs but offered no systematic way to identify and measure token waste. This paper introduces a unified framework: record and normalize complete agent trajectories, identify removable spans with an LLM-as-a-Judge (LLMaJ), and map spans back to the original trajectory for token-level measurement. Crucially, the identified waste is validated by direct intervention — removing marked spans, resuming agent execution, and measuring changes in task outcomes and subsequent actions, rather than trusting the judge. Online pruning experiments remove "41-46% of the model's reasoning while largely preserving task performance across two distinct agent benchmarks". Three structural findings: waste varies more across tasks and agent settings than across models; reasoning and visible execution exhibit different waste patterns; and different harnesses introduce distinct inefficiencies under the same model and task. Framing: token waste has "a system-level structure that aggregate token usage alone cannot reveal", making it an actionable optimization signal for harness design rather than just model tuning.

https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07238

### 2611.07286 — How Should Agents See Code? Rethinking Code Representations for Agentic Software Engineering
cs.AI (Cross submissions)
Most coding agents access repositories through raw files, lexical search and shell commands, treating code as text. This paper treats code representation as a design variable, studying five repository-map workflows — abstract syntax trees, call graphs, module dependencies, documentation-enriched search, and a hybrid — with Claude Code Haiku 4.5 against a raw-tool baseline on LinuxFLBench (fault localization), SWE-bench Pro (repo-level codegen and repair), SWT-bench (test generation) and SecBench (security patching). ### 2611.07976 — QRAE: Query-Relevant Attention Entropy for Context Compression in DeepSearch Agents
cs.LG (New submissions)
DeepSearch agents repeatedly search and carry retrieved sources into subsequent reasoning, expanding context, token consumption and latency, "although many results do not contribute to later reasoning". QRAE is an online context-compression method using sparse evidence-assessment heads to decide which intact retrieved chunks persist across reasoning steps. It scores bounded candidate batches through paired prefills: a query-free control suppresses shared attention responses, while normalized entropy measures the breadth of the positive response within each chunk. High-scoring chunks enter a persistent evidence whitelist and stay in the reasoning context; uncertain chunks stay outside reasoning context but remain in a candidate cache for later re-evaluation — an explicit hedge against premature discarding. It "requires neither an additional generative compression pass nor modification of the attention operator". Reported: task success improves by an average of 2.35 percentage points, with end-to-end latency down 30% and reported token usage down 45% on average, and the gains "generalize to a model with nearly 100B parameters".

### 2611.07595 — Evolver: Turning Agent Exploration into Evolving Capabilities
cs.AI (Cross submissions)
Agent exploration produces valuable procedural knowledge, but much of it stays trapped in transient execution traces. Evolver is an experience-driven framework that turns exploration into persistent, reusable capabilities "without updating foundation model parameters", built around structured Gene assets with experience acquisition, retrieval and feedback-guided evolution, and it plugs into existing agent harnesses. Evaluated with Claude Code and Codex over four benchmarks (Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-bench Multilingual, SWE-rebench, GAIA) on a selected diagnostic set of 320 paired executions, Evolver improves task success from 83.13% to 87.50% (+4.38 percentage points) while reducing recorded task-execution token consumption by 24.24%. The efficiency claim is strengthened by a matched-success subset: "among 265 pairs successful under both conditions, token consumption decreases by 17.57%", showing gains even where success is preserved. Interpretation offered: persistent procedural experience substitutes for model-level training; caveat — the diagnostic set is 320 selected pairs, and selection criteria are not described in the abstract.

### 2611.07652 — Don't Prompt Your Agents. Equip Them: Skillware as a Modular Standard for Sovereign, Auditable, and Deterministic Capability Execution for Logical Systems
cs.AI (New submissions)
The paper's premise is that agentic systems are brittle because they ask models to dynamically write code, guess API payloads and memorize operational constraints through prompt engineering, "which burns massive context windows, inflates API bills, and produces brittle agents that fail unpredictably in production". Skillware is an architectural framework and capability registry that separates what an agent wants to do from how it is executed: each capability bundle is a versioned software artifact combining a typed JSON input contract, deterministic Python code, task directives, bundled datasets and automated unit tests, with in-process adapters translating bundles into native tool declarations for any model, from frontier cloud APIs to local open-weight runtimes "with zero network latency". Evaluation across 23 frontier and open-weight models and 368 benchmark conditions reports that small local models (3B-8B) equipped with Skillware "consistently outperform unequipped frontier models on complex multi-step workflows", cutting token usage by up to 84.8% and inference cost by up to 97.3% while "eliminat[ing] parameter errors entirely". Caveat: these are the authors' own numbers on their own registry and benchmark conditions, with no independent verification, and the "eliminate parameter errors" claim is absolute.

### 2611.07393 — CacheGraft: Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Latent-Space Document Enrichment
cs.LG (Cross submissions)
Transformer agents can communicate state not only as text but directly in latent space via KV caches. CacheGraft argues this cache-to-cache translation is useful beyond real-time agent communication and can serve retrieval-augmented generation: each corpus passage is encoded offline into a fused KV cache between a larger "sharer" model (4B in their experiments) and a smaller "receiver" model (0.6B), so the cache can be injected into the receiver at query time without deploying the larger model. Across five QA benchmarks and two independent locally-run judges, CacheGraft improves answer quality over the text-only receiver baseline in 29 of 30 comparisons, of which 17 of the 30 are statistically significant. The notable property: "these gains are zero-shot with respect to the fuser, which was never trained on retrieval data". The proposed use case is compute-constrained settings where only the small model can be deployed at inference time. Caveat: initial results, small models, and the paper self-describes as a "promising avenue" rather than a validated system.

## Safety, Alignment & Interpretability

### 2611.07216 — Compositional Unalignment: Auditing Safety in Large Vision-Language Models
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
LVLMs are vulnerable to compositional harm: an image and a prompt benign in isolation become harmful through cross-modal reasoning. Recent alignment methods report strong compositional-safety benchmark scores, but an LLM judge "only measures observed behavior and cannot reveal how robustly safety is integrated into the model". The paper treats un-alignment as a complementary robustness audit. Two findings: minimal supervised fine-tuning reaches an LLM-judge score comparable to a multi-stage RL pipeline, so "similar benchmark scores cannot establish robustness by themselves"; and recognition and refusal of compositional harm are decoupled — nine standard direction-finding methods detect compositional harm with up to AUC=0.99, yet ablating the recovered directions either leaves refusal intact or destroys model coherence. CMaG, a training-free un-alignment method, measures refusal intention via a refusal-versus-compliance log-probability margin, takes its gradient with respect to mid-layer activations, and contrasts it against partially benign pairs to isolate the cross-modal interaction term; a single backward pass over 80 examples yields a direction that monotonically converts refusal into compliance. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B, CMaG "reduces compositional safety scores by up to 65 points while over 98% of outputs remain coherent" and un-aligns models hardened by defensive training. The paper contains unsafe model outputs by design.

### 2611.07071 — Shaping & Monitoring Models through their Internal Representations
cs.LG (New submissions)
Mechanistic Shaping shapes models through their internal representations during training, so that models follow specifications about which concepts they *express* (measured via outputs or internal representations, e.g. no harmful content) and which concepts they *rely on* to decide (e.g. not a candidate's gender when screening a résumé). Across vision, medical imaging and language, scales up to 32B parameters, and training paradigms (classification, SFT, DPO, GRPO), it "substantially reduces targeted behaviors while preserving task performance". Because training against a representation can also modify it, the paper asks whether shaped concepts remain monitorable, and introduces a hierarchy of monitor classes that distinguishes a concept escaping one probe from a concept being truly gone. Three geometric circumvention mechanisms are characterized: drift away from the monitor, erasure from the layer, or persistence "in higher-order statistics as a nonlinear encoding", explained via a product-code construction. Empirically, "shaped concepts largely remain monitorable: refitted linear probes recover the concept in most settings, and the original probe remains reliable whenever gradients do not flow through it". Framing: interpretability as a training tool, with a correspondingly rigorous interpretability-based science demanded.

### 2611.08281 — Multimodal-SEA: Benchmarking AI Agent Security under Multimodal Social Engineering Attacks
cs.CL (New submissions)
Personal AI agents face social engineering built on forged multimodal content, but existing evaluations focus on text dialogue. Multimodal-SEA is an interactive benchmark of 608 expert-designed tasks grounded in real-world fraud cases, covering 14 domains and 152 attack scenarios with prepared multimodal content across four difficulty levels, and reflecting distinct Chinese and English social and cultural contexts. Agents inspect content, verify requests and execute actions in simulated applications, with attack outcomes grounded in verifiable tool execution logs; a multimodal attack harness integrates task information, attack progression control and multimodal content construction for controlled red teaming. Evaluating seven widely used multimodal models across six dimensions, "all are vulnerable, with attack success rates of 7.1% to 34.5%". Two findings with design implications: adding multimodal content raises attack success from 19.9% to 29.2% "despite increased proactive verification coverage", and stronger safety instructions reduce attack success "without improving recognition of deceptive multimodal content". The same section notes that lower attack success for some models comes with excessive refusal of legitimate requests — so ASR alone is a misleading safety metric.

### 2611.07640 — Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks
cs.AI (Cross submissions)
Indirect prompt-injection benchmarks predominantly plant instructions that redirect a tool-using agent toward an attacker-chosen task. This paper defines IPI by *authority* rather than instruction-like wording: planted content is an attack when it attempts to influence a decision its source is not authorized to determine. The benchmark makes that boundary explicit — in each of 90 validated cases across three tool-use environments, the user request specifies the source that resolves the targeted decision, and every attack tampers with a decision required by the benign task rather than replacing that task. Attacks with the same harmful outcome target up to six parts of decision making: objective, decision rules, available actions, expected consequences, reading of observations, or beliefs about current state. Key result: without a defense, attacks on decision rules, available actions and state have *higher* mean success than objective attacks across five LLMs — so the standard objective-only attack is the weakest probe. A detection-and-removal defense cuts objective-targeting attacks to 2.4% success but leaves observation- and state-targeting at 9.5% and 12.1%, "and this gap persists under detection-optimized rewrites"; a tool-policy defense still allows 15.4% success vs 17.0% undefended. Conclusion: "evaluations whose attacks redirect the task or target only the agent's objective can therefore overestimate the coverage of these defenses."

### 2611.07586 — Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Against Covert Misalignment
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
Chain-of-thought monitoring is a key control — reading the agent's reasoning and flagging misalignment before it becomes action — but its reliability depends on the realism of the red team. Existing red-teaming relies on shared templates (e.g. casting a side task as a prerequisite), which treat the side objective as an exogenous insertion and leave task-specific, contextually grounded evasions underexplored. TraceMRT is an automated Trace-level Monitor Red-Teaming framework that reframes the side task as "a coherent sub-goal of the primary task" and searches trace-shaping strategies whose induced trajectories complete the misaligned objective while reading as natural execution. Black-box attacks against state-of-the-art monitors such as GPT-5.2 and Claude-4.5 "reveal substantial vulnerabilities even in top models". The countermeasure is a Bayesian Trajectory Monitoring framework combining global context and local segment evidence to detect covert misalignment, reported at "0.91 AUC and 0.74 TPR@FPR=0.05". Caveat: all numbers are author-reported against black-box monitors, and no absolute baseline monitor AUC is given in the abstract for comparison.

### 2611.08226 — "Blind Compliance": Coercive Control in AI Jailbreaking
cs.CL (Cross submissions)
This is a conceptual/position paper rather than an empirical one. Jailbreak research has focused on the system's output — whether disallowed content is ultimately produced — an approach "which draws largely on classical cybersecurity" and, the authors argue, "does not address the uniquely relational dimension introduced by generative AI: in many cases the user is not just accessing a technology via an exploit, but is in conversation with it as part of the exploit". Drawing on interpersonal violence scholarship, the paper presents a framework of coercive control categories distinguishing embodied from disembodied dynamics, then maps how disembodied coercive control is "structurally embedded in modern AI training paradigms" and exploited via a suite of persona-based jailbreaks. The reframing: AI safety and alignment is not merely a technical security problem but "another surface upon which we see enacted the relational dynamics of power and control". Caveat: the framework is argued from existing scholarship and case analysis; no new quantitative evaluation is reported in the abstract, so treat it as a lens rather than an evidenced result.
