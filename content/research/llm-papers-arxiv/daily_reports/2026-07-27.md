## Notable Papers: July 24-27 Batch

### 1. J-CoT: Chain-of-Thought in J-Space
**arXiv:2607.21981** — cs.CL, cs.AI | July 24, 2026

Novel recurrent reasoning framework that replaces natural language as the CoT interface with **J-space** (vocabulary-indexed coordinate system in hidden representations). Key innovations:
- Expresses intermediate states as vocabulary-indexed coefficients (J-thoughts) at cycle boundaries
- Maps back into full hidden representation for next reasoning cycle
- Requires neither fluent intermediate rationale nor recurrence over complete hidden state
- J-CoT-Zero matches or exceeds strongest latent-reasoning baselines on every benchmark
- J-CoT-Train achieves highest scores across mathematical, scientific, coding, and structured path-reasoning tasks

**Key insight:** The bottleneck in CoT is the natural language interface itself — J-space provides a linguistically grounded but non-verbal recurrent mechanism that preserves information selection/organization while removing fluency constraints.

### 2. LeAct: Learning to Reason from Expert Actions
**arXiv:2607.21856** — cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL | July 23, 2026

Treats expert system CoT as a **latent variable** — experts commit to actions without writing reasoning, but that reasoning can be recovered. Key approach:
- Student samples candidate CoTs for each expert action, retains those measurably improving probability of recovering the action
- Reaches solver's numerical floor on small enumerable games
- 5× closer to solver than strongest expert-iteration baseline at larger scale
- At Flop Hold'em (~10^9 infosets): wins head-to-head by +60 mbb/g
- Only training recipe that improves on direct imitation on robotics probe

**Key insight:** Expert systems (game engines, theorem provers, planners) are a categorically new source of reasoning teachers — they produce near-optimal actions with verifiable quality but leave CoT as latent structure to be recovered.

### 3. MetaEvolve: Cultivating Core Meta-Skills with RL
**arXiv:2607.21971** — cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL | July 24, 2026

Identifies that test-time scaling via self-evolution (e.g., AlphaEvolve) hinges on **meta-skills** largely neglected by post-training: self-reflection with environment feedback enabling multi-round refinement. MetaEvolve framework:
- Synthesizes evolution trajectories as training data (current program, fitness score, history of attempts)
- Trains via RL with verifiable rewards from test case execution
- 10.01% absolute improvement over strongest baseline in-distribution, 24.12% OOD
- 46.9% relative improvement on open-ended algorithm optimization outside training domain

**Key insight:** Domain-agnostic meta-skills (self-reflection, environment feedback integration) transfer broadly to open-ended problems where rich training signals are scarce — explicitly cultivating them via RL is a principled path toward autonomously self-evolving AI.

### 4. Skill Self-Play: Frontier LLM via Co-Evolving Skills
**arXiv:2607.22529** — cs.CL | July 24, 2026

Identifies fundamental dilemma in self-evolution: **task diversity vs verification reliability**. Environment-bound methods get precise feedback but narrow domains; open-ended self-generation has variety but lacks verification, allowing misleading rewards. Skill Self-Play resolves this via:
- **Proposer**: generates challenging tasks conditioned on dynamically sampled skills
- **Solver**: explores candidate solutions to push capability boundaries
- **Skill Controller**: collects execution feedback to update and expand skill library
- All three co-evolve in continuous RL self-play loop

**Key insight:** Agent skills are the middle ground: each skill ensures deep, verifiable execution in a specific scenario while dynamic routing across skills maintains open-ended task variety.

### 5. Enough is as Good as a Feast: RL Mitigates Task Conflicts
**arXiv:2607.22039** — cs.CL | July 24, 2026 | Published at ICLR 2026

Systematic study of RL-trained vs SFT-trained LLMs for **model merging**. RL significantly reduces task conflicts and results in less performance degradation after merging. Three key factors:
1. On-policy training data controls gradient updates in smaller magnitude — reduces risk of overwriting existing knowledge
2. RL objective progressively reduces magnitude and number of conflict parameter updates as model converges ("enough is as good as a feast")
3. Joint optimization of positive/negative examples steers model toward unbiased task-specific parameter subspace

**Key insight:** RL is inherently better suited for model merging than SFT — the optimization dynamics naturally suppress conflicting updates rather than fighting them.

### 6. Consensus-Based Relative Preference Evaluation
**arXiv:2607.21632** — cs.CL | July 19, 2026

Introduces **Relative Intelligence Index (RII)** — measures how frequently a model's responses are preferred by a panel of diverse LLMs under blind conditions. Addresses limitation of traditional benchmarks that rely on static datasets and objective scoring where multiple answers may be acceptable.

**Key insight:** Aggregate inter-model agreement is a scalable proxy for perceived response quality — not aligned with objective correctness, but prior work suggests correlation with human judgments.

### 7. Molt: PyTorch-Native Training for Agentic RL
**arXiv:2607.21653** — cs.LG, cs.CL | July 22, 2026

NVIDIA's **Molt** framework addresses the cost of algorithm change in agentic RL research. Each modification threads through trainer, distributed backend, and rollout glue — cost lands on researcher every iteration. Molt is:
- PyTorch-native: codebase compact enough for a researcher to hold in their head and for AI coding assistant to read and reason about in entirety
- Asynchronous loop training for multimodal and MoE policies
- Never trains on a token it did not generate (consistent in tokens, policy versions, model semantics)
- Statistically comparable to state-of-the-art Megatron-based stack under matched protocol

**Key insight:** The bottleneck in agentic RL research is framework complexity, not algorithm quality — lean PyTorch-native infrastructure enables faster iteration.

### 8. LLM Capability Taxonomy: From Isolated Tasks to Structured Capabilities
**arXiv:2607.22182** — cs.CL, cs.AI | July 24, 2026

Maps 15,934 LLM-focused papers from ACL/AAAI/ICML/NeurIPS (2023-2025) through multi-model annotation onto a **multi-layer taxonomy** of 14 capability domains and 91 subskills. Key findings:
- Direct research attention concentrated on Language-Semantic Competence (22.3%), Reasoning (21.3%), Planning/Decision-Making (13.5%), Perception (12.3%)
- Six domains appear in fewer than 2% of papers
- Theory of Mind + Social Reasoning shows highest lift among co-occurring pairs (lift=30.84)
- Language-Semantic Competence + Reasoning forms highest-volume pair (n=1,864, lift=2.47)

**Key insight:** Shifting unit of analysis from isolated tasks to structured capabilities enables research organization, coverage audits, evaluation interpretation, and testable hypotheses for diagnosis, training, and transfer.
