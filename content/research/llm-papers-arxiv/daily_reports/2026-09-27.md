### 1. Agent-Editing World Model (AEWM): Editing Noisy Reasoning for LLM Agents
**arXiv: 2609.28416** | Shuang Sun et al. | cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG

AEWM is a world model for LLM agents that focuses on modeling task progress rather than simulating tool responses. The key innovation is addressing **task-state contamination** — where unsupported assumptions and outdated plans persist in history and distort subsequent decisions.

Key components:
- **Action Judge**: classifies decisions as Critical, Exploratory, or Noisy
- **State Revision**: edits noisy reasoning-action continuations from observed history
- **EditAct**: integrates these capabilities with real execution — changes the state underlying decisions rather than providing critiques

Results across six benchmarks and three agent backbones show 3.2–6.7 point improvements over the strongest baseline. AEWM achieves 70.5% macro-F1 on the Action Judge benchmark. Rejection sampling fine-tuning on verified EditAct trajectories (AEWM-RFT) improves over Self-RFT by 2.2–2.6 points.

> **Key finding:** The agent's internal task state — not just its outputs — is a site for intervention. Editing reasoning history directly outperforms critique-based approaches.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28416

### 2. Control-Token Injection: Silencing Chain-of-Thought to Bypass Safety in Tool-Using Agents
**arXiv: 2609.27542** | Usama Muhammad et al. | cs.CR | **Security Critical**

This paper demonstrates a **critical safety vulnerability** in tool-using LLM agents. The attack appends a single string of channel-control tokens to a user message, which causes the tokenizer to render a reasoning turn as already complete — the model writes no chain-of-thought and proceeds directly to the tool call.

On gpt-oss-20b under its published tool sandbox:
- Mean reasoning channel drops from **52.5 tokens to zero** on every trial
- The tool call still fires on every trial
- The attack converts **39.6% of refusals into completed exfiltrations** on overtly malicious requests
- Whether a tool-call generation fires is decided by the **harness parser, not the model** — two parsers shipped for Gemma agent give opposite outcomes on identical greedy generations

Defenses evaluated: input sanitization, parser hardening, and empty-reasoning detection — with empty-reasoning detection catching the basic attack but missing adaptive benign decoys.

> **Key finding:** LLM safety is a joint property of the model and the decoding harness. Both halves are attackable from untrusted input. Safety evaluations that test models in isolation miss this attack surface entirely.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27542

### 3. LEGO: Expert GraphRAG + Expert Chain-of-Thought for Legal Reasoning
**arXiv: 2609.27009** | Qingjing Chen et al. | cs.CL, cs.IR | **Accepted to EMNLP 2026 (Findings)**

LEGO addresses two structural challenges in legal reasoning: (1) existing RAG methods overlook normative relations among legal provisions, and (2) vanilla CoT generates plausible rationales without enforcing normative structure.

Architecture:
- **ExpertGraphRAG**: Uses expert-annotated civil code graph encoding normative relations + greedy normative-coverage retrieval algorithm to dynamically extract provision subgraphs
- **ExpertCoT**: Organizes retrieved provisions and case facts into structured Provision-Fact-Conclusion reasoning

With Qwen3-8B backbone, LEGO achieves **40.53% exact-match accuracy** on LawExamQA_Civil, outperforming evaluated RAG and CoT baselines while performing comparably to larger models. Ablation studies confirm the individual and complementary contributions of both modules.

> **Key finding:** Legal reasoning requires structured retrieval over normative relations, not just semantic similarity. Combining expert-annotated graphs with structured CoT outperforms both generic RAG and generic CoT approaches.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27009

### 4. MISVO: Minimally Invasive Steering Vector Optimization
**arXiv: 2609.30218** | Mahyar Fazlyab et al. | cs.LG, cs.AI | **Efficiency**

MISVO addresses test-time reward optimization via pre-logit steering. The core problem: unregularized reward optimization can substantially alter output distribution and degrade generation quality.

Method:
- Penalizes interventions using the **local KL geometry** of the induced token distribution
- Uses a **Fisher quadratic** that measures distributional sensitivity with analytic gradient computed through matrix-vector products
- Derives exact decomposition of sequence-level KL gradient into an analytic Fisher term and a suffix score-function term
- For fixed generation horizon, the suffix term is second-order in steering magnitude

Across preference and code-generation tasks on models with approximately 1B–14B parameters, MISVO achieves the **highest mean reward in six of seven model-task settings**, with diversity and coherence scores close to Best-of-N.

> **Key finding:** Test-time steering benefits from Fisher-based regularization that respects local KL geometry — this preserves output distribution quality while achieving reward objectives.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30218

### 5. Return or Revise? Recoverability for RAG Revision Decisions
**arXiv: 2609.30087** | Jeremy Gwinnup et al. | cs.CL, cs.IR

Addresses a key RAG question: should the system return the existing draft answer or revise it using retrieved evidence? The insight is that **draft confidence is insufficient** — the decision requires estimating the effect of the specific revision.

Key concept: **recoverability** — grading both the returned draft and its candidate revision under the same correctness judge, making repair, harm, and the gap to oracle observable.

On 25,870 held-out open-domain questions:
- A scorer trained on paired outcomes outperforms draft-correctness scorer in all nine Llama setups
- Gains 0.23–0.68 accuracy points at development-selected thresholds
- When a draft-free standard-RAG answer is also available, choosing between draft and that answer is stronger by ~2 points for Llama and ~4 for OLMo
- Adding candidate revision as a third option yields **no significant gain** over choosing between draft and standard-RAG

> **Key finding:** Revision only helps when the system can predict recoverability. In the absence of that signal, draft-free RAG is a stronger default than naive revision.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30087

## 🔍 Meta-Analysis: Three Themes

### Theme 1: Safety Is a Joint Property of Model + Harness
The Control-Token Injection paper (2609.27542) is the clearest demonstration yet that treating safety as a model-only property is insufficient. The decoding harness — the software that renders chat templates and parses tool calls — is equally attackable. This has implications for red-teaming protocols, safety benchmarks, and deployment practices.

### Theme 2: Editing Internal State Outperforms External Critique
AEWM (2609.28416) and LEGO (2609.27009) both show that intervening on the reasoning structure itself — editing task-state history or structuring provision-fact relationships — produces better outcomes than generating critiques or explanations. This points toward a new class of agent architectures built around **state editing** rather than **critique-based refinement**.

### Theme 3: Test-Time Steering Needs Better Regularization
MISVO (2609.30218) and the recoverability paper (2609.30087) both address test-time interventions: steering vectors and RAG revision. Both highlight that naive interventions degrade quality — the key is principled regularization that respects the model's output distribution.
