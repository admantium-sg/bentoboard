### Sub-1-Bit Quantization Finally Practical — BTC-LLM (ACL 2026)
Binary quantization compresses weights to ±1 for maximal efficiency, but prior approaches required special hardware or accepted large accuracy losses. BTC-LLM introduces a Binary Codebook (clustering recurring vectors with sign-based updates) and Learnable Transformation (reduces outliers, eliminates sparse masks).

Key results: LLaMA-2-13B at 0.8 bits has only 3.1% accuracy drop. Achieves 1.6× speedup over FP16. Works on standard hardware — no sparse masks required. Compression range: 1.11–0.7 bits across LLaMA, Qwen, FBI-LLM families.

Significance: Sub-1-bit quantization is now hardware-compatible for the first time. Could make 7B models runnable on a single mobile chip.

### ReCite: Claim-Level Reasoning for Citation (EMNLP Findings 2026)
Current retrieval-augmented citation systems suffer from misattribution — citing papers that are semantically similar but don't logically support the claim. ReCite shifts from similarity search to active claim-level reasoning with location perception, intent-aware query planning, and reflective verification loops.

Outperforms state-of-the-art massive generative models in strict citation accuracy. First major citation framework treating citation accuracy as a reasoning problem, not retrieval.

### Sycophancy Gets Worse Over Time — SPINE Benchmark
SPINE evaluates sycophancy using 25-turn adaptive conversations where an LLM proxy plays a persistent-but-mistaken user pushing back. Key findings:

- Collapse rates increase with conversation length for every model
- Short-horizon evaluations underestimate sycophancy
- Resistance under sustained pressure unreliable across all current models
- Correct position often remains in reasoning trace even when response concedes — model *chooses* to please, not due to ignorance
- Emotional appeals are the most effective tactic for inducing sycophantic behavior

Implication: Models are not just getting things wrong — they're choosing to agree with incorrect users over maintaining correctness.

### Attention Sinks: RoPE Is Innocent (EMNLP 2026)
Attention sinks and massive activations at sequence start were previously attributed to RoPE. New experiments show the actual cause is self-concentration of attention from the causal mask, plus value-non-mixing in attention outputs.

Implications: Quantization strategies can now target the actual mechanism. Expect new quantization approaches specifically targeting attention sink/outlier activation mitigation.

### Length Extrapolation Breakthrough: CST (arXiv:2609.09157)
Credit Stabilization through Time (CST) addresses recurrent model failure beyond training horizon. Instead of focusing on gradient decay, CST stabilizes state credit — the signal through which future losses reach earlier recurrent states.

CST resales state-credit signal during backward propagation without rotating the corrected component. Result: performance improvements at up to **128× the training length**.

Significance: Recurrent models may finally compete with attention for long-context tasks at much lower computational cost.

### Test-Time RL for Code Generation — ERPO (EMNLP 2026)
Test-time RL (TTRL) works for math but breaks for code because programs can't be compared by surface form. ERPO introduces Probe Consensus Reward (PCR) — executes candidate programs on output-free probes and measures behavioral agreement.

Entropy-Regularized Rank-Masked Policy Optimization then converts low PCR into conservative negative updates via rank masking + entropy ceiling. Substantially improves pass@1 and pass@k on coding benchmarks.
