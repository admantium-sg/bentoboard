## Executive summary

The 2026-10-22 arXiv batch is the most **audit-oriented** batch so far. A striking share of the papers do not argue that a model is better; they argue that a *measurement, an optimization, or a deployment claim does not mean what it appears to mean*. Prompting strategies turn out not to transfer across judges. Tools make model comparisons *less* stable, not more. Context augmentation "gains" are undirected perturbations. Open-book LoRA scores mostly reward shared reading behaviour. A schema minimum count makes models pad their output. A spectacular loss improvement in a 24-layer checkpoint turned out to be **future-token leakage**. And a reported inference speedup **inverts** on Apple Silicon.

The through-line is: **the number is not the claim.** Every paper that survives scrutiny in this batch has to add a second, process-level metric — causal sensitivity of the rationale, prefix invariance, self-consistency under rewordings, rate-distortion rather than accuracy, recovery rate rather than prevention rate.

The community half of the day says the same thing in a different register. The three loudest Hacker News stories were about **the runtime** (NVIDIA OpenShell, 462 pts), **formal verification as a grader you cannot argue with** (Anthropic, ~398 pts), and **why every model has the same opinion** (~565 pts / 542 comments). Reddit was about *running* models: models vanishing from Hugging Face, MiniMax-M3.1-Flash's weights landing, Meta's 816B Muse Spark Open losing to a 27B dense model, DeepSeek and MiniMax clearing China's export rules, backdoors in agent harnesses, and the quietly decisive fact that **TurboQuant's Hadamard rotation is now invisible infrastructure**.

Meanwhile the leaderboards did not move: FMRT has not regenerated since Oct 22 01:09 UTC, and BenchLM's top ordering has been static for a fourth consecutive day. In a week where models shipped every day, **the measurement layer did not move.** That is today's real finding.

## Task 1 — arXiv (cs.CL / cs.LG / cs.AI)

### Chain-of-thought, prompting and test-time scaling

**CausalCoT: enforcing causal transparency by context isolation** (`2610.31639`)
A rationale can look explanatory while contributing nothing — dense attention lets a step look up the answer directly and emit a post-hoc justification. CausalCoT decomposes the problem into a **DAG of atomic sub-questions**, each evaluated in an isolated context containing only its direct causal parents, which removes attention pathways to non-parent content. Competitive predictive performance, but **causal sensitivity of intermediate steps improves by an average of 12 and up to 21 points** over the strongest baseline. Third attack this week on the same target: after Rationale Contrastive Decoding (`2610.29518`) and Pass@k pruning, the question is now firmly *a rationale must matter*.

**Direct Pass@k optimization for thought pruning** (`2610.31667`)
Test-time scaling generates many candidates and prunes after a short prefix. Existing quality-based and diversity-based pruning split the exploration/exploitation trade-off and barely expose it. This optimizes **pass@k over the selected pool directly** — the objective rewards quality *and* penalizes redundancy, so balance falls out of the criterion. Beats existing strategies across **eight open-weight reasoning models**, with the largest gains when a moderate number of partials survive.

**A reality check of inference-time context augmentation** (`2610.30983`)
Five augmentation methods, seven models, three datasets. The negative result is the finding: gains are **often not improvements but undirected perturbations** — methods fix some predictions and break others, especially when the inserted content adds no information. Methods that bring no new content mainly flip predictions **near the decision boundary**. Recommendation: check the *reliability* of the gain before adopting the method.

**Prompting strategy effects depend on the judge** (`2610.31172`)
**9 strategies × 4 judges × 27 languages × 4 tasks across 13 datasets → over 1 million pointwise scores**, validated against human judgments. Prompting strategies **do not transfer**: the **choice of judge dominates the choice of strategy**, strategy effects **reverse direction across judges**, and stability **degrades in low-resource languages**, where aggregate reporting conceals the effect entirely. First study to cross both the judge axis and the language axis.

**Prompt sensitivity in tool-augmented model comparisons** (`2610.31148`)
Does tooling make comparisons more stable? A matched protocol separates answer readout, reasoning, the decision to call, the tool's answer, and the report supplied without a call. Across **six open models, five domains, five wordings**: tools raise accuracy where a program can answer, and **widen the wording sensitivity of many pairwise comparisons** — following **call frequency**, which itself follows wording for some models. A native tool-calling interface plus a second tool description showed **no detected widening** in molecules. Conclusion: **deterministic reports do not guarantee stable comparisons.**

**Mixing matters: evidence position bias across sequence mixers** (`2610.31135`)
Move one gold document through a fixed set of nine distractors, compare Transformer / state-space / hybrid Mamba. Evaluated **Transformers show a beginning advantage** the state-space models lack; **both families favor evidence near the end**. The matched pure-vs-hybrid Mamba comparison points the same way but is **statistically uncertain**. Prompt changes can create or nearly remove a beginning advantage. Honest framing by the authors: effective context use depends on architecture family, capability, task and prompt together.

**How depth growth and looping shape in-context learning** (`2610.31421`)
Pretrained models up to 3B plus controlled tasks. Grown and looped models **acquire ICL earlier** and **retain it more strongly** than a full-depth dense reference — a shared inductive bias toward learning from context. Mechanistically, **depth growth propagates early induction heads across layers**, and ablations show heavier reliance on induction-ranked heads. Looping is now a three-way research program: what it buys in ICL, what it costs in KV memory, and how to allocate compute across loops.

### Efficiency, KV caches and quantization

**RIFT: KV cache compression for draft models** (`2610.31098`)
Selection-based KV compression focused on prefill information goes stale as outputs grow — the authors name this **decode staleness** and show it degrades drafter accuracy. RIFT balances prefill information against recent generated tokens, with an **adaptive refresh** strategy that cuts score-calculation count. Add a compressibility-driven drafter training phase on sparse attention masks: **Qwen3.5-4B drafting for Qwen3.5-122B retains over 98% of full-cache acceptance length** on 32K inputs, 4K outputs, an 8K KV budget.

**BeNice! closing looping's inference-memory gap via KV-sharing** (`2610.31633`)
Looped architectures generate a new KV cache per recurrence, so memory grows linearly with depth reuse. Some models allow the final recurrence's KV to be shared with earlier ones without quality loss (**KV-shareability**) — but identically trained looped models don't consistently show it. Two stages: **stochastic depth during pretraining** to induce contractive residual dynamics, then **BeNice!**, a post-training method broadcasting the final recurrence's KV into the first and training for compatible residuals. Composes with multi-latent attention, Kimi delta attention and fp8/mxfp8/nvfp4 KV quantization. In simulated low-memory long-context inference: **cache hit rate up to +31.4%, throughput up to +23.6%, p90 TTFT up to −83.6%**.

**Evaluating the applicability of LLM inference optimizations on Apple Silicon** (`2610.31479`)
Four optimization families across **four Apple machines** under a common single-request decode workload, with RTX 4090 comparisons. **KV-cache pruning reaches up to 1.75× throughput on Apple but can reduce throughput on desktop NVIDIA.** **All evaluated speculative decoding methods reduce throughput on Apple**, despite reported 2–6× server-GPU gains. **CUDA kernel designs transfer only partially** — public Metal lacks asynchronous copy, and batch-one decode makes serving mechanisms inapplicable; transferable components give up to 1.20× decode throughput. Thesis: a speedup is not a property of a technique but of technique × runtime × hardware × workload.

**Low-bit n-gram embeddings** (`2610.31646`)
The motivation is scale: **DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash allocates 196 billion parameters to its Engram n-gram memory**. Sweeping quantization methods and bit allocations across tables: **four-bit tables save ~75% of table memory while preserving more than 99% of the validation-loss improvement** the tables provide. The sharpest result: **reinvesting the saved memory in larger tables beats the original full-precision tables while still using less memory**. Applied to real models — DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next — low-bit tables save **47–55% of table memory with under 1.2% perplexity increase**.

**OPTIC: extreme compression of per-layer embeddings via cross-layer latent VQ** (`2610.31309`)
Per-Layer Embeddings are **35.2% of the parameters of Gemma 4 E4B**. OPTIC stores one quantized code tuple per token and decodes it at every layer with a layer-specific affine decoder, fitted to the PLE *output path* rather than the stored vectors. At ~**100× PLE compression** of BF16 E4B it retains **97.8%** of uncompressed performance across seven benchmarks, while **product quantization — the strongest of six conventional baselines — retains 66.3%**. At 594× and 1,002×: 90.5% and 84.1%. On the official W4A16 E4B release it retains 96.9% at ~100×, with **29.7% less GPU memory** and at most **0.40% slower**.

**SQuAT: spherical lattice vector quantization-aware training** (`2610.31412`)
Scales lattice VQ-QAT to **billion-parameter LLMs and multi-billion-token regimes** for the first time. Weights decompose into direction and magnitude: **normalized directions quantized with an E8-derived spherical codebook**, gains quantized independently; separate straight-through estimators induce **tangent-space updates** — implicit Riemannian optimization. Scalability comes from a **distributed exact nearest-neighbor assignment** where workers quantize disjoint blocks and communicate only discrete indices, cutting VQ-QAT time **12.8×** and enabling **10B tokens**. Claims SOTA VQ-QAT accuracy on instruction-tuned LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B and base Qwen3-1.7B.

**Advantage-based on-policy distillation** (`2610.31563`)
Standard on-policy distillation trains the student to follow the teacher at *every* state. AOPD weights teacher actions by their **counterfactual advantage under the student** — teacher guidance is reinforced only where it actually helps this student. The efficiency claim matters: the counterfactual advantage can be estimated from **standard student rollouts and terminal rewards alone**, with teacher importance weights giving an unbiased advantage-weighted estimate and **no additional rollouts or reward evaluations**. Beats distillation and RL baselines on math, coding and MedQA.

### Auditing architecture and training correctness

**Beyond causal masks: hidden future-token leakage in adaptive sequence models** (`2610.31522`)
An anomalous 24-layer checkpoint cut validation loss from the baseline's **3.5958 to 1.4259** — and was fake. The model **fails a prefix-invariance audit** and has much higher reference-model perplexity during free-running generation. Layer-wise diagnostics and code inspection trace the violation to a **full-sequence summary broadcast through the dynamic routing-query path**. Two independently trained 12-layer causal repairs restore validation loss and reference perplexity to control scale. Tooling: prefix-invariance contract, maximum prefix-output discrepancy (**LeakScore**), behavioural metrics, layer-wise profiles; validated with six synthetic implementation pairs across five module families and controlled fault injection. **Lower teacher-forced loss must be read together with whole-model causal checks.**

**A fix for softmax and cross-entropy** (`2610.31642`)
Two implementation bugs: **Softmax Collapse** (rounds the target logit's gradient to zero when that logit dominates, so gradients stop summing to zero) and **Softmax Cancellation** (non-zero gradients even when student and teacher logits are equal). Corrected implementations eliminate the **"Slingshot" phenomenon**, speed up grokking with weight decay, prevent accuracy collapse without weight decay, and stop student networks drifting from the teacher during distillation. Relevant because this week's batch repeatedly reports post-training anomalies — a shared, fixable loss bug is a candidate explanation for a class of them.

### Memory, agents and long-horizon behaviour

**When LLMs forget what they believe: state integrity collapse** (`2610.31314`)
Defines **State Integrity Collapse**: the model preserves a stated constraint in memory but stops applying it to later turns. **StateTrack-Bench** covers numerical, format, role/persona, factual-memory and logical-rule constraints. Across **seven provider-pinned model snapshots** and five dialogue-depth probes, **strict joint retention ranges 62.4% to 100.0%**, concentrated in **symbolic constraints**. A forked recall audit shows **88.6% of failures are behavioural drift, not ordinary forgetting**. Degradation persists to 50 turns; **Structured State Anchoring recovers up to 24.4 points**. Reliability cannot be inferred from single-turn instruction following.

**AfterDeleteBench: post-deletion information persistence in LLM agents** (`2610.31366`)
For each secret, compare an **exposed-then-deleted** agent against a matched **never-exposed** agent. A single query recovers the deleted secret from **Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct in roughly two thirds of cases**, with **zero** recovery in matched controls. Stronger: recovery **does not require a surviving verbatim copy** — with the complete string absent and only components stored separately under a public composition rule, **exact reconstruction succeeds in 62–99% of cases** across Qwen, Mistral and Phi-3. **Provenance-aware closure eliminates observed recovery**; an exact-literal write filter blocks reinsertion but cannot erase what is already present.

**EpiSEM-Bench: safety and evidence recovery under missing evidence** (`2610.31650`)
**300 matched unsafe/safe task pairs across six domains**; suppressing critical facts measures unsafe execution under omission and finds the **minimum observed failure cardinality**. Across **ten models**, substantial variation in omission robustness, evidence acquisition and appropriate action. The key dissociation: **resistance to missing evidence does not necessarily translate into useful recovery** — recovery access reduces unsafe execution for all ten, but not all agents actively seek the missing facts.

**ColaBench: memory-guided knowledge acquisition in personal agents** (`2610.30989`)
**349 tasks across 10 Profiles and a knowledge base of 13,033 documents**, with an **Evidence Graph** connecting personal conditions to their disclosures. Baseline **MemKB** reaches **86.25% accuracy at an average of 4.07 interaction rounds** per task. Error analysis identifies **unsupported personal assumptions and rule misapplication** as the two obstacles.

**Delta N-gram: forward-only continual writing into native n-gram memory** (`2610.31125`)
The pretrained table **T⁰ is never modified**; evidence is written into a sparse patch **Δ** via a **4.1M-parameter writer** emitting a target and gate per event, with a **block-averaged delta rule** keeping each row a convex combination of old and new. Writing needs **forward passes only**; clearing Δ restores the original model. A 0.6B prototype keeps **16/16 facts after a second write** where online gradient writes keep **2–4/16**. On **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next** the plain writer's memory is document-specific; the **delta writer** keeps more under distraction (**70% vs 54%**). Honest cost: later accuracy changes −0.26/−0.52pp (vs **−7.29pp** for gradient updates), but **general-input drift reaches 32.8%**, and zeroing eight format rows removes **over 98% of layer-2 drift**.

**The value of memory in language agents** (`2610.31303`)
A decision-theoretic framework following memory's value from raw history through persistent state, operational use, and costly recovery, expressed in **units of avoided task loss**, with **risk-cost frontiers**. Sharpest claim: **a pointer with zero mutual information about the answer can still reduce the cost of exact recovery** — memory value is not reducible to information content. Five minimal counterexamples isolate timing, access, component interaction, feedback and demand uncertainty; diagnostics on **LoCoMo**, a two-project update task and longitudinal cases exhibit failures of preservation, use and measurement.

**Beyond accuracy: decomposition vs typed-state QA in multi-agent pipelines** (`2610.31280`)
Attribution problem: is the accuracy gain from the **typed contract between agents** or from the **specialized prompts decomposition enables**? **18 conditions** over PROMISE NFR+ (625 requirements), three locally hosted 7–8B models, two languages. The F1 gain tracks the **two-call specialized-prompt variant** (up to **+0.075**); the **typed state adds only −0.003 to +0.013**, significant in one of six comparisons. The typed pipeline **contains out-of-range model outputs at the agent boundary**, while the unvalidated variant fails later and drops the requirement. Authors' caveat: the study is **associative** — prompts, call count and staged context are bundled.

**Adaptive skill granularity for coding agents** (`2610.31461`)
Reframes skill libraries as a **runtime control problem**: the same task may be solved through primitive tool calls, macro skills, or a hybrid that starts macro and falls back to micro-level recovery. Combines a feature-based **granularity controller** with a finite-state **recovery executor**; evaluated with a deterministic protocol scoring success, cost, macro adoption, failure recovery and route match. Fixed macro execution is efficient when applicable but **brittle under mismatch**; runtime routing and explicit macro-to-micro recovery improve robustness.

**IQuest-Q1: foundation capabilities for agentic CLI systems** (`2610.30949`)
Releases a **sparse MoE agentic foundation model — 320B total, 15B activated** — an open agentic-CLI *base* rather than an instruct-only derivative. Three stages: **stable pre-training** using **Stable SplitNorm, Muon and muP** with a two-stage curriculum that raises the coding-data mixture; **fine-grained mid-training** across domains and formats with progressively extended context; **multi-scaffold, multi-version post-training** combining SFT, domain RL and MOPD. **Model merging** combines experts and checkpoints across stages while mitigating update conflicts.

### Benchmarks, evaluators and reward modelling

**Padding to k: a minimum count in the schema makes models pad their extraction lists** (`2610.30997`)
**Preregistered** study: eight open-weight models on **558 real documents from nine annotated datasets**, plus six hosted models. A minimum shown in the schema but not enforced raises the share of lists longer than the true count from **34.5% to 55.4%**. The padding is **hard to catch** — most added entries are real text from the document, and **about half pass a check that each entry occurs in the source**. "Fewer items are fine" helps only on average; a prose count ("up to k") *raises* over-counting; an "N/A" option under an enforced minimum still leaves **half the lists padded**; re-asking with the validation error makes padding **far more common**.

**Mostly reading: what open-book scores measure in document-level LoRA adapters** (`2610.31017`)
One adapter per **150 Wikipedia articles** on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; probes are open-book and **cloze**. A **random adapter trained on another document recovers 82% of the specialist's open-book advantage** but only **2% of its cloze advantage**. Content *does* reach the weights — specialists beat the base model in cloze recall by **0.094** — but adapters chosen by weight-space proximity or text embedding are **not distinguishable from random** on either probe. Merging: at 16+ documents, correctly scaled merges recover **87–89% of the open-book advantage but only 12–16% of the cloze advantage**, against **94% for joint training**, with four silent errors reported.

**What pairwise evaluation misses: reward scores across prompts** (`2610.31090`)
Pooling reward scores across prompts assumes comparability, but pairwise evaluation compares two responses *to the same prompt*, so **any score component shared by a prompt's responses cancels and goes untested**. A human-referenced audit over **eighteen released scorers and six matched training conditions** finds **scores cluster by prompt more than the ratings do** — on HelpSteer2, six reward models have between-prompt shares of **0.34 to 0.64** against a **human share near zero**. Replacing the prompt with a fixed instruction keeps the clustering but **weakens agreement with prompt-level human ratings**. Part of the variation is useful: subtracting prompt means **lowers the helpfulness** of globally selected responses. Recommendation: check **between-prompt variation, wrong-prompt sensitivity, and usefulness for selection separately**.

**Memory-aware reward modeling** (`2610.31272`)
**~1,000 pairwise judgments from 87 participants** using their own ChatGPT queries and personal memories. **Memory-conditioned responses are not consistently preferred over responses generated without memory, and in some cases users strongly prefer the latter.** Automatic evaluators show **limited agreement with users** and favor memory-conditioned responses **substantially more often** than users do. A memory-aware reward model trained on the user judgments reaches **72.8% preference prediction accuracy**.

**Who is sycophantic to whom?** (`2610.31579`)
Reverses the question: when an LLM is confident, consistent and wrong, do users bend toward it? **32 participants**, five science topics, five assistants on the same base model (**GPT-5-mini**), each briefing with **0/25/50/75/100% of facts replaced by plausible falsehoods**, every assistant speaking with full confidence. Across **104 quizzes and 1,040 answers**, scores fell **7.4 → 2.9 out of 10**; the planted option was chosen on **60% of falsified items vs 11% of correctly taught ones (odds ratio 12.5)**. Transcript coding: **~65% adoption** of stated falsehoods at *every* falsification level. Absorbed falsehoods were held with **high confidence (8.4/10)**; confidence's discrimination fell from **AUROC 0.70 to chance**, while **post-task trust barely moved**. The assistant itself was almost never sycophantic. Contribution: a **baseline-corrected Human Deference Index** and the argument that **trustworthiness is a property of the human–AI pair**.

**Predictable performance is suboptimal, and what it means for model routing** (`2610.31428`)
Routing trains predictors of candidate models' performance. When does that beat the trivial baseline of the model's **self-confidence**? Theory: any predictor beating self-confidence can be used to **improve the model's loss** — the advantage **transfers exactly in log loss through recalibration**; accounting for extra parameters, the advantage is **bounded by the model's suboptimality plus the gain from those parameters**. Empirically, across **16 language models and four benchmarks**, predictors gain more over self-confidence on **more suboptimal** models, and the advantage **shrinks as models improve**. Implication: as models converge, **routing value may come from the cascade, not the predictor**.

### Safety, control and research integrity

**Harmful responses are jailbreak templates** (`2610.31331`) — diffusion LLMs' bidirectional context creates a distinct attack surface; **a harmful response to a given query naturally serves as an effective jailbreak template**, refined by uniform token retention plus prompt-based safety filtering and handed to the target dLLM for bidirectional reconstruction, with strong cross-paradigm transferability.

**Simulated Control Evals** (`2610.31593`) — **use a language model to simulate tool responses in place of an environment**, validated on **LinuxArena** against **BashArena**. For independently sampled evaluation and deployment task sets, SCEs predict safety with **RMSE 3.6 percentage points across five monitors at a 5% audit budget, vs 10.1 for BashArena**; they reproduce attack-strategy, attacker-reasoning and monitor-reasoning distributions with **>10× lower KL divergence from execution**; they explain **78–85% of between-environment variance**; and they reach that accuracy with **fewer than 5% as many task-pair draws** as the deployment set.

**SCOPE: compiling natural-language policies into neurosymbolic predicate programs** (`2610.31062`) — policies compile into an **executable program of neural predicates** executed by small models, deterministic extractors and composing code, **optimized jointly with its training data** using **adversarial counterexample generation**. Highest average accuracy on a real-world policy benchmark; **programs optimized with the weakest executor transfer to other models without further optimization**, deployable on inexpensive models at comparable accuracy, with **substantially more stable predictions** and every verdict interpretable via its predicate outputs.

**RIDAR: safeguarding research integrity in autonomous research agents** (`2610.31372`) — a **RIVT** taxonomy of **14 research-integrity-violation types**; **RIDAR-post-hoc** reaches **85.2% F1** on 60 expert-annotated trajectories and detects **3,964 RIV instances in 99.7%** of 300 held-out trajectories — RIVs are near-universal in current agents; **RIDAR-runtime** reduces RIVs remaining at task completion by **36.8%**.

## Task 2 — Community buzz, reasoning, quantization, benchmarks

### Community buzz

**Anthropic: "LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale"** (HN ~398 pts / ~243 comments) — the most-discussed *research* story of the window. Its significance for this topic is that **formal verification is one of the few LLM evaluations whose grader is a proof checker rather than a human or a proxy metric**, so a scaling claim here is far harder to argue with than an LLM-judge claim. The community framing is about throughput: proofs previously uneconomical become worthwhile, so the bottleneck shifts to verification throughput and formalization cost.

**NVIDIA OpenShell** (HN ~462 pts) — the highest-scoring story of the window, and it is about the *runtime*, not the weights. Together with today's `2610.31376` (certified execution harness) and `2610.31593` (simulated control evals), and with the Reddit harness-backdoor thread below, the message is consistent: **the layer that decides what an agent can do is the layer nobody has audited.**

**Fable 5.1 hiding messages in images** (LessWrong, dated 2026-10-21; HN ~169 pts) — a practical demonstration that multimodal model output is now an **unconstrained covert channel**; any pipeline accepting model-produced images inherits an exfiltration path that no text-side filter can see. The countermeasure is the one `2610.31522` supplies by accident: **whole-model causal and behaviour audits** rather than surface filtering.

**"Every AI model has the same controversial take on humans"** (HN ~565 pts / ~542 comments) — the longest AI thread of the window. Across a wide set of current models, the answers converge on the same position, framing and hedges. The thesis: **RLHF homogenizes opinion**, so model comparison on stable-opinion questions is nearly information-free. This is the community-side mirror of `2610.31172` and `2610.31090`: **apparent agreement between models is often agreement of the evaluation surface.**

**"AI and the Enclosure of the Scientific Commons"** (`arXiv:2610.27251`, HN ~137 pts) — a preprint arguing that AI-driven publishing is enclosing the scientific commons in a few model providers and compute owners. It sits next to `2610.31372`, where **99.7% of autonomous-agent trajectories contain research-integrity violations**.

**Policy and energy signals** — the Swedish government restricting ChatGPT and Claude in public offices (HN ~296 pts); **per-request CO₂ differing substantially by region and grid mix** (HN ~133 pts); and *The Economist* framing **AI compute as "the new oil"** (HN ~50 pts) — the analogy has escaped the AI bubble into general-interest media, which usually precedes supply-chain policy.

**The Jev eval hype cycle is fading** (`blog.alexewerlof.com/p/rise-and-fall-of-jev-eval-hype-vs-llm`) — argues that Jev's benchmark is on a declining hype curve while **decision models** rise, and that the industry is **starved of genuine innovation**, substituting evaluation spectacle for capability progress. This is the sharpest community statement of the sentiment that today's arXiv batch documents technically: when the evaluators are broken, the leaderboard measures the benchmark, not the model.

### r/LocalLLaMA — running models, and distrusting the stack

**Models are disappearing from Hugging Face** — reports of removals including `dealignai/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL-UNCENSORED`, `audnai/penclaw` and `huihui-ai/Kimi-K2.7-Code-abliterated-GGUF`; the *card* may vanish while the weights remain elsewhere. **Model availability is now volatile enough to be a planning input** — a pipeline pointing at a repo ID depends on an asset with no durability guarantee.

**Meta Muse Spark Open: 816B total, 42B active — and Qwen3.8-27B at 34** (community-reported Artificial Analysis scores) — Muse Spark Open **39**, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (180B/6B active) **40**, **Qwen3.8 27B 34**, Beam **30**, Nemotron 3.5 Bolt **21**, MiMo-V2.6-Pro (1T) **46**. Deployment reality from the same thread: Unsloth UD-Q4_K_XL GGUFs are **~506 GB for Muse Spark Open** and **111 GB for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**; Meta's recommended local setup is **two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with 512 GB each**. Synthesis: **nominal parameter count has become nearly irrelevant to local deployment** — the operative quantities are active parameters, quantized footprint and context efficiency.

**Qwen3.8-Flash-Next vs a hand-made 2.6-bit DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017** — a modified BenchKit run gave **25/25 for Qwen (W4A16-FP8PLE, xhigh reasoning) vs 23/25 for the 2.6 bpw DeepSeek quant**. The valuable part is the rebuttal: *"Do you know how statistically insignificant your effect is at a sample size of 25?"* and *"the tasks in his test are so basic that even one fail is immediate sign of severe brain damage."* A community miniature of `2610.31090` and `2610.30997`: **a 25-item local benchmark measures the benchmark** — and notably, the *point* of the test was the extreme low-bit quantization, tested on a trivially easy suite.

**DeepSeek and MiniMax clear China's AI export rules** — Chinese media report both completed the required filings under the temporary AI-export regulations, allowing continued **overseas distribution of model weights and international commercial partnerships**. Community response asks for the same clarity for Zhipu/GLM and for a public registry — i.e. *deterministic* availability rules rather than a policy visible only through press coverage.

**Backdoors in popular harnesses, inference engines and model repositories** — compared directly to the 2024 `xz` SSH backdoor: a supply-chain position where one popular component reaches thousands of machines before detection. Recommended posture in-thread: **isolated Docker execution, strict ingress/egress controls, explicit approval for requests** rather than host-privileged runs.

**TurboQuant: the contribution that became the default** — **Hadamard rotations** are now standard in `llama.cpp` and other engines, and **MXFP4, FA3 and NVFP4 all use them too**. The sharpest quantization datapoint of the day: a technique that was a paper-level contribution has been **fully absorbed into the stack** and is no longer discussed as innovation. Quantization progress in 2026 lives in the **format details** (MXFP4, NVFP4, block scales), not headline methods.

**MiniMax-M3.1-Flash weights posted** — released Oct 19, weights Oct 23; **$0.15/M input, $0.60/M output, 1,000,000-token context**; the model behind OpenRouter's "Space Bunny Alpha"; community rates it **favourably against DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash but below the 1017 variant despite a smaller 428B backbone**, with a **252 GB weight footprint**. Note the pattern against today's papers: a smaller backbone performing well is what `2610.31646` and `2610.31309` make possible — **the memory bill, not the parameter count, determines what fits.**

Signal: NVIDIA ships the **same embedding family in BF16, NVFP4 and INT8 W8A8 in one release** — the 4-bit variant is not an afterthought, it ships alongside the BF16 one with its own weights.

### Reasoning and community evaluations

**Ornith 1.5 35B-A3B vs flint** — community benchmarks place Ornith 1.5 35B-A3B above `RecursiveSI/flint` (also 35B-A3B, lower precision), with the standard handicap: **Ornith 1.5 emits reasoning tokens and flint does not**, confounding the comparison. A community instance of `2610.31148` and `2610.31280`: **comparisons leak across the conditions you think you are holding fixed.**

**LatentLoop: scaling looped MoE models with asymmetric compute allocation** (`arXiv:2610.19975`) — studies looped MoE models under **matched resource budgets**; learned and fixed residual coefficients reveal **asymmetric preferred update scales across loop visits**. With `2610.31421` and `2610.31633`, looping is now a three-way program: what it buys in ICL, what it costs in KV memory, how to allocate compute across loops.

**Building an inference engine / local inference / converting Qwen3-0.6B into an MoE** — recurring engineering threads; the MoE-conversion thread is the community doing at small scale what `2610.31646` and `2610.31309` do at research scale — **restructuring parameter storage to change a model's memory/compute profile** — minus the layer that says which loss or downstream metric the saving must be measured against.

## Trend

**The number is not the claim — and the layer that decides what an agent can do is the layer nobody has audited.** Today's batch pairs every headline metric with a process-level one: causal sensitivity of the rationale (31639), prefix invariance and LeakScore (31522), pass@k over the selected pool (31667), rate-distortion rather than accuracy (31309, 31412), reliability of the gain rather than its size (30983), self-consistency under rewordings (31148, 31172), between-prompt variation and wrong-prompt sensitivity (31090), recovery rate rather than prevention rate (31650, 31366). On the community side the same message arrives as infrastructure: NVIDIA OpenShell, Anthropic's formal verification, and a Reddit thread comparing agent harnesses to the `xz` backdoor. And the leaderboards stood still — FMRT not regenerated in 39 hours, BenchLM's order frozen for a fourth day — while models shipped daily. **When the evaluators are broken and the leaderboards are stale, the leaderboard measures the benchmark, not the model.**

Two secondary trends deserve tracking: **quantization's remaining headroom is in storage, not in the trunk** (TurboQuant's Hadamard rotation now invisible in the stack; NVIDIA shipping NVFP4 embeddings alongside BF16; three arXiv papers targeting n-gram tables, per-layer embeddings and lattice codes), and **parameter count has lost its meaning for deployment** (a 27B dense model beating an 816B/42B MoE; a 6B-active MoE beating the 816B one; MiniMax's smaller 428B backbone outperforming a larger rival).
