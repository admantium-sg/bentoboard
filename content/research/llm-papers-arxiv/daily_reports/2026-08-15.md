### 1. DARTree: Speculative Diffusion Decoding — Up to 9.73× Speedup
**arXiv:2608.13524** (Aug 13, 2026) — cs.LG

DARTree combines the best of speculative decoding and diffusion drafters. Standard diffusion drafters predict entire token blocks in parallel but use marginal (position-wise) distributions — not conditioned on tokens selected along each draft path. DARTree fixes this by extending a pretrained autoregressive correction head from chains to trees.

**Key results across 7 math, code, and chat benchmarks:**
- Up to 12.97 tokens accepted per verification round
- 98.6% more than DFlash, 27.9% more than Domino (same setting)
- Up to **9.73× lossless speedup** over autoregressive decoding
- Training-free method using a fixed-width candidate tree with best-first pruning

**Why it matters:** Lossless inference acceleration remains a key practical challenge. DARTree shows that combining diffusion drafters with tree-structured verification can significantly outperform both standard speculative decoding and chain-based diffusion correction. Practical because it's training-free — can work with existing pretrained AR models.
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13524

### 2. QuoteBench: Matched Scores Hide 55–73 pp of Command-Path Failures
**arXiv:2608.13547** (Aug 13, 2026) — cs.AI, cs.SE

LLM coding agents issue Bash commands through interfaces that serialize, wrap, and reparse model output. Standard evaluations use "matched" execution — same setup for generation and verification — but this conflates command-generation quality with post-generation transport failures.

**QuoteBench methodology:** Exact final-state validation across 56 one-shot tasks from 14 incident-derived families, crossing the generation/execution boundary around a deliberately unescaped added parser.

**Key findings:**
- Replaying the same reply through an added parser lowers success by **55.4 to 73.2 percentage points** (8 configurations)
- Disclosing the boundary recovers 30.4–60.7 pp for 6 of 8 configurations; zero or negative for the other two
- **GPT-5.6-sol's matched gap of -3.6 points hides -64.3 points of damage and +60.7 points of compensation**
- Raw generation is nearly saturated at the frontier — boundary adaptation is what still separates models
- Deployment configuration reorders models: 1 unambiguous reversal among 26 comparable pairs + 4 more on single-task margins

**Why it matters:** This is a fundamental critique of current agent eval methodology. If you don't isolate the generation contract from the execution path, you're not measuring what you think you're measuring. The practical implication: frontier raw generation is nearly solved; the remaining gap is transport-layer robustness.
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13547

### 3. When CoT Helps and When It Hurts: The Serial-Depth Bottleneck
**arXiv:2608.09942** (Aug 8, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG

Systematic empirical study of chain-of-thought prompting using the H_dp bandwidth bound as a conceptual framework. The key insight: CoT acts as a bandwidth bypass for serial computation that exceeds a transformer's single-pass capacity.

**Findings across 3 instruction-tuned models (Qwen-2.5-7B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B) and 5 benchmarks:**
- **High-depth P-complete tasks (GSM8K, MATH):** CoT gives +54 to +68 pp recovery — massive help
- **Shallow TC^0 tasks (MMLU, ARC):** CoT is structurally redundant — Delta in [0.0, +4.6] pp. High no-CoT baselines (up to 95% on ARC) may reflect contamination
- **Intermediate class (HumanEval):** Model-size-dependent transition: +23.2 pp (32B), +9.1 pp (8B), **-28.7 pp (7B)** — CoT actually hurts the smallest model

Cross-benchmark depth-recovery correlation: Spearman rho = 0.661 (p = 0.007, n=15). 9 of 15 McNemar tests significant after Bonferroni correction.

**Why it matters:** First systematic evidence that CoT is not a universal enhancer — it's a bandwidth bypass. For tasks that fit in single-pass computation, CoT adds noise without benefit. For small models on intermediate tasks, CoT can actively hurt. This should reshape how we think about CoT deployment.
**Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09942
