### Hybrid LMs: Attention vs Recurrence — Sharp Functional Split
**"What Attention Recalls and Recurrence Controls in Hybrid Language Models"** (2609.04434, EMNLP 2026)
Qwen3.5 and Falcon-H1 experiments with split-prefill and state-swap interventions reveal a clean division of labor:
- **Attention** = what was said (exact retrieval, 64–98% accuracy via KV cache only; drops to 0% via recurrence only)
- **Recurrence** = how it is said (output language, persona; 70–80% language accuracy via recurrence only, vs ~1% via KV only)
- State-swap confirms causality: answer value from KV side, language/style from recurrent side
- Recurrence-only generation also accepts words *never in context* but sharing meaning — suggesting recurrent state generalizes, not just recalls.

This is one of the cleanest mechanistic dissections of hybrid architecture to date. The practical implication: different components need different training regimes (retrieval-based RL vs. persona/style fine-tuning).

### RLVR Locks Solutions at the Entrance, Not Inside
**"Locked at the Entrance, Open Inside: Where RLVR Narrows the Solution Space"** (2608.29188)
Key findings across training setups:
- Solution coverage falls by **up to 67%** after RLVR training, halving even on problems solved across all checkpoints
- Contraction is **heavily concentrated at the trajectory entrance**: per-token likelihood shifts are **11–16× larger** before the first arithmetic operation than during downstream reasoning
- The policy fails to *access* valid solution families, not to *execute* computation once a path is chosen

Implication: test-time scaling (majority voting, beam search) is diminished on RLVR-trained models because the solution space narrowing happens early. This is a fundamental trade-off in current RLVR approaches — improving pass@1 at the cost of ensemble diversity.

### QeRL: Quantization as Exploration Enhancement for RL
**"QeRL: Beyond Efficiency — Quantization-enhanced Reinforcement Learning for LLMs"** (ICLR 2026, NVlabs)
NVFP4 + LoRA enables RL training of 32B LLMs on a single H100 80GB GPU. Key counterintuitive finding:
- Quantization noise **increases policy entropy** → enhances exploration → enables discovery of better reasoning strategies
- Adaptive Quantization Noise (AQN) dynamically adjusts noise during training
- 1.5× rollout phase speedup; faster reward growth than 16-bit LoRA and QLoRA
- 7B matches full-parameter RL on GSM8K (90.8%) and MATH 500 (77.4%)

The QeRL result upends the assumption that quantization is purely lossy — in RL contexts, controlled noise is beneficial. This connects to broader themes of stochastic computing and regularization in deep learning.

### SAE Features Across Languages: Recurrence ≠ Transfer
**"Causally Validating Multilingual SAE Translation Features in Gemma 2 and 3"** (2609.04808, BlackboxNLP 2026)
- >20 features activate frequently across all multilingual discovery settings
- **Causal validation reveals nearly all have small or inconsistent effects** when amplified/ablated across 23 language pairs
- Only **one feature transfers robustly**: Gemma 2 (L10, 5717) and Gemma 3 (L20, 2456) — consistently improves COMET when amplified, degrades when ablated

Methodology lesson: feature recurrence in SAEs overstates cross-lingual transfer. Only causal intervention validates actual behavioral relevance.

### Phonological Rule-Generalization in LLMs
**"How Do Language Models Represent and Use Phonological Information for Allomorph Selection"** (2609.04708, EMNLP 2026)
For English a/an selection:
- Phonological condition encoded in a **single linear direction** in trigger-token embeddings
- This direction **causally drives** article selection in wug tests (item-level causal validation)
- At prediction time: model forecasts the upcoming trigger token and uses its phonological feature to select the article
- Rule-like generalization extends beyond English to other languages' allomorph selection and to explicit phonological judgment tasks

Another demonstration that LLMs acquire rule-based representations, not just item-level memorization — and that these rules are mechanistically implementable (linearly readable, causally manipulable).

### Other Notable Papers

**MedProb** (2609.04336, EMNLP Findings 2026): Linear probing on frozen VLM representations outperforms prompting on medical VQA (PATH-VQA, SLAKE, VQA-RAD). Smaller VLMs contain more recoverable signal than generation-based evaluation reveals. Free-text generation has up to 10pp answer-position bias; probing has positional bias but differently distributed.

**CAGE** (2609.04647): Coherence-Aware Graph Encoding for RAG. Models between-chunk coherence via entity graphs + RGCN. Matches/outserts monoT5 on multi-hop benchmarks; structurally coherent context yields more precise answers even with comparable or lower retrieval recall.

**LentEx** (2609.04511): Latent Entity Extraction via synthetic data + instruction fine-tuning. First systematic LLM approach to LEE. SOTA on MTEB Clustering Benchmark. Good example of synthetic data generation solving scarce labeled data problems.

**Behavioral Traces for Workplace Agents** (2609.04556): Multi-resolution vocabulary of human behavioral traces (120 operator types, 25 episode types, 5 day-rhythm archetypes). Resolution-dependent interpretation — no single level is optimal across all agent-facing questions. Validated on 667M events from 50k users.

**BioASQ 14b** (2609.04999, CLEF 2026): Agent quorum mechanism where multiple agents with diverse prompts debate and iteratively converge on a consensus answer using adaptive document retention. LLM-as-judge framework for reranking. Phase A MAP rank 5 on Batch 1,3.

## Key Themes — Day 8

1. **Hybrid architecture mechanistic clarity** — The attention/recurrence split is now one of the best-understood functional divisions in LLM internals. Practical applications: route retrieval tasks through attention pathways, style/persona through recurrence.

2. **RLVR's entrance problem** — The finding that RLVR narrows solution space at trajectory entrances (11–16× larger likelihood shifts before first arithmetic op) is a significant and underappreciated limitation. Worth tracking whether this motivates new training algorithms.

3. **Quantization-as-regularization** — QeRL's finding that FP4 noise increases policy entropy and improves RL exploration is counterintuitive and potentially generalizable beyond mathematical reasoning.

4. **SAE feature validation gap** — The systematic finding that recurrent cross-lingual features don't transfer causally is important for interpretability work. Features that "light up" across settings ≠ features that actually steer behavior.

5. **Probing > generation for classification** — MedProb adds to growing evidence that reading internal representations outperforms generation-based evaluation for multiple-choice tasks.
