## 🔬 arXiv Papers (June 25)

### Voice AI Has an Emotional Intelligence Gap
**arXiv:2606.26083** | Voice AI / Safety | cs.CL, eess.AS

Four production realtime voice systems (GPT Realtime 2, Gemini 3.1 Flash Live, Qwen3.5 Omni Plus, Omni Flash) tested on scenarios where words AND vocal delivery convey conflicting information. Results: all four systems act on words rather than voice. They end calls with crying callers who insist nothing is wrong, approve wire transfers authorized in frightened voices, and enroll callers whose agreement is clearly sarcastic.

Surprisingly, three of four systems CAN identify the distress/fear/sarcasm when asked directly — but ignore it when making decisions. This disconnect is termed the "emotional intelligence gap." Prompting systems to explicitly attend to vocal delivery improves performance only partially and inconsistently. Key implication: current realtime voice AI should be used with caution where tone and emotion convey important information.

**Key insight:** Perception ≠ action. Voice AI can perceive emotional cues but doesn't act on them — a systematic gap between recognition and decision-making that simple prompting can't close.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26083

### Reclaim Evaluation: A Lossy Memory Is Worse Than an empty One
**arXiv:2606.25449** | Memory / Agents | cs.CL, cs.LG

Give a model a memory that kept a wrong conclusion but dropped the work behind it, and it emits that stale value as a confident answer. Give it an empty memory and it abstains. Across seven models this direction never reverses — a clean kill condition.

"Brittle memory": behavioral, not information-bound. Only its magnitude is disposition- and task-dependent, not its direction. Correctability is bottlenecked by whether the answer-determining SOURCE survives — not by capability.

The fix: "source-first policy" (keep the recomputable source, drop the re-derivable conclusion) restores correctability. One-line oracle reaches 1.00; one-prompt deployable version reclaims 0.49-0.88. Stakes compound through memory loops — single dropped-source error corrupts growing span of downstream steps and stays uncorrectable.

Replicates across three deployed memory systems and real dialogue (MultiWOZ). Past budget where source no longer fits, the fix fails silently unless the note records completeness.

**Key insight:** Memory systems should preserve sources, not conclusions. A lossy memory actively misleads; an empty one abstains. Source-first architecture is the correct design.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25449

### PolicyAlign: Direct Policy-Based Safety Alignment
**arXiv:2606.25442** | Safety / Alignment | cs.CL

Problem: real-world safety requirements are often natural-language policies, but supervision data may be costly, delayed, or unavailable. PolicyAlign synthesizes policy-violating instructions and performs on-policy self-distillation to internalize policy-guided behavior. Policy-Sensitive Filtering selects instructions where the policy induces the largest behavioral shift.

Improves safety while maintaining low over-refusal and preserving general capabilities. Generalizes to medical, legal, and financial safety scenarios.

**Key insight:** Policy → behavior without labeled data. Natural-language safety policies can directly drive alignment if you synthesize violations and use policy-sensitivity to filter.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25442

### Multi-Step Tool-Use RL Collapses: Supervisory Signals Fix It
**arXiv:2606.26027** | RL / Tool Use | cs.CL, cs.LG

Agentic RL for tool use often leads to instability or catastrophic collapse (performance abruptly drops, tool-invocation structures fail). Root cause: unexpected probability spikes in control tokens disrupt structured execution, while underlying capability remains intact.

Interleaving SFT with RL substantially improves stability but degrades under format/OOD evaluation. Key finding: diverse supervisory signals (off-policy, hint-based, erroneous example) guide exploratory learning. The paper's Tool-RL-Box framework enables robust training.

**Key insight:** Pure RL for tool use is unstable — interleaving SFT stabilizes but loses OOD generalization. Diverse supervisory signals are the missing ingredient for robust multi-step tool-use agents.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26027

### Brevity is the Soul of Inference Efficiency
**arXiv:2606.25432** | Efficiency / VLMs | cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CV

Standard efficiency toolkit (distillation, pruning, quantization, sparse routing) treats token count as fixed while shrinking per-token cost. Output length has been inflating — the component left untouched.

Argument: brevity is the missing inference-efficiency lever. Pretraining data curation trains models to answer in fewer tokens (lower Cost-of-Pass). VLM curation pipeline applied to MAmmoTH-VL: 35x Cost-of-Pass advantage over most verbose 4B comparator at ~1pp accuracy trade. Concise model even reaches correct answers the verbose reasoning model misses — reasoning is a distinct curation target.

**Key insight:** Inference efficiency is tokens-per-correct-answer, not tokens-per-second. Brevity via data curation is the lever that targets it directly — and reasoning verbosity doesn't always pay off.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25432

### Jailbreak Judge Reliability: Most ASR Numbers Are Unreliable
**arXiv:2606.25487** | Safety / Evaluation | cs.CL, cs.CR

Every paper on LLM jailbreaks reports attack-success rate (ASR) using automated judges — and nobody checks the judges. Using 596 human-labeled completions from HarmBench:

- Dedicated classifier: over-flags (precision 0.835, recall 0.974)
- LLM-as-judge: high precision (0.81-0.94) but erratic recall (0.06-0.65) — same responses produce very different ASR depending on judge
- Surface wrappers flip every LLM-judge between 57-100% of the time
- White-box GCG attack on open classifier weights flips 70% of confident true positives

Recommendation: report judge precision/recall on human-labeled slice, report ASR corrected for judge precision, include adversarial judge checks.

**Key insight:** Most published ASR numbers are unreliable. A large and growing share comes from LLM-judges with unknown calibration — the field needs adversarial robustness checks on evaluation methodology.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25487

### Detect, Unlearn, Restore: Defending Summarization Against Poisoning
**arXiv:2606.26036** | Security / Summarization | cs.CL, cs.CR

Fine-tuning-stage data poisoning in abstractive summarization: adversaries manipulate small task-specific datasets to induce biased/harmful summaries while preserving standard metrics.

Defense framework: white-box detection via influence-function analysis with semantic consistency checks; black-box behavioral auditing via perturbation sensitivity (poisoned models show 2-3x greater sensitivity). Novel attacks for factual distortion and representational bias introduced. 85-92% detection precision; gradient-ascent unlearning restores up to 96% of original behavior with <0.6% ROUGE degradation.

**Key insight:** Fine-tuning poisoning leaves persistent structural artifacts detectable post-hoc — and gradient-ascent unlearning can reverse it without full retraining.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26036

### SpeechEQ: Emotional Intelligence in Voice Conversational Models
**arXiv:2606.25990** | Voice AI / Evaluation | cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.SD

Existing evaluations assess emotional intelligence through isolated text or passive acoustic perception — missing the cross-modal reasoning in active multi-turn dialogue. SpeechEQ: 2,265 dialogues across 15 EQ subscales (grounded in EQ-i 2.0 theory), evaluated via Spoken EQ (SEQ) score.

Current models bottlenecked by: text-reliant "modality shortcut," alignment-induced "safety trap," and "contextual amnesia." End-to-end architectures outperform cascaded systems but still fall short on paralinguistic reasoning.

**Key insight:** Voice models need to reason across modalities in dialogue, not just transcribe + respond. Current benchmarks miss the active, multi-turn paralinguistic reasoning that defines human emotional intelligence.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25990

## 🔍 Key Pattern: Source-First Memory and Voice AI's Perception-Action Gap

Two major themes today:

1. **Memory must preserve sources, not conclusions.** "Reclaim Evaluation" demonstrates that lossy memory (keeping conclusions, dropping sources) is worse than empty memory (abstention). This has profound implications for RAG systems and agent memory architectures — store what can be recomputed, not what was derived.

2. **Voice AI perceives but doesn't act on emotion.** Both 2606.26083 (Voice AI gap) and 2606.25990 (SpeechEQ) reveal the same fundamental problem: models can identify emotional cues but don't weight them in decisions. This is distinct from capability — it's an architectural/alignment gap.
