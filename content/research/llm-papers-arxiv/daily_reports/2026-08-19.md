## New Findings Today (Aug 18–19 Listings)

### 1. Chain-of-Experience: Test-Time Learning via Iterative Experiential Traces
**arXiv:2608.18027** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL
- Introduces Chain-of-Experience (CoE): models accumulate experiential traces through iterative self or environmental feedback to form a continual improvement loop beyond zero-shot inference
- Instantiated with model self-feedback and environmental signals (correctness, public coding test pass rates); evaluated on math, coding, and knowledge domains across 8 LLMs including GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro, Claude-4.5 Sonnet
- Self-feedback alone yields substantial gains with 5.6% overall improvement AND 19% lower API cost vs feedback-free baselines
- Combining complementary feedback channels (model + correctness signals) yields additional gains beyond single channels
- CoE delivers higher accuracy per token than existing test-time strategies; positive correlation between base ability and improvement capacity
- Models robust under weak or spurious feedback; different feedback channels contribute to distinct improvement aspects; most gains emerge early in iterations
- **Key insight:** Test-time learning via accumulated experiential traces is a viable third paradigm alongside zero-shot inference and in-context learning — self-feedback alone is surprisingly effective and cost-efficient
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18027

### 2. TokEval: Principled Tokenizer Evaluation Beyond Fertility
**arXiv:2608.18062** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.LG | COLM 2026
- Tokenizers typically selected with minimal evaluation despite their direct impact on model capabilities
- TokEval: framework of intrinsic tokenizer evaluation metrics capturing linguistically and structurally meaningful properties — UTF-8 character boundary integrity, digit place-value alignment for math, etc.
- Controlled pretraining experiments varying only tokenizer training data mixture, pretokenization strategy, and training algorithm
- Key finding: information-theoretic metrics predict language modeling abilities (Spearman rho up to 0.80); structure-sensitive metrics (digit/line-break handling) correlate with task accuracy
- Enables replacing costly pretraining sweeps with intrinsic measurement where correlation holds
- GitHub: https://github.com/cimeister/tokenizer-intrinsic-evals
- **Key insight:** Tokenizer choice is not just about compression — structural properties like digit alignment and character boundary handling have measurable downstream impact on task accuracy
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18062

### 3. Uncertainty-Guarded LLM Judging with Finite-Sample Risk Guarantees
**arXiv:2608.17994** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL | COLM 2026
- Standard LLM-as-judge approaches lack formal control over false discovery rate among accepted verdicts
- Proposes risk-controlled framework: calibrates uncertainty thresholds using finite-sample Clopper-Pearson intervals so false discovery rate stays below user-specified α with high probability
- Two-threshold routing: parametric mode when confident, retrieval-augmented mode (web evidence + re-evaluation) when not; finite-sample guarantee carries over to routing without additional assumptions
- Maintains target error rate while achieving substantially higher coverage than single-mode baselines
- **Key insight:** LLM judging needs statistical risk control, not just confidence scores — finite-sample guarantees via Clopper-Pearson intervals make deployment-safe judging feasible
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17994

### 4. IOL-AI Challenge: Linguistic Reasoning as Benchmark for Generalizable Intelligence
**arXiv:2608.18011** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL
- International Linguistics Olympiad problems invert standard LLM reasoning benchmarks: solver must first discover the rules before reasoning within them (vs math/code where rules are given)
- 731 submissions from 46 teams under strict compute budget (1× T4, 30 mins); plus 15 unconstrained frontier/open model benchmarks
- Claude Opus 4.8 earns jury score equivalent to gold medal; both resource-constrained submissions score in bottom 5% of human contestants
- Critical finding: capability NOT determined by scale — 14B submissions outperform models twice their size; gains come from decoding/output-handling, not model capacity
- Automatic metrics rank systems exactly as jury does, but compress scale: upscore weak systems by ~13 points, understate strong ones
- Frontier model prior knowledge of problem languages does NOT significantly help solve tasks — linguistic reasoning is genuine generalizable skill proxy
- **Key insight:** Linguistic reasoning (discovering unknown systems) is the hardest and most generalizable reasoning benchmark — 14B models can match 28B+ when decoding is well-optimized
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18011

### 5. BEAR-Bench: Bilingual Enterprise/Academic Reasoning for MLLMs
**arXiv:2608.17895** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.AI
- Bilingual English/Russian benchmark for Multimodal LLMs on text-dense business and scientific documents
- 1000 human-annotated questions; evaluates 16 proprietary and open-weight MLLMs including Gemini 3.1 Pro and Qwen3.5-397B
- Clear headroom remains even for strongest systems — MLLMs still struggle with professional document reasoning
- Paper also compares hallucination detection methods on the failure cases
- **Key insight:** MLLM capability on real-world professional documents substantially lags general VQA — bilingual professional document reasoning is a near-term frontier
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17895

### 6. Decode-Generate-Steer Gap: Decodable ≠ Actionable in Frozen LLMs
**arXiv:2608.17843** (Aug 18, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.AI
- Studies geometric reasoning (CAD constraint satisfaction) in frozen LLMs to audit four properties: linear decodability, forced-choice generation, activation-level influence, behavioral steerability
- Pretraining substantially improves local geometric relation decoding, persisting even after positional cue controls; sketch-level DOF status highly decodable from random initialization with modest pretraining gains
- Critical finding: decodable information is NOT always actionable — generation fails to express decoded information, activation-restoration effects vanish while decodability persists, mean-difference steering does not reliably control outputs
- **Key insight:** Interpretability probes measuring decodability are insufficient evidence for behavioral steerability — the decode/generate/steer pipeline can break at multiple points independently
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17843
