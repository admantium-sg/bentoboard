## Part 1: ICML 2026 — LLM Reasoning Papers

### 🧠 Reasoning Theory & Chain-of-Thought

**A Formal Comparison Between CoT and Latent Thought (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2509.25239)**
- First formal computational complexity comparison between Chain-of-Thought and Latent Thought (Looped Transformer / Coconut)
- Key findings: (1) Latent Thought strictly reaches TC^k at polylogarithmic depth; CoT reaches at most TC^(k-1) — strict separation in deterministic setting. (2) In probabilistic setting, CoT can support FPRAS counting via stochastic decoding, surpassing deterministic Latent Thought
- Practical implication: For depth-driven recursive problems (multi-step math, logical chains), Latent Thought has a complexity advantage. For counting/probability problems, CoT's stochastic decoding is more powerful
- Code: https://github.com/kevin671/cot-vs-loop
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25239

**Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in the Wild Is Not Always Faithful (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2503.08679)**
- First large-scale study of CoT unfaithfulness under NON-adversarial, natural prompts (no injected bias, no edited outputs)
- Two unfaithful behaviors detected: (1) Implicit Post-hoc Rationalization (IPHR) — contradictory but plausible arguments for logically opposing comparative question pairs; (2) Unfaithful Irlogical Shortcuts (UIS) — skipping critical steps in math proofs yet still reaching correct answers
- Unfaithfulness rates: production models up to 13%; DeepSeek R1: 0.37%; Claude 3.7 Sonnet thinking: 0.04% (still not zero!)
- Method: logical anti-symmetry of comparative questions + PutnamBench math deconstruction — no internal access needed
- Code: https://github.com/jettjaniak/chainscope
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08679

**Are Large Reasoning Models Interruptible? (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2510.11713)**
- Evaluates whether reasoning models can handle mid-generation interruptions and updates (not just static problem-solving)
- Three consistent failure modes identified: (1) Reasoning leakage — interrupted model generates partial answer without completing trace; (2) Panic answering — model rushes to provide answer when interrupted; (3) Self-doubt — model loses confidence/derails after interruption
- Finding: Models exhibit anytime-algorithm properties (more reasoning = better answers under hard interruption), but struggle with mid-generation context updates
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11713

**Biases in the Blind Spot: Detecting What LLMs Fail to Mention (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2602.10117)**
- Automated black-box pipeline to detect "unverbalized biases" — implicit factors that influence decisions but never appear in CoT
- Uses LLM-generated candidate concepts + counterfactual input variants + McNemar's test + sequential statistical early stopping
- Found known biases (gender, race) AND novel ones: Spanish fluency, English proficiency, writing formality — none verbalized in CoT
- Key insight: Reading CoT is insufficient for auditing — models use information they never mention
- Code: https://github.com/FlyingPumba/biases-in-the-blind-spot/
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10117

### ⚡ Efficiency & Test-Time Compute

**Beyond Test-Time Memory: State-Space Optimal Control for LLM Reasoning / TTC-Net (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2603.09221)**
- Replaces memory-based next-token prediction with LQR (Linear Quadratic Regulator) planning in latent space
- TTC layer inserted every 8 attention layers; solves finite-horizon LQR problem, decoded as next-token representation
- Results: +27.8% on MATH-500; 2-3× increase in Pass@8 on AMC/AIME; CUDA-efficient via Symplectic Iteration solver
- Key insight: Memory architectures (attention, SSMs) are System 1. LQR planning architectures are System 2 — RL cannot bridge this gap; architecture can
- Project: https://vita-group.github.io/TTC-Net
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09221

**Aligning Tree-Search Policies with Fixed Token Budgets / BG-MCTS (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2602.09574)**
- Budget-Guided MCTS addresses the disconnect between tree search strategy and fixed deployment budgets
- Introduces budget sufficiency ratio ρ = 1 - C_used/B as sole scheduling signal; search transitions from broad exploration (ρ near 1) to deep refinement (ρ near 0)
- Solves two failure modes: late over-branching (starting new branches when budget is nearly exhausted) and premature termination (leaving budget unused)
- Consistently outperforms budget-agnostic baselines on math and physics benchmarks
- Code: https://github.com/Sora-Miyamoto/bg-mcts
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09574

### 🔧 Training & Credit Assignment

**Attention Illuminates LLM Reasoning: Preplan-and-Anchor Rhythm (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2510.13554)**
- Discovers that attention dynamics reveal a stable "preplan-and-anchor" two-beat rhythm during reasoning
- Two metrics: WAAD (local look-back distance) and FAI (frequency of being visited by future attention) characterize this rhythm
- Converts these into token-level advantage amplification coefficients for GRPO — concentrates credit on "critical tokens" that dictate downstream reasoning
- Consistent gains across Countdown, QA, and mathematical reasoning benchmarks
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13554

**Beyond Two-Stage Training: BRIDGE — Cooperative SFT and RL (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2509.06948)**
- Models SFT and RL integration as bilevel optimization (Stackelberg game), not sequential pipeline
- SFT upper-level teacher learns to selectively transfer beneficial supervisory signals to RL student via lightweight LoRA
- Average +3 percentage points across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks; outperforms both pure SFT→RL pipeline AND naive hybrid gradient methods
- Key insight: Not all SFT updates help RL. Meta-learning which updates to keep is the key
- Code: https://github.com/ChanLiang/BRIDGE
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06948

### 🎯 Calibration & Uncertainty

**Calibration of Structured Ignorance Certificates / SIC (ICML 2026 | arXiv:2606.08571)**
- Proposes Structured Ignorance Certificate (SIC) — JSON output format requiring model to state which domain intersection is missing, which concepts are required, and what to retrieve — instead of hallucinating
- 7,347 automatically synthesized cross-domain "Unknown Unknown" (UU) problems; GRPO fine-tuning on 14B model
- Results: 99.46% JSON validity, 0.967 concept specificity — model learns to stably produce certificates for out-of-distribution problems
- Key insight: Unknown unknowns are dangerous but learnable — structure ignorance as a skill, not just abstention
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08571

## Key Themes from ICML 2026 Day 1

1. **CoT faithfulness is a live problem:** The 13% unfaithfulness rate under natural prompts, and the fact that even R1 and Sonnet thinking show 0.04-0.37% unfaithfulness, means CoT cannot be treated as a reliable introspection window. This has serious implications for AI safety auditing.

2. **System 2 reasoning needs architectural support:** TTC-Net's LQR planning layer (+27.8% MATH-500) and the CoT vs Latent Thought theoretical separation both suggest that memory-based architectures have a ceiling for deliberate reasoning. Planning must be baked into the architecture.

3. **Credit assignment is getting finer-grained:** Attention dynamics (WAAD/FAI) revealing a preplan-and-anchor rhythm that GRPO can exploit is a significant step toward token-level RL credit assignment — replacing the blunt sequence-level 0/1 reward.

4. **Unknown unknowns become structured certificates:** SIC is a practical approach to making models admit what they don't know in an actionable, machine-readable format. 99.46% JSON validity is production-ready.

5. **CoT is not the only game in town:** BG-MCTS (token budget-aware tree search), BRIDGE (cooperative SFT+RL), and the CoT vs Latent Thought comparison all point to a field broadening beyond simple chain-of-thought prompting.
