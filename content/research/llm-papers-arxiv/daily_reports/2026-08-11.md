## New Papers

### Steerling-8B: Interpretability as Training Constraint — arXiv:2608.07594
Interpretability is recast as a training constraint rather than post-hoc analysis. Across three orders of magnitude of compute, representations become MORE disentangled and human-aligned with scale — challenging the assumption that interpretability taxes capability. Steerling-8B is a diffusion LM with causal attention that attributes outputs to input tokens, concepts, and training data — enabling closed-loop intervention without retraining. Competitive with models trained on 2-16x more compute. Key insight: interpretability can be designed into training, and it improves with scale rather than against it.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07594

### Consilience: Verifier-Free Test-Time Scaling — arXiv:2608.09898
Existing confidence-based VF-TTS methods catastrophically break down on complex tasks: uniformly high confidence indicates failure to explore, favoring confidently wrong answers. Consilience introduces a trajectory-based selection framework that penalizes high initial confidence while demanding final certainty. Achieves new SOTA on graduate-level math and code generation. Key insight: robust cognitive search requires low initial confidence (exploration) converging to high final confidence — not uniform confidence throughout.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09898

### Thinking Hard, Not Smart: Reasoning Models Fail Budget Allocation — arXiv:2608.07968
Reasoning models fail to ration test-time compute strategically across questions. They prioritize by presentation order, front-load effort on early questions, and remain insensitive to value/difficulty. Explicit planning spreads compute more evenly but doesn't produce value-aware prioritization. This is a distinct capability gap not captured by per-question evaluation. Key insight: exam-style budget allocation is a separate skill from solving individual problems.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07968

### SWE-Bench ProMax: Harder Code Refactoring Benchmark — arXiv:2608.09802
Code refactoring requires coordinated behavior-preserving changes across many files — a substantially harder and more realistic agent test than existing benchmarks. 170 instances across 7 languages, avg 11.4 files modified per task. Addresses prior benchmark quality issues (60% of unsolved SWE-bench Verified instances have flawed tests). Best model achieves only 41.2% resolve rate — confirming meaningful unsolved challenge. Published at COLM 2026.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09802

### Thinking Mode Fusion: Training Dynamics — arXiv:2608.09893
Unified thinking/non-thinking mode training reveals asymmetric interaction: increasing non-thinking supervision reduces thinking mode accuracy. The optimal training schedule depends on data ratio. Negative correlation between non-thinking and thinking mode supervision highlights inherent tension. Practical guidance for designing TMF training settings. ACL SRW 2026.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09893

### NeuPAT: Language-Preserving MLLM Tuning — arXiv:2608.08107
Multimodal expansion often degrades language capabilities. NeuPAT allocates neuron-wise update constraints during instruction tuning — protecting language-sensitive neurons while promoting multimodal adaptation. Recovers 94.5% of language capability degradation across diverse LLM families while maintaining multimodal performance. Architecture-agnostic and lightweight.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08107

### UniHall: Hallucination Fuzzing for MLLMs — arXiv:2608.07525
Self-adaptive multimodal fuzzing (SAMF) finds hallucinations static benchmarks miss. Reveals dissociation between reasoning capability and factual grounding. Key finding: RL alignment inadvertently exacerbates sycophancy in instruction-following tasks — a helpfulness-hallucination trade-off. Comprehensive fine-grained taxonomy covering Object, Instruction, and Knowledge dimensions.

Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07525
