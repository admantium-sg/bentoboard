# Daily Report — 2026-09-06

## New Insights Summary

### 1. ESPO: Error-Structured Prompt Optimization (EMNLP 2026)
Evolutionary prompt optimizers like GEPA suffer from "prompt bloat" — prompts up to 3× longer yet no more accurate. ESPO decomposes optimization into Diagnose (cluster errors into structural patterns), Propose (four complementary strategies), and Select (bootstrap stability). On 7 NLP benchmarks: +3.76pp accuracy over GEPA (74.67% vs 70.91%), with 47% shorter prompts. Standout result: Qwen3 GSM8K jumps from 15% to 91.40%. Key finding: diversity without bootstrap selection actually hurts performance by 1.20%.

### 2. Compile by Training: NL Specs → Reusable Neural Functions (EMNLP Demo)
Turns natural-language specifications into reusable neural functions. Teacher models generate task-specific examples → train small adapter for compact interpreter. Result runs without teachers and can be stored/versioned like software. On FuzzyBench-Hard: 83.6% semantic accuracy where fast compiler produces no exact matches. Higher accuracy with higher compile-time cost (~1 min vs seconds). Deployed in interactive service for website helpers, 3D avatars, English-Claudish translation.

### 3. Representational Alignment for Adversarial-Robust Safety (EMNLP 2026)
Standard behavioral alignment leaves moral categorization structure largely unchanged; adversarial robustness gaps persist. Representational Similarity Optimization (RSO) directly aligns latent representations with human moral judgments WITHOUT supervising outputs. Across 23 LLMs: models fail to distinguish opposed moral categories or preserve fine-grained typicality. RSO improves adversarial robustness but at cost of explicit judgment accuracy. Key insight: prototype-based categorization is weakly preserved in current LLMs — and transferring this principle yields generalizable safety under adversarial conditions.

### 4. Beyond Shallow Alignment: Refusal Circuits Are Fragile (EMNLP 2026)
Comparing SFT, reasoning-augmented fine-tuning (RaFT), and ORPO across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, Qwen3-8B: training method reshapes internal refusal computation. No method achieves all three desired properties: refusal not concentrated in fragile components, safety without capability cost, and correctable via targeted edits. Caution against treating current post-training as reliable defense for security-critical use.

### 5. Multi-Perspective Hallucination Detection: Single-Pass Benchmarks Undercount Errors (EMNLP 2026)
Single-pass hallucination benchmarks achieve scale at cost of undercounting factual errors. Multi-perspective adjudication (first-pass + LLM-as-Judge + medical-expert + evidence-based) reveals: first-pass annotators frequently miss errors caught by adjudicators; LaJ misses errors annotators catch. Disagreement among adjudicators means no single source is sufficient. Applied to existing benchmark, same pattern of missing annotations emerges.
