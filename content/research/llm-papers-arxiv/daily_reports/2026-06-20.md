# Daily Report — June 20, 2026 (Saturday)

## New Findings

### 1. GEMS: Multi-Semantic Steering Without Collapse
**arXiv:2606.19946** (June 18, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.LG

Training-free activation steering enables single-direction behavior injection but breaks when multiple semantic directions are superposed. GEMS identifies two independent collapse sources:

- **Distributional deviation**: Additive perturbations accumulate in norm across layers, driving activations outside the training distribution
- **Directional interference**: Non-orthogonal semantic vectors mutually dampen when superposed

GEMS maps each source to a geometric constraint: norm-preserving weighted superposition + real-time orthogonalization. On GSM8K, injecting three concurrent non-mathematical directions preserves accuracy at 98% (baseline 92%), while unconstrained addition collapses to 4%. On Wikitext-2, same injection incurs only 2.2% PPL increase. Layer-level probes confirm orthogonalized signals survive the FFN pathway. Qualitative transfer from 3B to 31B across architectures.

Key insight: Multi-directional steering is solvable — orthogonalization is the missing ingredient.

### 2. H-RePlan: Hierarchical Recovery for Cross-Device Agents
**arXiv:2606.20487** (June 18, 2026) — cs.CL

Real-world computer-use tasks span multiple applications and devices. Existing multi-device agents recover coarsely — retry same strategy, reassign subtask, or revise global plan. H-RePlan separates device-local strategy recovery from orchestrator-level global replanning via a compact cross-layer failure abstraction. Each device has interchangeable execution strategies. HeraBench injects strategy- and device-level failures across Linux and Android workflows. Substantially outperforms single-strategy and coarse-grained baselines with higher completion, instruction adherence, and perfect-pass rates at lower token cost.

Key insight: Scope-aware hierarchical recovery is essential for robust multi-device agent execution.

### 3. BitsMoE: Spectral Energy-Guided Bit Allocation for MoE Quantization
**arXiv:2606.19468** (June 17, 2026) — cs.LG

Standard quantization allocates bits uniformly across MoE experts — ignores that different experts have different importance and sensitivity. BitsMoE uses spectral energy to guide per-expert bit allocation, optimizing the precision budget based on actual expert contributions.

Key insight: MoE models need heterogeneous quantization — uniform allocation wastes bits on less critical experts.

### 4. StylisticBias: 15 Attributes Account for 80% of MLLM Social Bias
**arXiv:2606.20527** (June 18, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.CV (ICML 2026 Workshop)

Controlled benchmark with 500 photorealistic base faces, ~50 single-attribute variations each (~25K images total). Keeps identity fixed, varies one visual attribute at a time. Key findings: age and body type dominate identity-level effects; fashion style drives largest attribute-level shifts. About 15 attributes account for nearly 80% of total variation in social judgments. Sensitivity is strongest for socioeconomic and style-related judgments semantically aligned with appearance.

Key insight: MLLM social bias is concentrated in a small set of visual cues — targeted mitigation is feasible.

### 5. NarraDolma: Narrative Structure in Pretraining Data
**arXiv:2606.19468** (June 17, 2026) — cs.CL

First fine-grained study of narrative features (agency, setting, events — 11 dimensions) in a 3-trillion-token pretraining corpus (Dolma). NarraBERT: RoBERTa-based model for narrative prediction, applied to 3M passages. Key findings: narrative structure is measurable at scale; narrative qualities are unequally distributed across sources and topics in ways current curation practices neither measure nor account for.

Key insight: Narrative reasoning in LLMs depends on how narrative qualities are distributed in pretraining data — this distribution is now auditable.
