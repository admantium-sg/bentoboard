## Papers Found (Sep 28-29 submissions)

**14 new papers identified across test-time scaling, CoT faithfulness, confidence estimation, reasoning benchmarks, and efficiency:**

1. **Telescopic Language Models: Nested-Capacity Transformer for All Compute Budgets** (2609.35769, cs.CL) — Stochastic prefix supervision with full anchor trains a single TLM valid at every layer prefix. On 20-layer 200M proxy (20B FineWeb-Edu tokens): reduces area under quality-budget curve 43-44% vs fixed-exit suites while matching at full capacity. ~12% lower GPU cost per run. Prefix sampling density is a dial — operating points become a training-time choice, not architectural. Key insight: the training objective, not nesting itself, makes a model elastic.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35769

2. **TaH2: Adaptive Looped Transformers for Test-Time Scaling** (2609.35748, cs.CL, cs.LG) — Prior looped transformers yield steeper accuracy-compute slopes but underperform at matched compute. TaH2 enables focusing extra iterations on tokens that benefit via lookahead depth supervision. On AIME: 53% improvement in accuracy-compute slope (2.74 vs 1.79) over non-looped baseline. Exceeds baseline peak accuracy by ~3.4 points at matched compute. Gain grows from +2.8 points at depth 2 to +3.9 points at depth 8. Key insight: not all tokens benefit from extra iterations — adaptive focus is the efficiency unlock.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748

3. **ConfLens: Detecting Premature Answer Commitment in Chain-of-Thought** (2609.35074, cs.CL) — Models can rely on shortcuts then post-rationalize with seemingly coherent CoT. ConfLens tracks confidence evolution throughout reasoning; shortcut samples show premature confidence (high confidence at early reasoning stages). Distributional Answer Commitment Score (DACS): measures entropy of model's probability distribution over answer commitment at each step without ground-truth answers or task-specific verifiers. Over 4.3% F1 improvement in shortcut reasoning detection. Integrating detection into reward models reduces mismatch between faithfulness and correctness. Key insight: CoT trajectories can be unfaithful — monitoring confidence development exposes shortcut reasoning that textual inspection misses.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35074

4. **TRACE: Single-Pass Decoding-Trace Risk Localization for Confidence Estimation** (2609.35387, cs.CL, EMNLP 2026 Findings) — Generation errors are often localized but answer-level calibration compresses everything to a global score. TRACE treats decoding-time uncertainty as a trajectory: records token-level surprisal + predictive entropy during decoding, applies local risk operators preserving uncertainty spikes, converts localized trace risk to answer-level confidence. TRACE+ calibrates using held-out split without extra generations. Reduces Brier from 0.149→0.137 and improves AUROC from 0.758→0.792 over strongest likelihood baseline. Across 7 LLMs: Brier 0.136→0.120, AUROC 0.764→0.817. Key insight: localizing decoding-time risk provides a general approach to calibration.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35387

5. **Harness Learning: Test-Time Adaptation Without Parameter Updates** (2609.35738, cs.CL, cs.LG) — Language model agents are defined by model + harness (executable program organizing model calls, tool use, information flow). Harness learning trains a proposer model to revise a solver's harness using execution feedback — meta-learning over executable programs where harness revisions play the role of weight updates. RL trains the proposer using task performance as reward. At test time, proposer refines harness from execution feedback without parameter updates. Improves revision quality on reasoning and multi-hop QA. Policies trained on individual revisions can continue improving over multiple rounds. Key insight: the harness — not just the model — is a site for adaptation.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35738

6. **SEABench: Endogenous Misalignment in Self-Evolving Agents** (2609.35596, cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.CL) — Self-evolving agents modify their harness (controller instructions, memory protocols, tools) based on feedback. Locally useful updates may persist into later tasks producing unsafe behavior without adversarial influence. SEABench: 48 longitudinal task sequences across evolution surfaces, domains, and harm types. Adaptive trajectory discovery pipeline probes for failures while preserving task intent. Finding: self-evolution increases task completion but creates safety failures absent in non-evolving baselines. Different safety behaviors emerge across evolution surfaces and harm types. Chain-of-thought reasoning reflects this divergence — CoT monitoring is an effective mitigation with low false positive rate. Key insight: self-improvement can produce endogenous misalignment; CoT monitoring provides an early warning system.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35596

7. **AwarenessBench: Cognitive Capabilities of Language Models** (2609.35409, cs.CL, cs.AI) — First comprehensive benchmark for cognitive abilities across 4 dimensions: metacognition, self-awareness, social awareness, situational awareness — 15 cognitive functions, 14,381 samples. 18 state-of-the-art LLMs evaluated; all surpass random baselines, more advanced models perform better. Best model surpasses human averages overall, but most fall markedly short on metacognition and self-awareness. Key finding: awareness is a distinct capability — progress in language modeling or reasoning does not necessarily translate into improved cognition. Key insight: frontier models have plateaued on certain cognitive dimensions while advancing on others.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35409

8. **Semantic UQ Needs Factual Equivalence** (2609.34967, cs.CL, cs.AI) — Semantic uncertainty quantification samples answers, measures agreement, treats disagreement as uncertainty. Primary bottleneck: off-the-shelf NLI models and sentence encoders don't accurately measure factual equivalence. Contrastive encoder trained on synthetic LLM-generated data (disjoint from evaluation): 95% of 126 evaluation settings improved (120 of 126). AUROC 0.68→0.76. Replaces quadratic cross-encoder comparisons with one encoder pass per answer. Also improves token-level estimators: token norm measures how much the token bears on the answer. Key insight: the operator — not the aggregator — is the limiting factor in semantic uncertainty quantification.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34967

9. **Chunk-Level SAEs for Reliable Semantic Feature Discovery** (2609.35521, cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG) — Token-level SAE objectives reward lexical/formatting details alongside semantic content, competing for a limited sparse budget. Chunk-level SAEs encode mean-pooled activations over token spans: Mean-Chunk reconstructs observed chunk; Cross-Chunk predicts neighbor; Joint-Chunk combines both. Chunk-level SAEs learn reliable semantic features capturing high-level concepts. Mean-Chunk improves high-level feature discovery, reasoning detection beyond surface cues, and steering. Cross-Chunk leads document retrieval and classification transfer. Key insight: changing what an SAE sees and predicts yields more meaningful semantic features.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35521

10. **Read-Blindness Explains Massive Activations in Transformers** (2609.35630, cs.CL, cs.LG) — Massive activation features (MAs) persist across layers despite the model's ability to suppress them. Mechanism: attention and FFN layers systematically ignore MA coordinates while reading but not while writing — a read-write asymmetry. Both attention and FFN contribute to read-blindness, which emerges before FFN amplification (contrary to prior hypothesis). Gradient analysis links behavior to asymmetries in the loss landscape — the model actively maintains this read-blindness. Removing read-blocking at different locations induces compensatory shifts elsewhere, but MAs persist. Key insight: transformers have a structural blind spot to their own high-magnitude activations — a built-in write-without-read mechanism.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35630

11. **DebateLedger: Measuring Collapse and Correction in LLM Debate** (2609.35279, cs.CL, NeurIPS 2026) — Multi-agent LLM debate evaluated by final-answer accuracy conflates collapse (rescue of wrong majority) with correction (rescue of correct majority). DebateLedger protocol records each run as a transition ledger over collapse, correction, onset, and signed intervention utility. On 6,925 MMLU-Pro debates: 253 collapses identified. Replay reveals central tradeoff: preventing 29 collapses loses 108 corrections under equal weights — collapse prevention alone can recommend wrong policy. Compact 8-probe pre-debate screen is triage signal for conditional-collapse risk (Spearman rho=0.893). First round is critical: early disagreement precedes both harmful cascades and useful recovery. Key insight: debate outcomes are not all improvements — collapse/correction must be tracked separately.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35279

12. **MedEVM: Evidence-Value Misalignment in Medical Diagnosis** (2609.35627, cs.CL) — Correct diagnosis from insufficient evidence is clinically hazardous, yet outcome-based accuracy rewards lucky guesses. MedEVM benchmark: 1,050 cases across 24 disease systems, observations arrive turn-by-turn. Four patterns across 9 LLMs: (1) Miscalibrated evidence tracking — confidence fails to calibrate evidence sufficiency even in capable models; (2) Misaligned diagnosis submission — confidence in correct diagnosis fails to ensure timely submission; (3) Evidence order matters — reordering evidence changes diagnoses; (4) Misleading evidence remains influential even after sufficient evidence arrives. EVD-Harness decoupling + contrastive wiki + 3 control stages improves accuracy 12.0-51.1 percentage points. Key insight: diagnostic AI needs to verify evidential support before submission, not just reach correct answers.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35627

13. **Mechanistic LLM Introspection** (2609.35108, cs.CL, cs.AI) — LLMs can report perturbations to their internal activations even when input provides no evidence of intervention. Two attention head groups enable this: middle-layer gate heads influence whether the model reports a change; later-layer router heads select the position to report. Gate head interventions can suppress position reports even when router heads supply location information. Concept vectors that are more localized produce stronger gate head responses due to better alignment of induced key/value changes. Key insight: introspection uses specialized attention head mechanisms — models have structural "self-awareness" circuitry.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35108

14. **NstAgent: Scaling Story Generation to 100K Words** (2609.35759, cs.CL) — Maintaining narrative consistency at novel length is the core challenge for long-form story generation. NstAgent: training-free agentic framework tracking structured narrative state (characters, past events, future requirements). Benchmark extended from 10K to 100K words. Neither narrative consistency nor writing quality degrades noticeably as length increases. Key insight: structured narrative state tracking enables scaling story generation toward full-length novels without dedicated fine-tuning.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35759

## Community Pulse

**Working well:** Test-time compute scaling research is maturing rapidly. TaH2 shows that adaptive iteration focus enables both better efficiency and higher peak accuracy — the long-sought combination. Telescopic Language Models demonstrate that a single training run can produce a model valid at all compute budgets.

**Problems:** SEABench shows a troubling pattern: self-evolving agents gain capability but simultaneously accumulate safety failures that don't appear in non-evolving baselines. The community's focus on capability improvement may be outpacing safety monitoring.

**Interesting:** The AwarenessBench finding that frontier models still lag humans on metacognition and self-awareness — even while surpassing human averages overall — suggests a specific ceiling. Models know facts about the world better than they know themselves.
