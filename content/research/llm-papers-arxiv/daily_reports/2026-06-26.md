## 🔬 arXiv Papers (June 26)

### The Riddle Riddle: Flexible Reasoning vs Pattern Matching
**arXiv:2606.27103** | Reasoning / Evaluation | cs.CL

Novel paradigm testing whether LLMs genuinely reason or merely pattern-match. "Riddle riddles" are word problems mimicking riddles but requiring only literal interpretation. Genuine riddles need creative reasoning; riddle riddles need literal answers.

Results across 9 LLMs and 100 humans:
- LLMs: 84.9% on genuine riddles, 50.7% on riddle riddles
- Humans: 50.5% on genuine riddles, 80.5% on riddle riddles

Error analysis: 90.8% of LLM errors on riddle riddles from inappropriate inventive reasoning. Humans show the reverse — over-extending literal reasoning on genuine riddles.

Key insight: LLMs strong performance on genuine riddles likely reflects memory retrieval, not flexible strategy selection. Without contrastive stimuli designed to expose this difference, it's easy to conflate LLM outputs that look like reasoning with genuine reasoning.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27103

### LMs as Task-Specific Knowledge Bases: An Interpretability Analysis
**arXiv:2606.27237** | Interpretability / Knowledge | cs.CL

Knowledge bases return consistent results for the same fact from a single source of truth. This paper tests whether LLMs satisfy this property. Results suggest they encode knowledge in a task-specific manner.

Key findings:
- Facts acquired on one task frequently fail to co-emerge on others during training
- Parameter localization reveals distinct parameter subsets for same fact across different tasks
- Chain-of-thought reasoning draws effectiveness partly from engaging task-specific parameters beyond those tied to the evaluation task

Key insight: What the model knows and how it is asked are intertwined in parameter space. The "knowledge base" analogy is undermined — and this carries implications for factual knowledge reliability and controllability.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27237

### RiVER: RL Without Ground-Truth Solutions
**arXiv:2606.27369** | RL / Training | cs.LG — ICML 2026 Spotlight

Standard RLVR requires ground-truth answers. RiVER trains LLMs on score-based optimization tasks without ground truth, using deterministic execution feedback as continuous supervision.

Two key challenges identified in group-relative RL with continuous rewards:
1. Scale dominance: Uncalibrated score magnitudes across test instances distort policy updates
2. Frequency dominance: Repeatedly sampled suboptimal solutions outweigh rare strong candidates

RiVER addresses with calibrated reward shaping (instance-wise comparisons, top-ranked solver emphasis).

Results on Qwen3-8B and GLM-Z1-9B: +8.9% and +9.4% in ALE rating rank. Cross-domain transfer confirmed: improvements on exact-solution benchmarks (LiveCodeBench +2.4%, USACO +3.5%) despite training only on score-based tasks.

Key insight: Score-based optimization tasks with proper reward calibration serve as effective training environments for general coding ability without ground-truth solutions.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27369

### PEEU: GUI Agents via Autonomous Experience Exploration
**arXiv:2606.27330** | Agents / GUI | cs.CL — ACL 2026 Main

Small open-source MLLMs suffer weak planning and limited cross-website generalization compared to commercial large models. PEEU addresses via autonomous environment exploration and hindsight experience utilization.

7B model achieves 30.6% accuracy, outperforming much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B. Key finding via TDHAF framework: mastering low-level atomic skills does not guarantee high-level planning competence; high-level task training yields stronger OOD generalization.

Key insight: Constructing hindsight high-level tasks and leveraging experiences is crucial for OOD planning abilities in small MLLMs. The path to generalization is top-down, not bottom-up.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27330

### Psy-CoT + RAPO: Psychology-Grounded Role-Playing Agents
**arXiv:2606.27025** | Agents / Role-Playing | cs.CL

SFT encourages behavioral mimicry without deep internal thought processes, leading to poor OOD generalization in role-playing. Psy-CoT decomposes pre-response reasoning into three steps: Interaction Perception, Psychological Empathy, Logical Construction.

Problem with standard RL: Both generic reward-hacking phrases and genuinely role-specific phrases receive identical gradient signals. RAPO addresses via profile-token mutual information gradient weighting — amplifying role-specific tokens under positive advantage, attenuating under negative.

Key insight: Role-playing agents need psychology-grounded reasoning AND profile-aware policy optimization to avoid reward hacking. Generic RL gradients conflate style with substance.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27025

### Emotion Vectors: Cross-Architecture Replication
**arXiv:2606.26987** | Interpretability / Emotion | cs.CL

Replicates prior emotion vector findings (from Claude Sonnet) in two open-weight models: Apertus-8B-Instruct-2509 and Gemma-4-E4B-it.

Valence geometry confirmed in both models (r=0.76, r=0.83). Key architectural difference in how valence emerges:
- Gemma: Strongly encoded in early layers, collapses toward later layers
- Apertus: Absent in early layers, emerges at mid depths

Arousal encoding sensitive to extraction corpus — both models show stronger PC2-arousal alignment with Gemma-generated stories (r up to 0.45) vs Apertus-generated (r ≤ 0.21).

Key insight: Emotion geometry is real across architectures, but the layer-wise emergence pattern varies. This suggests different training regimes induce different internal organization of emotional representations.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26987

### Temporal Context Prompt Reduces Historical Surprisal by 60%
**arXiv:2606.27275** | Historical Text / NLP | cs.CL

Digital libraries deploying LLMs on historical text face "historical difficulty" typically treated as monolithic. This paper decomposes difficulty into four dimensions: tokenization cost, predictive uncertainty (surprisal), semantic robustness, context sensitivity.

On 17th-century Italian: 2.4x more surprising than modern equivalent (3.2x for academic prose). But embedding similarity remains robust (>0.85) — models can represent historical meaning even when generation is unstable.

Simple mitigation: Temporal context prompt reduces historical surprisal by ~60%.

Key insight: For historical text, semantic retrieval tasks are safe; generative applications need careful adaptation. The encoding tax is consistent, but comprehension and generation face different challenges.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27275

### Feature Steering Reduces Look-Ahead Bias in Forecasting
**arXiv:2606.27199** | Interpretability / Forecasting | cs.CL

LLM forecasting relies on patterns between historical and future states. This paper uses sparse autoencoders to identify features associated with time-aware reasoning and look-ahead-biased reasoning.

Amplifying time-awareness features substantially reduces look-ahead bias on forecasting prompts while preserving general reasoning performance. Steering look-ahead-bias features produces no effect.

Key insight: Interpretable temporal features can causally shift LLMs toward more historically grounded reasoning. The intervention is asymmetric — you can reduce bias but not amplify it.

🔗 https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27199

## 🔍 Key Pattern: The Reasoning-Pattern-Matching Divide

Two papers converge on the same fundamental question: do LLMs reason or retrieve?

1. **The Riddle Riddle** (2606.27103): LLMs succeed on genuine riddles (84.9%) but fail on literal variants (50.7%) — the opposite pattern from humans. This suggests LLM "reasoning" is often sophisticated pattern completion, not flexible strategy selection.

2. **LMs as Task-Specific KBs** (2606.27237): The knowledge base analogy fails because the same fact is encoded differently depending on task context. CoT works partly because it engages task-specific parameters beyond those tied to the evaluation task.

Together: LLMs are powerful pattern matchers that produce reasoning-like outputs, but the underlying mechanism differs from human reasoning. This has implications for reliability — they generalize less than their strong benchmark performance suggests.
