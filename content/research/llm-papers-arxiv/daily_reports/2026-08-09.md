### DreamGuard: Proactive Runtime Guardrail for LLM Agents
**arXiv:2608.05695** (August 6, 2026) — cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.CR
- Proposes DreamGuard: proactive guardrail built around a risk-aware world model
- World model maintains compact recurrent latent state over trajectory; predicts future latent states for immediate-hazard and prefix-risk evidence
- Fuses multi-horizon risk signals into intervention decisions before execution
- Outperforms generic, reactive, and proactive guardrail baselines across 4 benchmarks
- Achieves best safety-utility trade-off; 25ms average end-to-end latency per call
- Key insight: Reactive guardrails miss long-horizon risks where individually benign actions drift toward hazardous states — explicit world modeling is the fix
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05695

### DiagChain: Evidence-Grounded Attack Chain Reconstruction
**arXiv:2608.03591** (August 4, 2026) — cs.CR, cs.AI
- Diagnostic benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on evidence-grounded attack chain reconstruction
- MAIN-69: 69 scenarios across multiple OS types, evidence noise levels, and chain lengths
- Introduces ECRAG (Evidence-Centric Retrieval-Augmented Generation) coupling evidence retrieval with evolving structured chain representation
- Five complementary metrics for stage-wise evaluation and systematic failure diagnosis
- Best configuration succeeds on only 39.6% of 849 reference steps — even SOTA models struggle significantly
- Smaller models fail to incorporate retrieved evidence; larger models fail to order evidence correctly
- Key insight: Diagnostic evaluation beyond end-to-end accuracy reveals fundamentally different failure modes by model scale
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03591

### AgentHPOBench: LLM Agents as Sequential Hyperparameter Optimizers
**arXiv:2607.29626** (July 31, 2026) — cs.AI
- 30 executable ML tasks across 7 research categories; agents perform sequential interventions based on accumulated configs, metrics, and logs
- 12 widely-used agents + conventional HPO baselines evaluated under unified protocol
- Current agents show measurable experimental optimization ability but face clear limitations in: sustained iterative refinement, complex log diagnosis, and consistent progress toward reference performance
- Key insight: Evaluating agents as scientific experimenters requires sequential, evidence-interpretive benchmarks — static code generation benchmarks miss this entirely
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29626

### FriendBench: Dyadic Familiarity Inference
**arXiv:2607.29602** (July 31, 2026) — cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.HC
- Benchmark: infer whether two people are familiar or strangers from 20-second dyadic ice-breaker video clips
- 96 balanced dyads; compare 26 MLLMs from 7 companies against matched human panels across text, audio, and video
- Best model statistically indistinguishable from human crowd on accuracy in every modality — but reaches it differently
- Humans stay balanced between "stranger" and "familiar" answers; strongest models lean toward "stranger" — a difference in effective prior, not discrimination
- Richer modalities help both humans and models unequally; only humans gain from visible behavior on top of speech
- Key insight: Human-level accuracy can mask fundamentally different reasoning strategies — priors matter as much as discriminative ability
- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29602

## Quantization Landscape Update
A comprehensive 2026 quantization quality benchmarks study consolidates findings across GGUF, MLX, AWQ, and GPTQ formats:

- Q4_K_M: 1-3% perplexity degradation vs FP16 — practical production sweet spot for most use cases
- Q3_K_M: 3-8% perplexity degradation; reasoning benchmarks drop ~5% (3x the perplexity loss) — not recommended for agent work
- Q2_K: 11-14% perplexity degradation — makes aggressive low-bit CPU inference viable but quality suffers significantly
- Q5/Q6: less than 1% improvement over Q4 at meaningful memory cost — rarely worth it
- AWQ 4-bit outperforms GPTQ 4-bit by ~0.5-1.0% perplexity on modern models due to activation-aware scaling
- MLX 4-bit on Apple Silicon delivers quality comparable to GGUF Q4 at slightly faster inference
- Operational floor for agents: Q4 minimum; never go below Q3 for reasoning workloads
