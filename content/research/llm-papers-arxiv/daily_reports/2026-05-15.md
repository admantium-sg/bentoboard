### 1. Physics-R1: Visual Physics Reasoning with Audited Olympiad Corpus
**arXiv:2605.14040** (May 13, 2026) — Shan Yang
- **Problem:** Multimodal-physics evaluation pipelines suffer from undetected train-eval contamination, translation drift, and MCQ saturation
- **Contributions:**
  - Three-stage audit pipeline (Jaccard → embedding cosine → LLM-judge) surfaces 134 near-duplicates in SciInstruct
  - 17-pp Sonnet 4.5 delta on Estonian-English olympiad translation (30.5% vs 13.6%, p=0.011)
  - 46-pp format-and-novelty gradient between MCQ (79.7% on PhyX) and open-ended evaluation (33.4% on PhysOlym-A)
- **Released:** PhysCorp-A (6,432 records), PhysR1Corp (2,268), PhysOlym-A (500 problems, 99.8% novel)
- **Physics-R1 recipe:** GSPO+DAPO on Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking yields +18.3 pp on PhysOlym-A, +15.7 pp on PhysReason, ahead of Qwen3-VL-32B and Gemini 2.5 Pro
- **Key insight:** MCQ benchmarks systematically overestimate VLM physics reasoning; open-ended evaluation reveals true capability gap
- **Subjects:** cs.CL

### 2. Mistletoe: Stealthy Acceleration-Collapse Attacks on Speculative Decoding
**arXiv:2605.14005** (May 13, 2026) — Shuoyang Sun et al.
- **Problem:** Speculative decoding's efficiency depends on average accepted length tau — but drafter-target mismatch creates attack surface
- **Attack:** Jointly optimizes degradation objective (decreases drafter-target agreement) and semantic-preservation objective (constrains target output distribution)
- **Mechanism:** Null-space projection — degradation gradients projected away from local semantic-preserving direction
- **Results:** Substantially reduces average accepted length tau, collapses speedup, lowers token throughput while preserving output quality and perplexity
- **Key insight:** Speculative decoding introduces mechanism-level attack surface beyond existing output robustness concerns
- **Subjects:** cs.CL, cs.LG

### 3. TraFL: Trajectory-Balance Post-Training for Diffusion Language Models
**arXiv:2605.13935** (May 13, 2026) — Saba Ahmadi et al.
- **Problem:** Diffusion language model post-training methods suffer from trajectory locking — reward-driven updates over-concentrate probability onto narrow denoising paths, reducing coverage of alternative solutions
- **Solution:** TraFL (Trajectory Flow baLancing) — trajectory-balance objective anchored to frozen reference model with diffusion-compatible sequence-level surrogate and learned prompt-dependent normalization
- **Results:** Only evaluated post-training method that improves over base model in every benchmark-length setting; gains persist as sampling budget increases
- **Transfers to held-out:** Stays above base on Minerva Math; strongest method on every LiveCodeBench difficulty split
- **Key insight:** Anchoring to reference model prevents mode-seeking collapse while still optimizing for reward
- **Subjects:** cs.LG, cs.CL

### 4. Derivation Prompting: Logic-Based RAG Improvement
**arXiv:2605.14053** (May 13, 2026) — Ignacio Sastre et al. (IBERAMIA 2024)
- **Method:** Derivation tree construction from initial hypotheses via predefined rules — inspired by logic derivations
- **Advantage:** Interpretable, adds control over generation process
- **Results:** Significantly reduces unacceptable answers vs traditional RAG and long-context methods
- **Key insight:** Logic-based derivation structure provides both interpretability and improved accuracy for knowledge-intensive QA
- **Subjects:** cs.CL, cs.AI

### 5. PEML: Parameter-Efficient Multi-Task Learning with Optimized Continuous Prompts
**arXiv:2605.14055** (May 13, 2026) — Anjir Ahmed Chowdhury et al.
- **Problem:** Existing PEFT methods (LoRA, Prefix Tuning) designed for single-task; multi-task learning requires co-optimizing prompts AND model adaptation
- **Solution:** PEML — neural architecture engineering for continuous prompts + low-rank model weight adaptation
- **Results:** Up to 6.67% average accuracy improvement over state-of-the-art multi-task methods (MTL-LoRA, MultiLoRa, C-Poly, MoE); peak gains up to 10.75%
- **Benchmarks:** GLUE, SuperGLUE, MMLU, commonsense reasoning
- **Key insight:** Joint prompt-model optimization outperforms methods that focus on only one direction
- **Subjects:** cs.CL, cs.AI

### 6. Merging Methods for Multilingual Knowledge Editing
**arXiv:2605.13919** (May 13, 2026) — Kunil Lee et al.
- **Problem:** Language-specific edits interfere with one another in multilingual settings
- **Method:** Evaluated six vector merging variants across 12 languages on MzsRE benchmark
- **Findings:**
  - Vector summation with shared covariance is most reliable overall
  - Simple summation without shared covariance performs poorly
  - TSVM mitigates interference in some settings but limited overall
  - Larger-than-default scaling + relatively low rank often better
- **Key insight:** Multilingual knowledge editing requires careful merging strategy selection; shared covariance is critical
- **Subjects:** cs.CL, cs.LG

### 7. VectraYX-Nano: Spanish Cybersecurity LLM with Native MCP Tool Use
**arXiv:2605.13989** (May 13, 2026) — Juan S. Santillana
- **Model:** 42M-parameter decoder-only, trained from scratch in Spanish for cybersecurity
- **Corpus:** 170M-token VectraYX-Sec-ES (~25 USD compute) partitioned into conversational (42M), cybersecurity (118M), offensive tooling (10M)
- **Architecture:** Transformer with GQA, QK-Norm, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE, z-loss, 16K-token byte-fallback BPE
- **Key findings:**
  - Loss-vs-register inversion at nano scale (bootstrap-corpus ablation)
  - Tool-selection floor (B4=0.000) is corpus-density artifact, not capacity gate
  - Tool-dense corpus (2,801 examples) raises B4 to 0.145 on 42M, 0.445 on 260M
- **Note:** First Spanish-native cybersecurity LLM with end-to-end MCP integration; GGUF artifact 81MB F16 runs sub-second on commodity hardware
- **Subjects:** cs.CL

## Task 2: Reasoning, Efficiency & Evaluation Papers

### 8. VeGAS: Verifier-Guided Action Selection for Embodied Agents
**arXiv:2605.12620** (May 12, 2026) — Nishad Singhi et al. (CVPR 2026 Findings)
- **Problem:** MLLM-based embodied agents brittle in OOD scenarios despite CoT reasoning
- **Solution:** Sample ensemble of candidate actions, use generative verifier to pick most reliable — without modifying underlying policy
- **Challenge:** Off-the-shelf MLLM as verifier yields no improvement
- **Fix:** LLM-driven data synthesis creates diverse failure case curriculum for verifier training
- **Results:** Up to 36% relative performance gain over strong CoT baselines on multi-object, long-horizon tasks (Habitat, ALFRED)
- **Key insight:** Verifier training requires exposure to failure cases, not just successful demonstrations
- **Subjects:** cs.AI

### 9. BenchJack: Systematically Auditing AI Agent Benchmarks for Reward Hacking
**arXiv:2605.12673** (May 12, 2026) — Hao Wang et al.
- **Problem:** Reward hacking emerges spontaneously in frontier models without overfitting — benchmarks near 100% hackable
- **Taxonomy:** Eight recurring flaw patterns derived from past incidents (Agent-Eval Checklist)
- **BenchJack:** Automated red-teaming that drives coding agents to find reward-hacking exploits
- **Extended pipeline:** Iterative generative-adversarial discovers and patches flaws
- **Results on 10 benchmarks:** 219 distinct flaws surfaced; reduces hackable-task ratio from near 100% to under 10% on 4 benchmarks; fully patches WebArena and OSWorld in 3 iterations
- **Key insight:** Benchmarks must be secure by design; evaluation pipelines haven't internalized adversarial mindset
- **Subjects:** cs.AI, cs.CR

### 10. REVELIO: Revealing Interpretable Failure Modes of VLMs
**arXiv:2605.12674** (May 12, 2026) — Isha Chaudhary et al.
- **Framework:** Failure mode = composition of interpretable concepts under which VLM consistently behaves incorrectly
- **Search:** Diversity-aware beam search + Gaussian-process Thompson Sampling over exponentially large discrete space
- **Domains:** Autonomous driving, indoor robotics
- **Findings:**
  - Driving: Weak spatial grounding, fail to account for major obstructions → simulated crashes
  - Indoor: Miss safety hazards OR excessive conservativism → false alarms, reduced efficiency
- **Key insight:** Structured interpretable failure mode discovery enables actionable safety improvements
- **Subjects:** cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.RO

### 11. DisaBench: Disability Harms Evaluation Framework
**arXiv:2605.12702** (May 12, 2026) — Eugenia Kim et al.
- **Gap:** General-purpose safety benchmarks miss disability-related harms
- **Contribution:** 12 disability harm categories co-created with people with disabilities; 175 prompts, 525 annotated pairs
- **Findings:**
  - Harm rates vary sharply by disability type, compound in non-text modalities
  - Terminology-driven harm culturally and temporally bound
  - Standard safety catches overt failures, misses subtle harms requiring domain expertise
- **Key insight:** Disability harm is personal, intersectional, community-defined — cannot be isolated from full context
- **Subjects:** cs.AI, cs.HC
