### Reasoning & Chain-of-Thought

**2610.34951** Improving Reasoning with Rollout Agreement for Group-Based Policy Optimization — Blind paired analysis of GRPO rollouts shows that low-agreement *correct* traces carry far more spurious reasoning than high-agreement ones, so the authors turn rollout agreement into an explicit reward term (RoAR, computed from existing on-policy groups; no human rationales, no extra sampling, no process evaluator). Across 126 pairs, low-agreement traces had 35% spurious reasoning vs. 22% for high-agreement. On five math reasoning benchmarks a 1.5B GRPO-RoAR model reaches **55.5% mean pass@1** (zero-shot, 7K training examples), exceeding GRPO by **+6.0 pp** and GRPO-CARE by **+5.5 pp**. Blind audit of 100 problems solved by all four 1.5B models: spurious rates **18% (GRPO-RoAR) vs. 32% (GRPO), 27% (GRPO-CARE), 37% (DRA-GRPO)** — i.e. the accuracy gain and the reasoning-validity gain point the same way. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34951

**2610.34949** Correlation-Aware Exploration for Long-Trace Reasoning — Argues entropy regularization is the wrong remedy for GRPO policy collapse on thousand-token CoT: pushing mass toward the vocabulary tail conflicts with the entropy decay convergence needs. Info-GRPO instead builds diversity *across whole traces* via a latent seed injected into prompts, correlating policy with a latent prior and guiding seed-conditioned entropy to convergence. On **AIME24, Avg@8 improves over GRPO by 3.75%, 1.66%, 4.16% and 4.17% on four backbones**, and it reaches the **same majority-vote accuracy with 8× fewer samples**. Code/models stated as "will be publicly released". https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34949

**2610.36849** When All Rollouts Fail: Policy-Compatible Joint Decoding for Hard-Problem Reasoning — On problems beyond current ability every rollout fails, so group-relative estimators (GRPO, RLOO) collapse to zero advantage; expert trajectories fix correctness but sit in low-probability regions and decay into surface imitation. PICO performs distribution-constrained *joint decoding*: expert data guides token-level sampling while preserving the student's relative token probabilities, with a dynamic top-k mechanism confining sampling under a finite divergence budget, optimized jointly with on-policy rollouts via importance sampling. Reported as consistently outperforming standard RLVR and prior off-policy baselines across reasoning and agentic benchmarks (abstract gives no point estimates). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36849

**2610.36709** Is Token Confidence Just Chain Length? A Signal-Processing Audit of Reasoning Traces — Treats per-token confidence as an audio-like signal and audits the popular "sliding-window mean confidence over 2048 tokens, stop at the lowest window" early-stopping / chain-ranking heuristic. Sampling **16 chains × 500 problems from two math benchmarks** with a small open reasoning model, they find the windowed-minimum score mostly measures chain *length*: ranking by length alone does as well or better, and after accounting for length the score carries **no information about correctness**. Mechanism: the minimum of a longer slowly-drifting stretch is lower by chance alone. The spectrum does hold a residual signal (incorrect chains fluctuate more rapidly token-to-token) but it is too weak to improve the vote; neither sliding average nor a classical change detector beats simply sampling fewer chains. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36709

**2610.37553** Collective Dynamics of Self-Reinforcement: How Cross-Task Coupling Drives Policy Collapse in Unsupervised RLVR — Theoretical account of label-free RLVR (reward = agreement with own majority vote) collapsing to a single answer on every problem. Models it as a collective instability: sharpening slows by 1/N for N training problems while the shared mode grows at rate ∝λ² independent of N, giving a **critical problem count N_c ≈ ln(1/δ)/(λ²G)**; simulations confirm a sharp transition, N_c ∝ λ⁻² up to a log correction, a single critical amplification λ²NG, log dependence on batch size, and no collapse for confident initial models. Kernel theory identifies a protective coupling and a marginal-entropy bonus; in label-free RL of a small Transformer, removing the shared direction prevents collapse entirely and restoring it causes collapse, with collapse growing in training-set size and base-model entropy. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37553

### Prompt Engineering & Optimization

**2610.37651** GEPA-Curator: Curated Learning for Self Improving Agents — Argues reflective prompt optimizers like GEPA learn from failed agent rollouts, but many failures come from harness limits, stochastic execution or noisy judges and provide no useful (or actively harmful) learning signal. GEPA-Curator adds task curation (filtering unreliable-signal tasks) plus prior-success retrieval (feeding successful trajectories as extra context to the optimizer). Evaluated on GoBrowse, ALFWorld, AppWorld and τ-bench with **Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 5 as optimizer models**; it matches or exceeds both baselines' mean performance, with **gains up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37651

**2610.36774** Batching the Reflective Optimization Loop: Parallel Proposals Make GEPA Faster and Better — Extends the open-source GEPA optimizer to propose and evaluate a *batch* of candidates per optimization step instead of one, exploiting idle parallel capacity. Across a sweep on two tasks, **most batched runs finished in ≤ half the wall-clock time and the fastest in ~a third to a quarter**; batched settings also scored higher on held-out tests — **LiveBench-Math 68.9% → 72.1%** (at 2×2) and **HoVer 49.0% → 60.0%** (at 8×1). A simple runtime model explains the speedups under a finite worker pool, and batched settings spend metric-call and dollar budgets more efficiently. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36774

**2610.35083** Ask Before You Answer: A Question-Driven Evidence Reasoning Architecture (QERA) for In-Context Learning — Prompting over user context: they show the request is not the specification — **>80% of CL-bench Life rubrics share <60% of their content words with the final request**, and models answering directly pass only **67%** of implicit rubrics vs **81%** of explicit ones. QERA converts the request into a requirement checklist plus typed information needs (code-filtered for faithfulness), answers from the whole conversation, then re-asks the same questions with code-verified quotes and applies a guarded local repair — **~4 LLM calls per task, no training, no rubrics**. On **all 405 tasks with four frontier LLMs** QERA has the highest solving rate on every LLM (**21.0% average**, vs. **18.2%** for the strongest of three inference-time baselines and **14.6%** for direct answering), leading every baseline significantly when pooled, while using fewer tokens than PSCI and **4–5% of the tokens of Ctx2Skill**. GPT-6-Luna ablations attribute the gain to a fixed answer protocol (**+3.8 rubric points**) and request-specific questions (**+1.6**), concentrated on implicit rubrics. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35083

### Efficiency: Distillation, Quantization, Serving

**2610.36950** DCKV: Learned Transform Coding of the Prompt KV Cache in Hybrid Language Models — A differentiable codec for the KV cache and, per the abstract, the first KV-cache codec trained end to end; transform, quantizer and entropy models (conditioned on prompt tokens and decoded neighbours) are optimized jointly against a frozen LM for both assigned code length and output change. On **Qwen3.5-2B**, DCKV stores a compressed token in **>55× fewer bytes than bf16 at a loss of at most 1.8 points** on three long-context benchmarks, and in **>100× fewer bytes at a loss of at most 3.6 points**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36950

**2610.36520** Folding Attention Biases into Keys Makes Compacted KV Caches Run on Standard Kernels — Attention Matching compacts a KV cache by keeping some keys per head and adding a bias to each kept key's logit, but FlashAttention and vLLM paged caches have no input for that bias. The authors fold the bias into the keys along one closed-form direction per head fitted on context queries, producing a plain cache usable with the model's own query path; output error is bounded by how far each query's projection on that direction is from 1. With equal per-head budgets, **unmodified vLLM serves folded caches through a KV connector, matching Hugging Face's answers on all 51 Qwen3-4B questions and its greedy output on 180 of 200 Qwen3-14B LongBench samples**; on QuALITY and LongHealth the fold is **within noise of AM's accuracy in 13 of 14 settings**, with lower KL to the full cache than dropping the bias in all 12 where both ran. Under published per-head budgets it is within noise of AM in 9 of 10 settings but **no longer beats dropping the bias in KL**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36520

**2610.36355** WAKMan: Workload Aware KVCache Manager — Prefix caching reuse is decided by the eviction policy, and production traffic mixes single-turn, multi-turn and agentic request types that prefer different policies: agentic steps return within seconds and favor recency, while popular prompts and long conversations return later and favor frequency. Over **20 workload mixes, no single policy always wins**: vLLM's default LRU gives **12% higher cache hits than frequency-oriented S3FIFO on agentic-heavy mixes but trails it by up to 22% elsewhere**, and a single workload parameter can flip the winner. WAKMan picks the eviction policy from live traffic without knowing request types or reading prompts, improving hit rate over LRU by **5 percentage points on average** with the lowest mean and worst-case optimality gap of all policies. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36355

**2610.37386** Channel Selection for Mixture-of-Experts Decoding Needs No Calibration Data — MoE decoding at small batch is bottlenecked by loading routed-expert weights; channel selectors usually keep channels above a threshold calibrated offline. The paper shows a calibrated threshold gives neither of its two advertised benefits: it doesn't fix the read volume (**Prox with thresholds calibrated for an 18% read budget actually reads only 12.9% on average** across their benchmarks), and top-k without calibration matches or beats the threshold version on the same score in **40 of 43 comparisons at 13% and 18% budgets (all 23 on generation tasks)** while avoiding the repetition-loop collapse calibrated thresholds cause on Gemma 4. Their own reader, built entirely from the model's weights (ternary sketch screening + low-rank recovery of skipped channels), **retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads across three post-trained MoE models**, with Triton kernels running MoE blocks **1.60–1.90× faster than optimized vLLM at batch one and up to 2.69× at larger batches**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37386

**2610.37442** CRISP: Co-designing Pruning and Scheduling for Efficient MoE Inference on Edge Servers — Edge MoE inference is dominated by expert-weight movement, not computation; uniform pruning and expert dropping ignore skewed expert-combination frequencies and asymmetric per-expert contribution within a combination. CRISP reorders neurons by importance so one stored expert can serve multiple execution widths, assigns differentiated execution to hot/warm/cold combinations, and overlaps expert loading with computation. Across **Mixtral-8x7B and DeepSeek-MoE-16B, CRISP improves throughput by up to 2.1× at the same memory budget while limiting accuracy loss to 3.6%** relative to full-width inference. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37442

**2610.36477** Proposing Better Rollouts for On-Policy Distillation — Shows the rollout policy in on-policy distillation (OPD) is usually treated as fixed, which creates a compounding teacher–student mismatch: the teacher must supervise trajectories the student samples but the teacher cannot itself reach, where supervision becomes noisy or misleading and produces patterns like repetition that then amplify across rounds. MPOPD derives a student-anchored, teacher-guided rollout distribution from a local trust-region objective over relative teacher support (a geometric mixture of student and teacher policies) and approximates it with a proposal policy computable in a single forward pass. Across **16 math, knowledge and medical reasoning benchmarks**, gains are consistent over OPD and recent variants; on math reasoning, **average accuracy improves by 6.91 points over OPD on Qwen3-4B-Base while average generation length drops 41%**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36477

### Evaluation & Benchmark Validity

**2610.36990** The State and Fate of Multilingual, Contextual Evaluation in the NLP World — Data-driven audit of **51 recent multilingual benchmarks spanning 242 datasets and 219 languages**, organized around coverage, representativeness and rigor. Coverage is wide but thin: **36% of evaluated languages appear in only one benchmark**, entire regions (Oceania, the Americas, Central Asia) are near-zero, and low-resource languages are evaluated on only **1–3 task categories vs. 14 for high-resource**. Representativeness is structurally compromised: **56% of all dataset-language instances are translated from English**, introducing artifacts and English-centric framing. Rigor is undermined by contamination that evades surface-form detection (translated benchmark leakage, parallel-corpus overlap). Dashboard at aka.ms/multilingual-evals. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36990

**2610.36767** Can LLMs Reliably Grade Olympiad Proofs? A Study of Mathematical Verification with LLMs — Builds a **human-curated dataset of USAMO 2025 candidate solutions with expert grading** and evaluates existing LLM verifiers across frontier models; evaluation covers **20 models, 7 metrics, 2 prompt styles, 3 inference methods, 3 prompt templates**, with SOTA models still leaving considerable headroom. Contributions: **TROJAN**, an iterative self-critique pipeline generating high-fidelity adversarial proofs at scale, and **MABGrader**, which reframes grading as arm selection over the discrete score space via a multi-armed bandit — **MABGrader outperforms the SOTA ProofGrader by 26.4% in Quadratic Weighted Kappa and reduces grading error by 14%**. Code and dataset released on GitHub. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36767

**2610.36554** PRISM-BENCH: Diagnosing Grounded and Faithful Multimodal Reasoning Beyond Final-Answer Accuracy — **1,000 expert-curated questions** in five under-represented reasoning categories, each with an idealized reasoning trace written by one human annotator and validated by another. A perception probe (model answers from its own description) attributes **roughly 85% of frontier-model failures to misperception**, placing the bottleneck at grounding; trace alignment predicts answer correctness across all **25 systems tested (macro AUROC 0.66)** and survives length control. New efficiency-aware metric **CORE reorders 24.2% of model pairs relative to accuracy** while staying uncorrelated with response length. Across 25 open and closed systems (incl. GPT-5 and Claude Opus 4.6) best accuracy is **66.0%**, and on the four categories with a human baseline the best system **trails humans by 18.4 points**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36554

**2610.36109** Beyond Update Counts: A Control Map for Evaluating Test-Time Model Updates — Existing evaluations of test-time updating (weights, adapters, memory) summarize by update count and immediate post-update performance, hiding what was updated, where, when and in what order. The Control Map records committed updates preceding each scored answer and scores acquisition, retention, locality and new-setting performance separately; it proves when counts alone determine final state. Findings: at a **760M-parameter checkpoint, 24 schedules with the same update count yield 19.7%–34.2% accuracy**; all six selected CounterFact LoRA settings learn the edit, while **ROME retains edits at all 13 operating points but fails locality at each**; no editor is best across models/benchmarks/tests. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36109

**2610.37229** Decision Models as Judges for Hallucination Detection — Tests cheap "decision models" (fixed label + probability) as an alternative to LLM-as-a-judge for hallucination detection, on binary benchmarks for five hallucination types (**9,852 items from six datasets**), comparing five decision models against generative judge **gpt-6-sol**. Performance depends strongly on type: the best decision model matches gpt-6-sol in balanced accuracy on context inconsistency and comes within **3 points** on factual errors but trails by **4–8 points** on logical and instruction inconsistency; decision-model probabilities are overconfident (though less than GPT-6 models). A confidence cascade of decision models improves on the best single model by **1.9–3.3 points** on three types and matches or exceeds gpt-6-sol at **1–3% of its cost**. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37229

**2610.37070** Correctness Conditioning as Selection in Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Evaluation — Shows that restricting CoT-faithfulness evaluation to correctly answered instances changes the measured population; in a seven-variable structural causal model the exact observed gap is **Δ = Cov(A, C)/P(C=1)**, separating difficulty confounding from shortcut-induced collider bias. A protocol review of **18 papers** distinguishes answer-correctness restrictions from agreement-based selection, benchmark construction and training-data filtering. Evaluating four sub-ceiling deployments on arithmetic tasks, all four show positive selection gaps; the primary **GLM-5.3 run yields a +7.4 percentage-point gap (n = 1000, p < 0.0001)**, pooled Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel **OR = 1.70 (p < 10⁻⁶)**, and an assumption-free covariance decomposition assigns **42.8% of that gap** to between-stratum variation in interventionable-operation count, leaving the majority unattributed. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37070

### Other Notable

- **2610.35226** *Trainable Boundary-Keeping Where No Formal Answer Exists* — Pre-registered, same-base 14B knock-in study of SFT+GRPO on evidence-sufficiency judgment: trained arm raises spontaneous correct refusal **25% → 100%** (40/arm; Wilson 95% CI lower bound 91.2%, non-overlapping with baseline interval [14.2%, 40.2%]), scrambled placebo collapses to 5%/7.5% with 91.1% false refusal; automatic false refusal on 281 well-formed claims only **1.1%**. Caveat stated by authors: rating sessions are *model* sessions, not human reviewers, and SFT saturation makes the GRPO increment not separately identifiable.
- **2610.34993** *Reason or Recite (traffic anomaly reasoning)* — Two-specialist design (LoRA-tuned 9B VLM on structured CoT annotations + 4-bit-quantized 27B fine-tuned on answers only + training-free LLM judge) ranked **first on the 2026 AI City Challenge leaderboard with mean score 0.6788** (leaderboard number is organizer-reported).
- **2610.37126** *Mechanistic Failure Modes of Weight Pruning* — Factorial GPT-2 pruning study (magnitude vs random × heads vs neurons × 3/50/70% sparsity) against the fully characterised IOI circuit: magnitude neuron pruning at 30% **inverts** the circuit (all 26 heads intact but Name Mover contributions flip sign, accuracy 17.7%) while perplexity rates it as less damaged than random head pruning (**74.6 vs 140.1**) — output metrics miss mechanistic damage.
- **2610.36587** *Efficient Quantization Training with Stochastic Rounding via Structured Subspace Randomness* — S3R shares one stochastic-rounding threshold per token across the hidden dimension and corrects the structured offset; **S3R-EC accelerates full INT8 training steps of dense LLMs on TPU v5p by 1.24–1.32× over BF16 and 1.11× over independent SR**.
- **2610.36617** *Balanced State Subspaces for Mamba-2 State Compression* — Balanced truncation on write–read subspace + HARP per-layer rank allocation: **halves the state dimension at +0.3 WikiText-2 perplexity (1.8%) across 130M–2.7B models vs. +1.6 (9.3%) for GHOST**; calibration is a single forward pass, an order of magnitude faster than GHOST's released protocol.
- **2610.37336** *CoS-VQ* — Coordinated shared-scale vector quantization for LLM weights; joint index–scale updates (via tangent-separable upper bound) beat QTIP and LLVQ on Qwen3.8-27B language modeling, coding and reasoning benchmarks; code released.
- **2610.34875** *RubriX: A Fast, Fine-Tunable Non-Autoregressive Rubric Judge* — LoRA-adapted encoder + softmax over scaled cosine similarities to rubric levels; **75.4% exact accuracy on held-out rubrics vs. 57.1% (TypeSafe Jev) and 49.8% (Qwen3-8B judge)**; latency ~28 ms per judgment from 10 → 3,000 tokens per level (Jev 44→211 ms, Qwen3-8B 32→516 ms), i.e. **7.7× faster than Jev and 18.8× faster than Qwen3-8B at 3,000 tokens**. Beats G-Eval with GPT-3.5 on every SummEval dimension and both QAGS subsets.
- **2610.35698** *From Self-Confidence to Statistical Accuracy* — Unified framework for confidence-stratified human auditing vs. judge-assisted evaluation on MMLU-Pro/MMMU-Pro: miscalibrated self-confidence can still support statistically valid stratified auditing, and under highly imbalanced confidence strata proportional allocation substantially reduces estimation error vs. equal-per-stratum allocation; judge-assisted error-corrected estimator via sensitivity/false-positive modeling.
- **2610.35826** *Understanding Jev-Style System One Models* — Option-shuffling test across two Jev versions, 12 Jev reproductions and 6 generative LLMs on **1,700 questions**: Jev's accuracy varies by **<2 pp** across 8 presentations, but its cumulative failure set **expands by 27.3–28.5%** relative to the original order, with first failures continuing into the final presentation.
- **2610.37166** *LiveSearch* — Live financial benchmark storing each question as executable verifier code; **70% of served questions change gold value day to day**, and under tolerance-aware matching a literally reused earlier snapshot fails on a majority of questions within a single day. Six contemporary tool-calling models reach **70.9%–76.7%** answer accuracy under general web search.
- **2610.35082** *CoSA: The Conflict of Skills Attack in Real-World LLM Agent Ecosystems* — Adds ≤5% extra Skills to an existing pool and raises policy-violation rate **35.02% → 58.45% (+23.43 pp)** across 5 LLMs, 3 harnesses, 288 tasks (CoSA-Bench); harness-level guard cuts violations **65.28% → 6.67%** on OpenClaw recruiting tasks while compliant completion is essentially unchanged (66.67% → 65.00%). Directly relevant to agent-skill marketplaces.
- **2610.37657** *Heads of Security (HoS)* — Learned per-head attention gates for instruction/data separation; black-box RL-Hammer attack reaches **97% ASR against GPT-OSS-20B and 85% against SecAlign but only 10% against HoS**; white-box Claudini (strengthened via autoresearch) needs **3× more FLOPs** to reach the same ASR on HoS. ASIDE achieves only 10% utility with tool use.
- **2610.35671** *Evaluating DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017* — Independent AEF-1-conformant safety evaluation across **34 benchmarks (5 in-house)** vs. V4.1 Flash, earlier DeepSeek V4 and closed baselines (GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, Gemini 3.8 Flash). *(Included only as a pointer — the model-launch angle itself was already covered on Oct 24.)*

### Novelty note

Compared with the Oct 24 section (which was dominated by vendor/product news — Kimi K3.1 Pro, Dessa speech-only model, Anthropic AISI cybertesting alignment assessment, OpenAI training-environment post, Samsung TV/Codex firmware, ModelCompare, Berget per-request CO₂, BenchLeader, BenchLM's reasoning-vs-overall column correction, FMRT snapshot drift, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 downloads, MiniMax-M3.1-Flash trending), today's arXiv batch is almost entirely **methodological and mechanistic** rather than launch-driven. Three things stand out as new: (1) **methodological self-criticism of CoT-era heuristics** — 2610.36709 shows a popular token-confidence chain-stopping heuristic is largely a proxy for chain length, and 2610.37070 derives the exact selection gap (Δ = Cov(A,C)/P(C=1)) introduced by restricting CoT-faithfulness evaluation to correct answers (+7.4 pp on GLM-5.3, n=1000); (2) **the GRPO/RLVR training stack is being attacked from several directions at once** — rollout-agreement as a reasoning-validity signal (2610.34951), entropy-vs-convergence tension for long traces (2610.34949), the all-rollouts-fail cliff (2610.36849), rollout-budget allocation (2610.37429), and a theory of dataset-size-dependent policy collapse with a critical N_c (2610.37553); (3) **serving-side work has shifted from "how much can we compress" to "what is the right unit of compression"** — KV-cache eviction matched to live traffic (2610.36355), learned KV codecs (2610.36950), bias-folding so compacted caches run on unmodified vLLM (2610.36520), and calibration-free MoE channel selection (2610.37386, 98.1% accuracy at 17.8% of routed-expert reads). Evaluation-validity work is also sharper than before: not just "benchmark X is contaminated" but auditable structural audits (51 multilingual benchmarks / 242 datasets / 219 languages with 56% translated instances) and benchmark designs where the *gold answer itself* goes stale within a day (2610.37166). Relative to Oct 24, there are essentially no vendor announcements or release-tracking items here, and no trending-mover signals — this batch is a pure research-methodology snapshot. Also note the small but distinctive cluster of "cheap judge" work (decision models at 1–3% of GPT-6-Sol cost, RubriX at 7.7–18.8× speedup over LLM judges), which continues a thread from the Oct 24 BenchLM corrections but with much stronger baselines.
