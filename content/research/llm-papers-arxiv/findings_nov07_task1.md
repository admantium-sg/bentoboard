## Efficiency, Quantization & Systems

### 2611.07593 — TACT-KV: Tri-Axis Cosine Transform for Compressing Volumetric KV Caches in Medical VLMs
**Category:** cs.LG (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Chongyu Qu et al. (15 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Vision-language models applied to 3D medical scans hit a structural memory wall: a single CT or MRI volume produces tens of thousands of vision tokens, and the resulting KV cache scales linearly with both batch and context. Existing KV compression methods operate on the cache's feature or token statistics, or apply 1D and 2D transforms, leaving the three-axis spatial structure in the cache unexploited. The authors propose TACT-KV (Tri-Axis Cosine Transform), a calibration-free KV compression method that exploits strong spatial correlations in visual KV states along all three anatomical axes of 3D scans; this correlation concentrates the KV cache energy under a frequency-domain transform. TACT-KV applies a separable 3D discrete cosine transform to the KV cache, allocates a per-layer bit budget across frequency bins via reverse water-filling (more bits to high-energy components), and quantizes each bin with a fixed scalar codebook, with a derived close-form relation showing the distortion reduction from adaptive bit allocation is dominated by the spectral concentration of the transformed cache. Across six medical VLMs and two 3D CT benchmarks, TACT-KV achieves the lowest 1-bit prediction-flip rate on eleven of twelve model and dataset pairs in the authors' main comparison; the same advantage reproduces on four general-purpose VLMs evaluated on video tasks. At equal high-bandwidth memory (HBM), TACT-KV scales the decoding batch size on a single GPU by up to 8×.
**Why it matters:** This is a distinct and under-explored branch of KV-cache compression — exploiting *3D spatial* structure rather than token statistics or 1D/2D transforms — and the "up to 8× decoding batch size at equal HBM" number is the kind of serving-level claim that directly matters for multi-modal long-context deployment. The finding that the gain transfers from medical 3D VLMs to general-purpose video VLMs is the interesting transfer claim.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07593

### 2611.07149 — FastAFD: When Does Attention-FFN Disaggregation Pay on Rack-Scale NVLink?
**Category:** cs.LG (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Yichao Fu et al. (6 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Attention-FFN disaggregation (AFD) separates attention from the experts of a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model and has been reported to raise decode throughput over colocated serving, but whether the advantage holds on rack-scale NVLink systems such as GB200 NVL72 remained unclear — prior results were obtained on other hardware, against narrower colocated baselines, or with analytical models. The authors build and tune FastAFD, an AFD engine for GB200 NVL72 that overlaps the MoE of one microbatch with the attention of the other, and compare it against the best vLLM wide EP setting. Experiments show that AFD has a throughput advantage of up to 1.17x over wide EP on Qwen3 and 1.16x on Qwen3.5, and matches it on MiniMax-M2.5. They derive an analytical model that predicts AFD throughput within 3% using only profiles of colocated vLLM, telling whether switching to AFD would pay off before deploying it. The model decomposes AFD's speedup into a batch gain (larger batches, since attention GPUs hold no expert weights), an overlap gain (shorter steps, since the MoE runs on the expert GPUs behind attention), and a disaggregation cost (the GPUs given to experts); AFD wins when the two gains outweigh the cost. This view explains which models benefit, and shows speculative decoding helps light-expert models most, where the enlarged overlap gain outweighs the larger disaggregation cost and adds a further 8% to AFD's advantage over wide EP. Looking ahead, serving the experts on accelerators that keep their weights in on-chip SRAM, such as the Groq LPX accelerators paired with Vera Rubin NVL72, would remove most of the disaggregation cost, raising the ideal speedup to 2.48x with speculative decoding.
**Why it matters:** A rare paper that pairs an open-source rack-scale serving engine with a predictive analytical model — the "predicts within 3% from colocated vLLM profiles alone" result is directly actionable for serving decisions without a hardware migration. The claim that disaggregation economics depend on expert-lightness is a concrete, testable hypothesis for MoE serving.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07149

### 2611.07263 — HyFlash: A Prefix-Memory Prior for On-Device Speculative Decoding
**Category:** cs.CL (New submissions) | **Authors:** Jongwoo Ko et al. (2 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Parallel block drafting accelerates speculative decoding by predicting multiple future positions in a single backbone pass, but on-device deployment constrains both drafter capacity and context storage. Under tight memory budgets, drafters must model long-range dependencies and fine-grained draft-position interactions without the growing KV-cache overhead of full-context attention. The authors propose HyFlash, a compact parallel drafter that introduces a prefix-memory prior to separate long-range context modeling from fine-grained draft-position interactions with limited capacity. Local attention captures recent context and draft-position interactions, while recurrent memory compresses historical target features into fixed-size states, giving effective local and long-range modeling with bounded draft-side memory. Experiments show HyFlash achieves higher acceptance than full-context attention with substantially less KV storage, with larger gains for shallow drafters and long contexts.
**Why it matters:** Speculative-decoding drafters are the dominant on-device latency lever, and HyFlash targets the exact memory bottleneck that makes full-context attention impractical on edge hardware. The "larger gains for shallow drafters and long contexts" trend is the practically interesting claim: it suggests the method helps most exactly where today's small on-device models live.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07263

### 2611.07570 — ACSeL: Adaptive Configuration Search for Hardware-Constrained LLM Inference
**Category:** cs.LG (New submissions) | **Authors:** Bokun Wang et al. (3 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Serving large language models under tight memory and latency service-level objectives (SLOs), including on edge devices, requires jointly selecting the model size, batch size, quantization algorithm, and mixed-precision quantization recipes; exhaustive search over this combinatorially large space is infeasible. Unlike data-center serving, which often prioritizes throughput or generation speed, resource-constrained deployments typically set memory and latency budgets as constraints and then seek the configuration with the highest generation quality among feasible ones. The authors propose ACSeL, a framework that identifies configurations predicted to satisfy deployment constraints while achieving high predicted generation quality. To enable efficient quality estimation they introduce Relative Model Quality Degradation (RMQD), a proxy based on the divergence between the candidate and reference models' next-token distributions over a small calibration corpus; across the evaluated model sizes and precisions, RMQD is inexpensive to evaluate and strongly correlated with downstream benchmark performance. ACSeL combines (i) a lightweight quality surrogate that predicts RMQD directly from a configuration, (ii) Few-shot Median Calibration (FMC), which calibrates operator-profile-based latency estimates using a small number of end-to-end profiles, and (iii) a structured search algorithm BandPareto for exploring layerwise mixed-precision configurations. Experiments on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 workstation and an NVIDIA Jetson Thor edge platform show that FMC substantially improves deployment feasibility, while BandPareto + FMC achieves the highest feasibility and the lowest RMQD gaps to the best measured configurations in the evaluation set.
**Why it matters:** This sits at the intersection of the efficiency and quantization themes in today's LLM work: automated joint selection of model size, batching and mixed-precision recipes under hard memory/latency SLOs. RMQD as a cheap next-token-distribution proxy for downstream quality is a reusable evaluation trick, and the edge-hardware (Jetson Thor) evaluation is more grounded than most config-search papers.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07570

### 2611.07988 — Compression Through a Context Lens: Evaluating Adaptation, Quantization, and Distillation in Arabic Legal LLMs
**Category:** cs.CL (New submissions) | **Authors:** Md. Rafiul Biswas et al. (3 authors)
**Abstract summary:** The authors note that model compression can facilitate deployment of large language models in resource-constrained legal settings, but that its effects on domain-specific performance may depend on the evaluation protocol. They investigate this using ArabLegalEval, a Saudi Arabic legal benchmark, under both with-context and no-context evaluation — the former provides supporting legal passages, the latter assesses performance without external context. Removing the context produces accuracy gaps of up to 27 percentage points for the base model. They examine three stages — domain adaptation, quantization, and knowledge distillation — across FANAR-9B, JAIS-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B. Four-bit quantization reduces model memory by 64-71%, with changes in no-context MCQ accuracy ranging from -3.2 to +1.6 percentage points. Distillation produces more variable results, with accuracy generally declining as student size decreases; a Qwen2.5-7B student distilled from FANAR achieves 76.6% no-context accuracy, compared with 77.2% for its teacher. The authors conclude that no-context evaluation provides a useful complement to context-supported testing when assessing the effects of adaptation and compression on Arabic legal language models.
**Why it matters:** A methodological warning that generalizes well beyond Arabic legal LLMs: any quantization/distillation benchmark whose items ship with retrieval context will systematically under-report compression damage, because the context masks it. The "up to 27 percentage points" gap between with- and no-context accuracy is the concrete number that makes the point.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07988

### 2611.07756 — Scaling Laws for SAE Training Data
**Category:** cs.CL (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Nikita Koriagin et al. (3 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Activation storage is often a bottleneck in sparse autoencoder (SAE) training. This paper studies how many unique activations an SAE needs when its total training budget is fixed. Quality improves rapidly as the buffer grows, then reaches a plateau where replaying the buffer is nearly as effective as using fresh activations throughout training; beyond this point, additional unique activations bring little benefit to reconstruction or interpretability, so the buffer need not grow with the training budget. The authors describe this diversity-versus-repetition tradeoff with a scaling law for limited data and validate it across dictionary sizes and token budgets, early, middle, and late layers of Llama-3.1 and Qwen3 model sizes, and BatchTopK, TopK, and JumpReLU SAEs; sweeps using mathematical text and Python code test the effect of changing the activation source. Evaluation covers explained variance (EV), reconstruction MSE, feature absorption, automated interpretability, and sparse probing. The resulting practical training rule is to choose the smallest buffer that reaches the quality plateau and replay it for the remaining budget, which in the authors' experiments reduces activation storage by 8--64× with negligible quality loss.
**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to interpretability research economics: an 8--64× cut in activation storage changes the compute budget of interpretability labs. The law is explicitly validated across multiple SAE variants, architectures and layers, so it should be actionable rather than a single-config observation.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07756

## Mechanistic Interpretability & Safety

### 2611.08438 — Understanding AI from the Inside: The State of Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety
**Category:** cs.CL (New submissions) | **Authors:** Fazl Barez et al. (27 authors)
**Abstract summary:** This is a survey of mechanistic interpretability for AI safety, framed around the question: does understanding how models work actually make them safer? The authors place mechanistic interpretability in the broader context of interpretability research and compare its mechanistic evidence with behavioural evaluation. They introduce the foundations of mechanistic interpretability — features, polysemanticity, transformer computation, and circuits — and survey methods for feature extraction and analysis, circuit discovery and causal verification, explaining intermediate representations in natural language, learning dynamics, and training-data attribution. Beyond surveying existing methods, they propose a unified framework for evaluating mechanistic interpretability claims and characterise six research orientations by their goals, assumptions, and trade-offs. Finally, they examine how mechanistic understanding can support AI safety, including knowledge editing, steering, and monitoring, and assess its limitations, failure modes, open problems, and relationship to behavioural safety measures. A repository of code notebooks with worked examples that reproduce key findings from surveyed methods is released alongside.
**Why it matters:** A large-author (27) survey with an explicit evaluation framework for interpretability claims is the kind of document that gets cited as a field-level reference point; the proposed unified framework for evaluating mech-interp claims is the substantive contribution, since it addresses a real methodological gap.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08438

### 2611.06989 — The Two-Ledger Model: Transaction-Grain Cost Accounting for Multi-Agent AI Systems Across Clouds, Models, and Systems of Record
**Category:** cs.AI (New submissions) | **Authors:** Vivek Acharya (1 author)
**Abstract summary:** Enterprises run business transactions through multi-agent AI systems spanning clouds, large language models (LLMs), orchestration harnesses, and systems of record, but finance receives AI cost only at settlement grain, aggregated by SKU and charge period — so the business question of what a given order, claim, or case cost in AI cannot be answered from billing data, because providers never see enterprise transaction boundaries. The FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification (FOCUS) gives billing data one schema across providers but remains settlement-grain even as it adds AI token and requester attribution. The author formalizes this grain mismatch, derives seven requirements, and proposes the Two-Ledger Model: a settlement ledger of FOCUS-conformant billing data joined to an attribution ledger of invocation-grain usage records built on OpenTelemetry GenAI telemetry. A layered correlation spine (trace context, A2A/MCP metadata, business-key correlation, probabilistic matching) assigns each record to its business transaction with recorded method and confidence; a versioned rate card prices usage in near real time; period reconciliation ties both ledgers to the invoice, and the unexplained residual identifies shadow AI. Costs are reported per order, claim, or outcome with agentic cost metrics. A simulation with known ground truth shows the spine sustaining cost coverage above 99% as trace propagation falls to zero, the reconciliation residual recovering planted unmetered spend within 0.24 percentage points while flagging provider billing errors, and mid-flight gating cutting p99 transaction cost by up to 61% at a 2-4% false-pause rate. The author proposes the attribution ledger as an invocation-grain FOCUS profile for standardization.
**Why it matters:** Cost attribution at transaction grain is the operational bottleneck for any production agent deployment, and the agentic-specific metrics here (per-order/per-outcome cost, shadow-AI detection) are the metrics enterprise buyers actually want. Single-author position-plus-mechanism paper; the simulation numbers are simulation-derived, so the >99% coverage and 0.24 percentage-point recovery are model-based, not field-measured.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06989

### 2611.08035 — Should We Pay This Much for Robustness? Efficient Proxy Certificates with Marginal Guarantees
**Category:** cs.LG (New submissions) | **Authors:** Soroush H. Zargarbashi et al. (3 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Modern classifiers remain sensitive to small input perturbations, while strong model-agnostic robustness certificates such as randomized smoothing are often too expensive for deployment, requiring an extensive number (e.g. 2000) of model forward passes per input. The authors propose a framework that delegates certification to a cheap proxy function while statistically controlling its deviation from the original sound certificate. Given an exchangeable unlabeled calibration set, the proxy is tuned via robust conformal risk control to guarantee a bounded marginal (rather than pointwise) false-approval rate: the probability, over future inputs, that the proxy accepts an input whose prediction is flipped due to perturbation. The resulting proxy certificate can be used either as a standalone marginal certificate or as a fallback mechanism that invokes the expensive certificate only when the proxy rejects; the authors provide both single-sample and sample-heavy calibration recipes. Empirically, as a standalone certificate the method reduces test-time certification cost to a single model forward while retaining much of the certified performance of randomized smoothing at small radii; as a fallback it recovers the certified ratio of randomized smoothing at a more modest speedup. This enables cheap yet effective robustness guarantees for larger architectures such as vision transformers and language models.
**Why it matters:** Robustness certification is the kind of rigour that gets demanded for deployed safety-critical models, and the marginal-vs-pointwise guarantee distinction is the honest framing of what a proxy certificate buys. The authors explicitly position it for "larger architectures such as vision transformers and language models", though the abstract's evaluation is model-generic.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08035

### 2611.07512 — RLGPA: A Reinforcement Learning-Guided Granularity Planning Attack for Decision-Based Sparse Adversarial Examples
**Category:** cs.LG (New submissions) | **Authors:** Chao Zhou et al. (5 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Decision-based sparse attacks must reduce perturbation support using only the victim's predicted label, and the best restoration strategy varies as support contracts: coarse removal works early, while tail states need finer, history-aware tests. The authors propose RLGPA, an online reinforcement learning method that schedules support-reduction executors and their granularity under a hard query budget. A masked Dueling Double DQN selects among seven stable policy--scale actions; each action produces support-relative restoration groups that are rebuilt after every macro decision, and a feasibility-preserving executor commits only adversarial restorations and feeds support gain and query cost back to the planner. Under the matched top-1-label oracle, RLGPA reaches 61% targeted ASR at sparsity 0.01 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 25% SparseEvo, 0% Pointwise), and 75.5%/73.0% on ResNet50/ViT at sparsity 0.005 (vs. 29.0%/15.5%). A fixed-probe test reduces median L0 from 165 to 23 after 40 images without any update. State-blind and state-shuffled controls degrade significantly, confirming that the learned policy uses attack state; the main gains come from state-conditioned executor scheduling and dynamic repartition. Source code is publicly available.
**Why it matters:** Black-box (decision-only) adversarial attacks are the realistic threat model for deployed image APIs, and the gap from 25% to 61% targeted ASR is large. The ablation design (state-blind / state-shuffled controls) is what makes the result credible rather than a lucky hyperparameter draw.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07512

### 2611.07432 — Typed Knowledge over Constructive Institutions
**Category:** cs.AI (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Hans-Martin Will (1 author)
**Abstract summary:** Type theories designed for mathematics or program verification need only that a proposition holds — proof irrelevance in conversion erases, at the kernel level, how a claim was established — whereas a persistent knowledge store must retain that record: who established a claim, by which derivation or external tool, and under which assumptions. The volume presents the theory of Eigenius, a store of typed knowledge over constructive institutions, and develops its metatheory from that requirement. The kernel theory is a dependent type theory with cumulative universes, an impredicative universe of propositions, inductive families, and an intensional identity type; proof irrelevance is an axiom with an explicit witness rather than a conversion rule, so proofs of a proposition are provably equal yet remain distinct terms. Definitional equality is joinability under an orthogonal rewriting system — confluent, injective on type formers, and decidable by a type-free test — while a separate subsumption judgment covers universe cumulativity, codomain-covariant function types, and declared class inclusions. Consistency is proved via a set-theoretic model; a justification logic over the kernel's propositions tracks evidence and support; environments are organized as an institution so store extensions are signature morphisms and external verdicts enter via comorphisms; a typed knowledge-graph theory with records, classes, and refinements is formulated; and the correctness of conversion, normalization by evaluation, and bidirectional type checking are proved relative to termination. A Lean 4 formalization accompanies the volume.
**Why it matters:** A formal, machine-checked foundation for persistent evidence-bearing knowledge stores — the substrate that agent memory, provenance-tracking and knowledge-graph systems need if they are to be auditable rather than merely plausible. The proof-irrelevance-as-axiom design (so provenance is retained at the kernel level) is the conceptually sharp move, and the Lean 4 formalization makes it verifiable.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07432

## Agents, Agents' Tools & Agent Cost

### 2611.07558 — SKIM: Pruning Large Language Model Agents via Selective Knowledge Informed Masking
**Category:** cs.CL (New submissions) | **Authors:** Moonseok Choi et al. (5 authors)
**Abstract summary:** LLM agents are increasingly deployed under structured generation, where grammar constraints enforce output format at inference time, whereas existing compression methods target unconstrained generation. The authors first identify a fundamental mismatch: under structured generation, grammar constraints reduce the model's output distribution to valid tokens, and the logits matter most at the small set of positions that decide the agent's action. Motivated by this, they propose SKIM (Selective Knowledge-Informed Masking), described as the first pruning and distillation framework for LLM agents under structured generation, with both stages guided by token role. SKIM partitions token positions into context, decision, and format categories, and this partition drives two complementary axes: (1) category-aware pruning saliency scores that preserve parameters most relevant for decision positions; and (2) an on-policy distillation objective that aligns student and teacher on valid tokens, paired with category-aware feature matching. The authors evaluate SKIM on tool-calling benchmarks and within multi-step agent loops, across multiple model scales and sparsity levels, reporting that SKIM consistently improves accuracy over baselines, with its superiority becoming most striking in high-sparsity regimes where conventional methods struggle.
**Why it matters:** Almost all current pruning work assumes free-form generation; tool-calling agents are the opposite case (grammar-constrained), and this paper identifies why that breaks existing saliency heuristics. The headline claim that the advantage concentrates in high-sparsity regimes is exactly where deployment economics live.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07558

### 2611.07683 — ReferClaw: Disambiguating User Intent for Egocentric Assistance
**Category:** cs.AI (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Hangyu Qin et al. (3 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Users of egocentric AI assistants often ask short, ambiguous questions whose intended referents cannot be uniquely resolved from video and dialogue history — for example, "Where did I leave it?" may refer to several previously handled objects. Existing VideoQA systems typically commit to a single interpretation, risking plausible but intention-misaligned answers. The authors introduce ReferClaw, an agentic framework that formulates egocentric streaming QA as an interactive process in which the assistant asks clarifying questions to resolve user intent before answering. Three cooperating agents identify candidate interpretations, request clarification, and generate answers grounded in the resolved intent and visual evidence. A bounded, cost-aware policy balances expected information gain against user effort while limiting clarification turns and question complexity. ReferClaw learns from interaction through intent-specific skill updates and process-reward-guided policy optimization with agent-level credit assignment. Evaluations on augmented egocentric QA benchmarks demonstrate improved answer accuracy and joint answer-and-grounding performance over existing baselines, alongside zero-shot transfer across datasets.
**Why it matters:** "Ask before answering" as a trained, cost-aware agent behavior is a pattern that generalizes well past egocentric video — ambiguity-aware clarification with explicit user-effort budgeting is the missing half of most assistant UX. The process-reward + agent-level credit assignment recipe is reusable.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07683

### 2611.07825 — Generate-then-Rank: Learning Symbolic Goal Interpretation for Embodied Agents
**Category:** cs.CL (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Jisen Lei et al. (2 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Goal interpretation is the task of translating a natural language instruction into a formal symbolic goal that a planner can execute, and it is critical for embodied agents because any error there propagates to every downstream planning and action step. Doing it well requires both generating suitable candidates and reliably selecting among them. The authors present a two-stage generate-then-rank approach to Goal Interpretation in the Embodied Agent Interface (EAI): first, supervised fine-tuning adapts a Large Language Model (LLM) to EAI's output convention; second, Candidate-Output Ranking from Errors (CORE) learns preferences over complete outputs from the model's own generated alternatives. Experiments were conducted on the household scenes of the Embodied Agent Interface Benchmark, evaluating multiple open source LLMs including DeepSeek-V3 chat, DeepSeek-Reasoner, and Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-72B. The proposed method outperformed the baseline models including LLMs with simple prompting, chain-of-thought, and knowledge graph based approaches.
**Why it matters:** A clean, general LLM pattern — generate candidates, then rank them with a learned preference over the model's own outputs — applied to the narrow bottleneck where embodied-agent errors compound. The comparison set (simple prompting, CoT, knowledge-graph approaches) makes the gain interpretable: the win comes from ranking, not from more prompting.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07825

### 2611.07730 — Seeing Beyond the Next Step: World-Model-Guided Human-Like Navigation in Multi-Agent Scenes
**Category:** cs.AI (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Seong-Eun Hong et al. (5 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Selecting a next motion in a populated scene is not just a matter of avoiding what is currently in the way: an action can be locally feasible yet drift onto the road instead of the crosswalk, cut into a stream of crossing pedestrians, or bottleneck a narrow passage. These failure modes are invisible from the current state alone, and acting on them requires reasoning about what each candidate action will cause. World models offer exactly this — action-conditioned predictions of the future — but rolling them out at every decision step is a cost deployable navigation systems cannot pay, and the field has largely traded foresight for speed. The authors argue this is a false dichotomy and place the world model at training time, as a preference oracle that distills foresight into a fast policy, rather than at inference time as an online planner. They pair a discrete visual action prior with two task-aligned world models, one for nearby social dynamics and one for future scene semantics, that score candidate actions by their predicted consequences and refine the policy through group-relative preference distillation. At deployment both world models are removed and the policy acts in a single forward pass. Across five held-out evaluation scenarios, this yields navigation that is simultaneously safer, more socially and group-coherent, and more goal-directed than strong reactive and learned baselines, with naive observers rating the resulting motion as more human-like.
**Why it matters:** The amortization trick — running the expensive world model at training time only, then discarding it — is a broadly applicable recipe for anyone who wants foresight in a deployed policy without inference-time rollouts. Worth noting that the evaluation is on navigation rather than general LLM agents.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07730

### 2611.08156 — La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling
**Category:** cs.LG (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Mateo Clémente et al. (8 authors)
**Abstract summary:** World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on pretrained vision-language backbones; both tie policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control, and it remained unclear how much these generation-oriented choices affect control. The authors introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder. Following Fast-WAM, La-JEPA learns to predict future features jointly with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art performance in clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 scenes and on LIBERO-Plus with correct instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competing policies; it runs substantially faster than Fast-WAM at comparable performance and outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0 without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to pi0.5 and above non-embodied-pretrained baselines. A design study shows that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction choices significantly impact the policy even when future prediction is skipped at inference. The authors correct the LIBERO-Plus instructions, as the officially released evaluator corrupts them for most variants, and re-evaluate several strong open baselines.
**Why it matters:** Two things matter here beyond the results: the design conclusion that "training-time future prediction choices significantly impact the policy even when future prediction is skipped at inference" is a genuinely surprising mechanistic claim, and the correction of a corrupted benchmark evaluator is a service to the field. The "smaller and faster without a generative pretrained backbone" result challenges the assumption that policy backbones must be generation-scale.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08156

### 2611.07513 — Concertina: Embodying Manipulation and Cognition in One Diffusion Vision-Language Model
**Category:** cs.AI (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Chengcheng Wang et al. (7 authors)
**Abstract summary:** Most vision-language-action (VLA) policies decouple manipulation from cognition by bolting an external action mechanism onto a vision-language model (VLM) — either quantizing actions into discrete tokens whose errors bear no stable correspondence to physical displacements, or delegating continuous trajectory generation to a separate action expert whose gradients compete with or are blocked from updating the VLM backbone. The authors argue manipulation and cognition should instead be embodied in the same model, shifting from a decoupled VLA to an Embodied Vision-Language Model (eVLM). They introduce Concertina, an eVLM placing continuous trajectory representation, blockwise generation, and physical-coordinate supervision directly on the computation and parameter path of a shared diffusion VLM backbone. Each control timestep is encoded as one continuous latent action token in the backbone hidden space, and the shared Transformer reconstructs temporally ordered action blocks via the same corrupt-and-reconstruct operator used for multimodal pretraining. Active action projections account for only 0.21% of parameters in the 4.46B model with zero separate action-generator layers, compared to 16.5%-35.1% dedicated action-expert capacity in representative continuous VLAs. Using their proposed Action Interface Audit, the authors confirm that cross-time trajectory coupling emerges in the middle and late layers of the shared backbone rather than at the action interfaces. On standard benchmarks, Concertina achieves 98.15% average success on regular LIBERO, 85.9% on the 10,030-task LIBERO-Plus benchmark, and 4.142 average completed chain length on CALVIN ABC-D.
**Why it matters:** The architectural claim — that a diffusion VLM backbone can absorb continuous control with only 0.21% action-specific parameters instead of the 16.5%-35.1% that dedicated action experts consume — is a direct efficiency argument for anyone building VLA systems. The Action Interface Audit is a nice mechanistic companion: it locates where trajectory coupling actually forms.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07513

## Multimodal & Vision Foundation Models

### 2611.07392 — WeLM-Image: A Unified Image Generation Model towards Visual Intelligence
**Category:** cs.AI (Cross submissions) | **Authors:** Canmiao Fu et al. (16 authors)
**Abstract summary:** The authors present WeLM-Image, a unified image foundation model that integrates multimodal understanding into text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, and multi-reference synthesis. Designed for single-GPU deployment in WeChat's large-scale consumer applications, the model processes text, reference images, and target latents as one interleaved sequence. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone combines shared attention with independent understanding and generation parameters to limit optimization interference; equipping each path with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules provides sparse capacity, with 31.7B stored parameters and approximately 5.4B generation parameters activated per target latent token. A generation-oriented data engine combines a knowledge-grounded visual vocabulary with large-scale visual clustering to balance semantic coverage and editing tasks, while training-time evaluation guides iterative data refinement; bilingual annotations and verified editing supervision support progressive training up to native 1.5K resolution.
